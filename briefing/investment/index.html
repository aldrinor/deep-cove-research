3Inside the Price

The hardware investment covers the AI servers, the Security Shell, installation and acceptance; three small items are quoted at the site. The protection is bought once and carried through every size.

Included at Every Size

  • The Core: the AI servers for the chosen size, with no internet path.
  • The Security Shell: the Records Librarian, the Web Librarian, the Chat, Records and Web Gates, the Front Door where clinicians sign in, the shielded rack, the Delivery Bay and the rack network.
  • Battery backup: an online double-conversion UPS sized at 1.25 times the design load, bridging to the hospital generator.
  • Installation: delivery, racking, integration with BC's sign-on and records systems, the on-site acceptance test and staff training.

Provided under the Agreement or Quoted at the Site

  • Software licence and support: per agreement.
  • Quoted at the site and delivered at every size: the Monitoring Gate, the Web Librarian's separate fetcher server and a pair of hardware security modules (US$950 each, public list price).
  • The room, its power supply and cooling, and hospital staff time: provided by BC.
  • Electricity.

The Security Shell costs the same at every size. At Clinic it is 47–54% of the hardware price; at Region it is 10–12%. That is why the hardware cost for each clinician falls from C$4,255 at Clinic to C$193 at Region.

No liquid cooling and no data-centre build from Clinic to Region. Province uses one liquid-cooled rack-scale system in a provincial facility.

4Capacity Guaranteed at Acceptance

Each installation is measured on site before go-live. The guaranteed figure is the contract number; the measured figure sits beside it, so BC can see the margin.

On delivery, recorded Kit Ask and Kit Think sessions are replayed on BC's installed hardware at the contracted number of simultaneous streams. It passes only when 9 in 10 users stream at 100 tokens per second or more, first words arrive within the contracted time, and no answer fails.

Speed at the Guaranteed Load

  • Kit Ask: first words in 1 second or less for 95% of questions.
  • Streaming: 100 tokens per second or more for 9 in 10 users, in the range of the fastest cloud AI services and about 19 times human reading speed.
  • Kit Think: its first progress line within 1 second; completion time for a hard medical question written into the acceptance schedule for each size.
Expert DetailHow Capacity Is Measured
  • Source: 220 independent measurements of this model in its released format, on each GPU type, dated 5–13 November 2026. The measurement source is provided under NDA.
  • Measured figure: the highest number of simultaneous sessions at which 9 in 10 users stream at 100 tokens per second or more.
  • Guaranteed figure: the highest number at which 9 in 10 users stream at 141 tokens per second or more on the same traces. The measurements used speculative decoding with a draft acceptance of 3.25–3.51 tokens per step; the guaranteed basis still delivers 100 tokens per second at an acceptance of 2.5 (100 × 3.51 ÷ 2.5 = 140.4). The on-site acceptance test replaces this margin with the measured figure for Kit's own text.
  • Per 8-GPU server, guaranteed / measured: H200 16 / 32 (64 at peak) · B200 384 / 576 · H100 8 / 24 · B300 544 / 544. At Hospital, one entry-class server serves 16 streams and carries on alone if the growth-class server fails; the other is the safety-test server and image-reading host.
  • Province: 10,888 simultaneous sessions at 204 tokens per second, measured at peak on 64 GPUs of one rack-scale system; × 16 = about 174,000 clinician-equivalents, more than every BC physician and regulated nurse at once. This is a peak figure on a different basis from the other sizes; Province's guaranteed figure is set in the site quotation.
  • Input length: on the growth-class rows the measured sessions carried 128,000–330,000-token inputs, against about 18,000 for a Kit Think turn; the entry-class rows used shorter inputs, so the on-site acceptance test sets the Clinic figure.
  • Streams and answers: Kit Think's team mode runs about 1.5 streams per answer in flight, so 16 guaranteed streams carry about 10 Kit Think answers at once (Hospital about 266, Region about 1,034). The 6,000-token Kit Think figure is the live average of all output tokens per answer across every model call (provider token counts: 16.69 million over 2,800 answers in 24 hours), so the busiest-hour clinician figures already include team mode.
  • Clinicians in the busiest hour = guaranteed simultaneous streams × 16. Per clinician-hour: 4 questions × (0.8 × 66 stream-seconds for a 6,000-token Kit Think answer + 0.2 × 7.9 stream-seconds for a 661-token Kit Ask answer) = 217.5 stream-seconds; 3,600 ÷ 217.5 = 16.55, rounded down to 16. The Kit Think length matches the live average of 5,960 tokens over 2,800 answers; the Kit Ask length is the measured average over 1,038 answers. At 8 questions in the busiest hour, halve the clinician figures. At 100% Kit Think, multiply them by 0.82.
  • Named staff: at a planning ratio of one in three named staff active in the busiest hour, multiply the busiest-hour figure by 3; Region then serves about 74,000 named staff. For scale, BC has 16,438 physicians (CIHI, 2025) and 62,538 regulated nurses (CIHI, 2024). BC's clinical lead confirms the ratio.
  • Speed lever: one growth-class server serves 576 users at 100 tokens per second or more, or 256 at 200 or more. A small reserved share of each growth-class server runs faster still, for emergency and short Kit Ask traffic.
  • Kit's current hosted service, for reference (measured with test questions, no BC patient data): Kit Ask first words in about 1.5 seconds (median, n = 690, 8–15 November 2026); Kit Think Medical's first visible line in about half a second (median, n = 104), with a hard medical question finished in about one to two minutes (emergency 52.1 s, dose 90.7 s, symptom 109.2 s medians). Completion times on BC's hardware are set per size in the acceptance schedule.

British Columbia

Checks and safety-tests each update again on its own test station, installs it with two people present through the Delivery Bay, and decides when it goes live. The previous version stays installed, so rollback is one step.

Grow

Deep Cove Intelligence

British Columbia

Sets the growth trigger and owns every server added. We recommend about 70% of guaranteed capacity in the busiest hour.

Expert DetailUpgrade Mechanics
  • No new network fabric. Each AI server holds the whole model, so added servers run as independent copies behind the Front Door's load balancer. No GPU-to-GPU link between servers is needed.
  • Redundancy: from Hospital, a second server keeps answering if one fails; from Region, N+1 on the growth-class servers.

9Owning Compared with Renting

An entry-class AI server costs what 1.7 years of renting the same GPUs costs; a growth-class server, what 1.4 years of renting comparable GPUs costs. Rented capacity sits in someone else's data centre, so it cannot have an air-gapped Core. With Deep Cove, patient records never leave the facility.

Expert DetailThe Payback Arithmetic
  • 8-GPU H200 at US$293,000 ÷ (US$2.52 per GPU-hour × 8 GPUs × 8,760 hours) = 1.66 years, at the contract rental rate in our November 2026 cost basis.
  • 8-GPU B200 at US$450,000 ÷ (US$4.65 per GPU-hour × 8 × 8,760) = 1.38 years, against a published 36-month reservation of the newer 8-GPU B300.
  • Both compare the AI server price alone with rental of the GPUs alone; the Security Shell has no rental equivalent.

10Questions from Finance and Procurement

What does Deep Cove cost?

The hardware investment is C$1.09–1.26 million for Clinic. Certified second-hand servers bring Clinic to C$0.95–1.03 million at the same capacity, or from C$0.83 million on previous-generation servers for about 130 clinicians. Hospital is C$2.44–2.96 million; C$4.80–5.85 million for Region; and from about C$8.1 million plus the Security Shell for Province. These are budgetary prices, November 2026, and dated quotes fix the price at order. The Monitoring Gate, the fetcher server and two hardware security modules are quoted at the site. Software licence and support are per agreement.

What sets the low and high end of each range?

The November 2026 market price of the AI servers, from the lowest to the highest published price. The Security Shell is a Budgetary Price, November 2026. Installation, integration, the acceptance test and staff training add 12% to the hardware.

Can a Clinic start on second-hand servers?

Yes, from a certified refurbisher that carries its own warranty, because GPU makers' data-centre warranties do not transfer in most regions. Firmware is re-flashed and every unit is inspected at intake. Same-generation servers keep Clinic's guaranteed capacity at C$0.95–1.03 million. Previous-generation servers cost C$0.83–1.02 million and guarantee 8 simultaneous streams, about 130 clinicians in the busiest hour.

What happens if a server fails?

From Hospital up, the other servers keep answering: 16 simultaneous streams continue at Hospital and 1,168 at Region. Clinic runs on one AI server, with a 4-hour on-site hardware service; adding a second server gives it failover.

Why is Clinic so much more per clinician?

The Security Shell is the same at every size: at Clinic it is about half the hardware price, at Region about a tenth. A growth-class server also guarantees 24 times the streams of an entry-class server for about C$0.26 million more. For any first site above about 250 clinicians in the busiest hour, a growth-class server is the recommended start.

Why not rent cloud GPUs instead?

Rented GPUs sit in someone else's data centre, so the Core could not be air-gapped and patient records would leave the facility. Owning also pays back: an AI server costs what 1.4 to 1.7 years of renting comparable GPUs costs.

Can rural sites use Deep Cove without their own installation?

Yes. A Hospital or Region installation serves rural clinics over the health authority's private network, encrypted, because the traffic is text. Questions travel only between BC health-authority facilities, never on the public internet, and patient records stay in BC's facility.

What does BC provide?

A room with space for one to three racks, hospital power with generator backup, cooling for 16 to 84 kW, a connection to the hospital network, and the people who sign the acceptance gates. The battery backup is in the price. Clinic, Hospital and Region need no liquid cooling and no data-centre build.

Kit is clinical decision-support software for licensed professionals. It shows its sources, and the clinician decides.

Image and document reading is non-diagnostic. Diagnostic imaging follows the Health Canada medical-device pathway.
