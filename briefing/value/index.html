Rural and remote teams

Support at 2 a.m. for every rural health centre

The need in BC. More than 200 rural, remote and Indigenous communities work with limited health care resources and isolated clinicians.

BC's virtual physicians have covered overnight emergency departments to prevent diversions in 12 communities.

Estimated about 1,000 rural emergency visits avoided a year

Published evidence: Cressman et al., npj Digital Medicine, 2025. Ho et al., CMAJ, 2025.

What Kit does, the published evidence, Kit's measured result and the inputs

What Kit does. A remote nurse or rural physician asks Kit Think Medical.

Kit puts red flags first, lists what must not be missed, gives the BC-guideline next steps, checks weight-based doses in code and states the transfer criteria.

It then drafts a one-page case summary for the call to Real-Time Virtual Support or the transport team.

Kit adds to BC's virtual physician networks.

It does not replace them.

Published evidence. Adding virtual physicians to BC's 8-1-1 line meant 16.14 fewer emergency visits per 1,000 callers within 30 days, across 445,630 users (Cressman et al., npj Digital Medicine, 2025).

In BC's urgent virtual support, 76% of encounters supported nurses at rural health centres (Ho et al., CMAJ, 2025).

Kit's measured result. On NOHARM, the safety of clinical recommendations, Kit scores 80.0 over 330 cases.

101 US physicians using conventional resources scored 42.2.

On Script Concordance Testing, reasoning under uncertainty judged against expert panels, Kit scores 72.8 over 174 items.

Illustrative estimate, inputs shown. 634,976 rural residents × 10% with one Kit-supported advice contact a year × 16.14 emergency visits avoided per 1,000 contacts = about 1,025.

This assumes a Kit-supported contact matches the 8-1-1 virtual-physician effect: the 16.14 per 1,000 was measured for virtual physicians, not for an AI tool, and BC replaces it with its own measured rate after go-live.

About C$0.52 million a year at C$504 per emergency visit, the cost used in BC's own 8-1-1 evaluation.

The larger rural value is not in dollars: clinicians supported where they work, and transfers decided on clear criteria.

Physicians

Less paperwork, more patients

The need in BC. BC has 8,263 family physicians.

Canadian physicians lose 20 million hours a year to red tape, equal to 9,093 full-time physicians.

Estimated about 275,000 physician hours a year, about 1.1 million 15-minute visit slots

Published evidence: Booth et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2026. Lukac et al., NEJM AI, 2025.

What Kit does, the published evidence, Kit's measured result and the inputs

What Kit does. After a visit, Kit drafts the chart note in the clinic's template, plain-language instructions for the patient and any referral letter, from the physician's own summary or the clinic's existing transcript.

For a referral, the Records Librarian gathers the labs, reports and medication history, read-only and within the physician's own access rights.

The physician edits and signs.

Published evidence. Across 1,637,227 encounters and 902 clinicians in 14 hospitals, AI documentation cut note time by 1.21 minutes per encounter (Booth et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2026).

In a randomized trial of 238 physicians, one AI scribe cut time-in-note by 9.5% (Lukac et al., NEJM AI, 2025).

Kit's measured result. On HealthBench Professional, physician-graded clinical conversations, Kit scores 61.9 over 525 tasks; specialist physicians scored 43.7.

The newest frontier models score higher on this board: Anthropic's Fable 5.2 68.1 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra 66.0, in independent evaluations by Artificial Analysis.

On MedAgentBench v1, Kit completes 94.3% of 300 clinical tasks in an electronic health record.

× 4 = about 1.1 million 15-minute visit slots

What Kit does, the published evidence, Kit's measured result and the inputs

What Kit does. Kit supports the licensed nurses and physicians who answer patients.

When the words describe an emergency, fixed code puts the 9-1-1, poison-centre or 9-8-8 instruction first, before any AI text.

Otherwise Kit gives the nurse sourced options to discuss: care at home, 8-1-1, a pharmacist, an urgent and primary care centre or the emergency department.

The nurse decides and advises.

Kit drafts a plain summary the patient brings to the visit.

Published evidence. BC's 8-1-1 virtual physicians meant 16.14 fewer emergency visits and 106.14 more primary-care visits per 1,000 users within 30 days, with health-system costs neutral over a year (Cressman et al., npj Digital Medicine, 2025).

Of 1,834 callers advised home treatment, none died (CMAJ Open, 2023).

Safety net

A safety net for every prescription and diagnosis

The need in BC. Canadian hospitals recorded 7.5 adverse events per 100 admissions, and 36.9% were preventable.

BC hospitals were in the sample.

In the NOHARM evaluation, omissions made up more than 80% of severe errors.

Estimated about 2,800 hospital bed-days a year, about 7.7 beds open all year

Published evidence: JAMA Network Open, 2025. Korom et al., 2025.

What Kit does, the published evidence, Kit's measured result and the inputs

What Kit does. Before an order is signed or a diagnosis is final, Kit lists what is missing: a monitoring lab, a contraindication, a renal dose adjustment, an interaction, a diagnosis that must not be missed.

It shows a short checklist with BC or Canadian sources, not an essay, because the NOHARM authors found that “AI-assisted physicians frequently omitted valuable AI-generated recommendations.”

Published evidence. Across 25 published reports and 44,640 participants, computerized decision support cut potentially inappropriate prescribing for older emergency patients by 40% (JAMA Network Open, 2025).

In 39,849 primary-care visits at 15 clinics in Nairobi, clinicians with an AI consult tool made 16% fewer diagnostic errors and 13% fewer treatment errors (Korom et al., 2025).

Kit's measured result. On NOHARM, Kit scores 80.0 over 330 cases.

The highest general-purpose model on the public NOHARM board scores 79.7 (Muse Spark 1.1), and 101 US physicians scored 42.2.

Illustrative estimate, inputs shown. 70,000 potentially preventable adverse events a year in Canada × BC's 13.5% population share = 9,463 × 5% prevented with Kit = about 473 × 5.96 extra hospital days each = about 2,820 bed-days ÷ 365 = about 7.7 beds

No dollar value is given, because no sourced BC cost per bed-day is used.

The adverse-event rates come from fiscal-2000 hospital charts and set the scale only.

The 5% input sits well below the 13% and 16% error reductions measured in Nairobi.

Modes, routing and scope
  • Kit Ask keeps simple, quick asks and streams its first words in about 1.5 s (median 1.51 s, n = 690, hosted service).

  • Kit Think Medical takes every medical, health, symptom, drug, dose and emergency question.

    It reads full pages in a bounded research loop of up to 12 tool steps, tests its draft against the leading alternatives, holds dose answers until a deterministic dose check runs and appends one computed safety line.

    The model's sentences are never edited.

  • Kit Think General takes other complex work: documents, multi-step analysis, planning, comparisons and code.

  • Deep Cove Research writes full cited reports in a median of 21.8 minutes (68 runs, hosted service).

    On DeepResearch Bench (board of 2026-10-28), Deep Cove Research Pro ranks #5 of 18 at US$2.50 per report and Deep Cove Research #6 of 18 at US$1.04 per report.

  • Medical questions never take the quick path. In validation, 0 of 47,110 medical benchmark prompts and conversation turns left the medical path.

    On the hosted service, 312 of 312 BC medical test questions went to Kit Think Medical (11–15 November 2026).

  • Health records on Deep Cove. The Records Librarian is read-only by default and acts with the requesting clinician's own access rights, so Kit sees only records that clinician may already open.

    Write actions such as placing orders come in a later release and always need clinician approval.

    The MedAgentBench results were run against the standard health-record interface (FHIR) in a test record system.

  • Notes. Kit drafts from the clinician's summary or the clinic's existing transcript. It does not record visits.

  • Images and documents. Reading is non-diagnostic: documents, forms and general photographs, never medical images for interpretation.

    It runs on a dedicated server from the Hospital size up.

    Diagnostic imaging follows Health Canada's medical-device pathway in a later phase.

Questions officials ask.

Is this a budget saving?

No. It is capacity returned to care. Freed physician and nurse time becomes more visits, faster handovers and safer orders.
