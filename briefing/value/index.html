Five Showcases, Each with Its Source

Each showcase pairs a BC need with what Kit does and the published evidence. Every estimate shows its inputs.

Rural and Remote Teams. Estimate, Inputs Shown

Estimated: about 1,000 rural emergency visits avoided a year

Support at 2 a.m. for Every Rural Health Centre

The Need in BC
More than 200 rural, remote and Indigenous communities work with limited health care resources.
What Kit does
Kit Think Medical puts red flags first and gives BC-guideline next steps. It drafts a one-page summary for the call to Real-Time Virtual Support.
Published Evidence
Adding virtual physicians to BC's 8-1-1 line meant 16.14 fewer emergency visits per 1,000 callers within 30 days. Cressman et al., npj Digital Medicine, 2025.

Illustrative Estimate, Inputs Shown634,976 rural residents × 10% with one Kit-supported advice contact a year × 16.14 per 1,000 = about 1,025. The 16.14 was measured for virtual physicians, not for an AI tool.

Physicians. Estimate, Inputs Shown

Estimated: about 275,000 physician hours a year

Less Paperwork, More Patients

The Need in BC
Canadian physicians lose 20 million hours a year to red tape. BC has 8,263 family physicians.
What Kit does
Kit drafts the chart note, the patient's instructions and the referral letter from the physician's own summary. The physician edits and signs.
Published Evidence
Across 1,637,227 encounters, AI documentation cut note time by 1.21 minutes per encounter. Booth et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2026.

Illustrative Estimate, Inputs Shown8,263 family physicians × 50% using Kit × 20 visits a day × 200 clinic days × 1 minute saved ÷ 60 = about 275,433 hours

Nurses. Estimate, Inputs Shown

Estimated: about 769,000 nurse hours a year, about 395 full-time nurses

Nurses at the Bedside, Not the Keyboard

The Need in BC
In a 36-hospital analysis of 767 nurses, documentation took 35.3% of nursing-practice time.
What Kit does
Kit drafts the SBAR shift handover and writes discharge teaching in plain language. It checks the medication list against the order.
Published Evidence
With AI drafting, outpatient letters went from 7.9 days to 14 minutes to finalize. Journal of Paediatrics and Child Health, 2026.

Illustrative Estimate, Inputs Shown61,547 RNs, LPNs and RPNs × 50% using Kit × 150 shifts a year × 10 minutes saved ÷ 60 = about 769,338 hours ÷ 1,950 hours per full-time nurse = about 395

Patients. Estimate, Inputs Shown

Estimated: about 3,200 emergency visits moved to primary care a year

Clear Next Steps at Any Hour

The Need in BC
29% of BC respondents had no regular family doctor or nurse practitioner in 2022.
What Kit does
Kit supports the nurses and physicians who answer patients. In an emergency, fixed code puts the 9-1-1 instruction first.
Published Evidence
BC's 8-1-1 virtual physicians meant 16.14 fewer emergency visits and 106.14 more primary-care visits per 1,000 users. Costs were neutral. Cressman et al., 2025.

Illustrative Estimate, Inputs Shown200,000 patient contacts a year supported by Kit × 16.14 emergency visits redirected per 1,000 = 3,228

Safety Net. Estimate, Inputs Shown

Estimated: about 2,800 hospital bed-days a year, about 7.7 beds open all year

A Safety Net for Every Prescription and Diagnosis

The Need in BC
Canadian hospitals recorded 7.5 adverse events per 100 admissions, and 36.9% were preventable.
What Kit does
Before an order is signed, Kit lists what is missing, such as a monitoring lab or a contraindication.
Published Evidence
Computerized decision support cut potentially inappropriate prescribing for older emergency patients by 40%. JAMA Network Open, 2025.

Illustrative Estimate, Inputs Shown70,000 potentially preventable adverse events a year in Canada × BC's 13.5% population share × 5% prevented with Kit × 5.96 extra hospital days each = about 2,820 bed-days ÷ 365 = about 7.7 beds

This is capacity returned to care, not a budget cut. Physician and nurse capacity come to about C$96.7 million a year. That is 0.27% of BC's C$36.1 billion health budget. BC replaces every input with its own data.

Expert DetailDollar Values, Inputs and Sources
  • Rural. About C$0.52 million a year at C$504 per emergency visit, the cost used in BC's own 8-1-1 evaluation.
  • Physicians. About C$57.1 million of physician capacity a year at C$207.42 an hour, or about 1.1 million 15-minute visit slots. Specialists are not counted.
  • Nurses. About C$39.6 million of nursing time a year at C$51.41 an hour.
  • Patients. About C$1.63 million of emergency-department cost at C$504 a visit. BC's own evidence shows the shift is cost-neutral overall.
  • Safety net. No dollar value is given. The 5% input sits well below the 13% and 16% error reductions measured in Nairobi (Korom et al., 2025).
  • Set by Deep Cove, replaced with BC data at go-live. 50% use; 20 visits a day; 200 clinic days; 150 shifts; 10 minutes per shift; 10% rural contact rate; 200,000 patient contacts (4%); 5% of preventable adverse events.
  • Sensitivity, physicians. At 0.37 minutes saved per visit, about 101,000 hours a year. At 2.5 minutes, about 689,000 hours.
  • What the evidence measures. The documentation trials measured listening AI scribes. Kit drafts from a summary or an existing transcript, so the base case uses the conservative 1 minute.

What Kit does, by role

Five groups of people, three plain verbs each. Kit drafts and checks; people decide.

Patients

Patients meet Kit through their care team.

  • DirectsSourced next-step options for the care team to discuss.
  • ExplainsAfter-visit instructions in plain language.
  • TranslatesThe same instructions in the patient's language.

“Turn these discharge instructions into plain language, in Punjabi.”

Nurses

  • Hands overAn SBAR shift handover drafted from the nurse's notes.
  • TeachesDischarge teaching at a plain reading level.
  • ChecksThe medication list against the order.

“Turn my notes into an SBAR handover for the night shift.”

Physicians

  • DraftsThe chart note, the patient's instructions and the referral letter.
  • ChecksAn omission checklist before an order is signed.
  • ListsRed flags first, then the diagnoses that must not be missed.

“Chest pain, age 58, normal first troponin. What must I rule out?”

Rural Clinicians

  • FlagsRed flags first, at any hour.
  • GuidesBC-guideline next steps, dose checks and transfer criteria.
  • SummarizesA one-page case summary for Real-Time Virtual Support.

“Summarize this case for the Real-Time Virtual Support physician.”

Administrators

  • AssemblesThe referral package a specialist's intake asks for.
  • ReadsScanned forms and documents, then drafts the reply.
  • ResearchesCited reports for planning and policy from Deep Cove Research.

“Compare published approaches to reducing emergency department waits in rural regions.”

Expert DetailModes, Routing and Scope
  • Medical questions never take the quick path. 312 of 312 BC medical test questions went to Kit Think Medical (11–15 November 2026).
  • Emergencies. 56 of 56 emergency test answers named 9-1-1 in their opening (13–15 November 2026).
  • Health records. The Records Librarian is read-only and acts with the requesting clinician's own access rights.
  • Notes. Kit drafts from the clinician's summary or the clinic's existing transcript. It does not record visits.
  • Images and documents. Reading is non-diagnostic and runs on a dedicated server from the Hospital size up.

How BC will measure value

Acceptance happens once, on site, before go-live. The value measures run every quarter after it, against a baseline BC records first.

Every measure comes from systems BC holds. NHS England asks that AI monitoring data be “collected and analysed independently from the manufacturer's.”1 The audit log sits on site with BC's keys.

Questions officials ask

Is this a budget saving?

No. It is capacity returned to care. Freed physician and nurse time becomes more visits, faster handovers and safer orders.

Does Kit record the visit?

No. Kit drafts the note from the clinician's summary or the clinic's existing transcript.

What does a rural site need on day one?

A managed device and the health authority's private network. A regional Deep Cove serves rural health centres, and questions never cross the public internet.

Why use other programs' results for the estimates?

They are the best published measures of this kind of work. At go-live BC replaces our inputs with its own data and measures the result every quarter.
