The Problem

Hosted AI sends the patient's details to someone else's servers.

Every question typed into a hosted AI service travels to its provider, with any patient details typed into it. If that provider answers to another country's law, it can be compelled to hand over data in its possession, custody or control, wherever the servers sit. BC law also requires a supplementary privacy assessment whenever sensitive personal information is stored outside Canada.

The provider also controls the model. Versions change and retire on the provider's schedule. An answer BC has checked can change without BC's sign-off.

BC has already decided to use AI in care. Budget 2026 commits the province to “responsibly deploy artificial intelligence (AI)… reducing the time that care providers need to spend on administrative tasks.” The open question is who controls it.

Expert DetailLegal Basis
  • Foreign compulsion. The US CLOUD Act reaches data within a provider's “possession, custody, or control” (18 U.S.C. §2713). The Government of Canada's white paper on data sovereignty and public cloud names the primary risk: “the US Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA) and the US government's ability to compel an organization subject to US law to turn over data under its control, regardless of the data's location and without notifying Canada.”
  • BC privacy law. FIPPA s.33.1 allows disclosure outside Canada only as the regulation permits. B.C. Reg. 294/2021 s.2 applies the supplementary assessment to sensitive personal information stored outside Canada. A Deep Cove installation stores and processes all patient information on site, so the assessment is not triggered.
  • Budget 2026, in full: “working with the health authorities and care providers to responsibly deploy artificial intelligence (AI), enabling faster, more efficient service delivery and reducing the time that care providers need to spend on administrative tasks.” BC Budget and Fiscal Plan 2026, p. 14.

Performance and Audit

Tested on the boards where medical AI is graded.

Kit Think ran 12 medical and 4 general public benchmarks on 14–15 November 2026. Ten boards were scored by their own published grader, three multiple-choice boards by exact answer match and three by a named judge whose prompt is in the pack; every answer is published in an audit pack.

Kit scores 80.0 on NOHARM clinical safety (330 cases) and 82.0 on MedAgentBench v2 health-record tasks (300 tasks), each on the board's official grader. On those boards the highest published model scores are 79.7 (NOHARM, Muse Spark 1.1) and 74.1 (MedAgentBench v2, Opus 4.6).

Kit has the top score shown on 4 of the 11 medical boards scored on a common scale; a rival model has it on the other seven. The widest gaps are diagnosis from published case reports (GPT-6.1 Sol 86.7, Kit 63.3) and sequential diagnosis (GPT-6.1 Sol 97.5, Kit 88.0). On the general boards, rival models lead GPQA Diamond and LegalBench.

  • System and settings. Kit Think, one attempt per item per run (PhysicianBench and SDBench: two full runs, both scored), on Deep Cove Intelligence's current hosted service with live web search on, the same kind of search the Web Librarian provides on a Deep Cove installation.   • Grading. Ten boards on their own published grader, unmodified; three multiple-choice boards by exact answer match, with the script in the pack; three that publish no scoring code by a named judge model, with its prompt in the pack.
  • As delivered. Every item was scored as delivered; no answer was edited, re-run or removed after grading. The one exception is two chest X-ray reports the judge's parser could not read, excluded from that board's mean and kept in its pack. Each pack's score recomputes from its own results file, and every zip is listed with its SHA-256 sum.
  • Small public sets. Vals Finance Agent and BrokenArXiv are complete public sets of 27 and 53 items.

Security and Architecture

The Core has no internet path.

Kit runs on the Core, an air-gapped server with no internet path, on hospital power with battery backup and the generator behind it. It cannot browse, send email or open a database. It can only place short, schema-checked orders through the Gates. A librarian on a separate machine checks each order, fetches the minimum needed and hands back plain text.

Patient records never leave the facility. The only outbound crossing is a few search words and the requests for the public pages they return, cleaned by the Web Librarian, with no patient identifiers. A site that chooses the Sealed Library has no outbound line; its library is refreshed through the Delivery Bay. A one-way monitoring link carries security events, never patient text, to the hospital's security operations.

Expert DetailGate Mechanism
  • The orders. The Core can send four kinds of order: a web search, a page read, a library lookup and a record lookup. It can never send a free web address or a database query; its search words are short, checked twice and rebuilt from approved terms. A page read can name only a result the Web Librarian itself returned.
  • The Gates. Each gate is a pair of one-way links that end in single-purpose checking proxies on separate hardware. No network session crosses a gate. Each message is checked against a fixed schema and against business rules. A message that fails is dropped whole, and every message is logged.
  • Record lookups run with the signed-in clinician's own access rights, never through a broad service account.
  • Returned text is rebuilt as plain text before it reaches the Core, so no web page, script or macro arrives as it was fetched.
  • Mapped to recognized standards. The design maps to NIST SP 800-53 (AC-4 information-flow enforcement) and SP 800-207, the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security's network-zone model (ITSP.80.022) and the UK NCSC “Safely Importing Data” pattern. It is mapped to these standards, not certified against them.

Value for BC Health

Rural Clinicians First

634,976 British Columbians, 12.7% of the province, live in rural areas (2021 Census). The clinician on shift there is often a nurse, at night, with no specialist down the hall.

BC's own record shows that remote support keeps rural care open. In 2021, BC's rural virtual physicians covered 39 overnight shifts in Dawson Creek and kept its emergency department from closing. Across rural BC, 76% of these virtual encounters were with nurses at rural health centres.

Kit gives that nurse a sourced second opinion at 2 a.m. Kit Think Medical puts red flags first, then next steps with BC sources such as BC Guidelines and HealthLink BC, with any dose checked before it is shown. It also drafts a one-page summary for the call to the virtual support line. Kit adds to BC's virtual support networks; it does not replace them.

Kit prepares the answer. The nurse and the physician on call make the decision.

Privacy and Governance

The clinician decides. BC holds the record.

Kit is clinical decision-support software for licensed professionals. The clinician decides.

Image and document reading is non-diagnostic. Diagnostic imaging follows the Health Canada medical-device pathway.

Every answer shows its sources, so the clinician can review the basis and make the decision. When a question describes an emergency, the 9-1-1, poison-centre or 9-8-8 instruction comes first, written by fixed code.

BC can audit everything Kit does. Every question, lookup and answer is logged on BC's storage, and BC's privacy officer can read the log. Before go-live, each installation passes an independent penetration test. The delivery plan includes an ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 42001 programme.

Questions from Officials

Where does patient data go, and who can see it?

It stays in the facility. Kit reads a record only through the Records Librarian, and only what the signed-in clinician may already see. The only things that leave are a few search words, cleaned by the Web Librarian, with no patient identifiers, and requests for the public pages they return. A site on the Sealed Library has no outbound line. For rural sites served by a regional installation, questions travel only on the provincial health network between BC health-authority facilities. Deep Cove Intelligence has no remote access and holds no copy.

Is patient data used to train the AI?

No. A Deep Cove installation has no training system. The model files are read-only and checked against their fingerprint at every start. The agreement forbids any use of BC data for training, tuning or product improvement, and that clause survives the end of the contract.

Which AI model runs inside?

Deep Cove does not trust the model. The model runs on the Core with no network, no credentials and no write path. The only thing it can do is place schema-checked orders through the Gates, and separate machines decide whether to act on them. The model's identity and its evaluations are provided to the buyer under NDA in due diligence.

How fast is it?

On Kit's current hosted service, measured with test questions (no BC patient data), Kit Ask streams its first words in about 1.5 seconds (median of 690 measurements), and Kit Think shows its first progress line within about half a second and finishes a hard medical question in about one to two minutes. On a Deep Cove installation, Kit Ask is specified at 1 second or less to first words (95th percentile) at the guaranteed capacity, answers stream in the range of the fastest cloud AI services, and Kit Think completion time is contracted for each size.

How is it updated?

Only through the Delivery Bay. Each update arrives signed, then is checked and safety-tested on a separate machine BC owns. BC's security officer and clinical safety officer sign it off, and two people install it in person. The previous version stays installed, so rollback is one step.

What if Deep Cove Intelligence is no longer there?

Kit keeps working. The installation needs nothing from Deep Cove Intelligence to answer questions: there is no licence server, no call-home and no remote off switch. The agreement sets the continuity terms, including a perpetual licence to the installed version and source-code escrow.

What is Kit not for?

Kit does not make the diagnosis or prescribe. It lays out the differential, red flags and next steps with their sources, and the clinician decides. It is decision-support software for licensed professionals. Image and document reading is non-diagnostic. Diagnostic imaging will follow the Health Canada medical-device pathway before it is offered. In an emergency, Kit's first line tells the person to call 9-1-1.
