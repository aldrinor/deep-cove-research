Abstract

Medical AI is often presented with a single score from a single benchmark, with no grader named and no answers to check. Results a health system can rely on need many boards, the newest rival models beside every score, each board's own grader, and every graded answer open to inspection. We ran Kit Think on 12 medical and 4 general benchmarks on 14–15 November 2026 and published Kit's answers, grades and checksums for each one. Kit posts the highest score shown on 4 of the 11 medical boards scored on a common scale, including clinical safety and health-record tasks; rival models score higher on diagnosis from published case records; and anyone can recompute all 16 results from the packs.

1Scores by Board

Sixteen boards, each shown with the newest Anthropic and OpenAI models with a published score, or run by us on the same items where the detail below says so (PhysicianBench, SDBench and MedCaseReasoning). On MedQA the OpenAI cell is OpenAI's highest published figure. Every row links to its audit pack. The two MAST image boards measure reading of public research images; as delivered, Kit does not interpret medical images, and its image and document reading runs on a dedicated server from the Hospital size up.

2Results at a Glance

Kit posts the highest score shown on 4 of the 11 medical boards scored on the same scale as their rivals. They are the two health-record boards (94.3 and 82.0), clinical safety on NOHARM (80.0) and reasoning under uncertainty on SCT (72.8). On MAST Chest X-Ray, Kit's 67.8 uses our stated conversion of the CRIMSON score; MAST does not publish its own, so that row is shown but not counted.

A rival model posts the top score on the other seven. The widest gaps are diagnosis from published case reports, where GPT-6.1 Sol scores 86.7 on MedCaseReasoning against Kit's 63.3, and sequential diagnosis on SDBench, 97.5 against 88.0. On MedQA and PhysicianBench the gap is under two points.

3How we measured

Every Kit number on this page comes from runs graded by the board's rules. Kit Think answered every item on 14–15 November 2026 (UTC): once on fourteen boards, and twice in full on PhysicianBench and SDBench, where both runs count. Every answer was scored as delivered: no answer was edited, re-run or removed after grading. One exception: two chest X-ray reports the judge's parser could not read are excluded from the 67.8 mean; scoring them at the minimum gives 67.6, and both are in the pack.

Ten boards were scored by their own published grader, unmodified. On MAST Chest X-Ray the CRIMSON score is converted to 0–100 by the formula in the pack. Three multiple-choice boards, MedQA, GPQA Diamond and LegalBench, were scored by exact letter or answer match, with the script in the pack. Three publish no grading code, so their packs name the judge model and include its prompt or rubric: MedCaseReasoning, BrokenArXiv and Vals Finance Agent.

Each rival number is either published or run by us, and the detail below says which. Published figures come from the board's own leaderboard, the model maker or an independent evaluator. On PhysicianBench, SDBench and MedCaseReasoning, we ran the rival models ourselves on the same items Kit answered.

Common to All 16 Boards

  • System. Kit Think in its evaluated configuration, run with each board's instructions, tools and time limits (up to 1,170 seconds per item; see Time Limits and Configuration). Medical boards ran on the medical path; image boards used Kit's image reading. The installed configuration is re-scored on site at acceptance.
  • Attempts. One per item on fourteen boards. PhysicianBench and SDBench were each run twice in full, and both runs count (200 scored runs each).
  • Web search. On for every board that allows it, through Kit's controlled search path on its current hosted service, with personal details removed from every search. On a Deep Cove installation the same lookups go through the Web Librarian, and the on-site score re-run at acceptance measures that configuration. The health-record boards ran against each benchmark's own test record server through the standard FHIR interface. No real patient record was used on any board.
  • Human Expert. A physician score appears only where the board's authors measured physicians and published the conditions: NOHARM and HealthBench Professional.
  • Sets under 100 items. BrokenArXiv (53 problems) and Vals Finance Agent (27 tasks) are complete public sets, labelled as such by their count line. One BrokenArXiv problem is worth up to 1.9 points; one Vals task up to 3.7.

4How Kit answers a question

A router written in code picks the path before a model writes a word. Anything about health, medicines, doses or emergencies goes to Kit Think Medical; when the rules are unsure, the answer is medical. Deep Cove Research runs when a full, cited report is asked for.

No mode reaches a source on its own. In Deep Cove, every lookup is a fixed-form order through the Gates to a librarian: the Web Librarian for public sources, the Records Librarian for the clinician's own permitted records. The modes differ only in how many orders they place.

5Inside Each Audit Pack

Every score on this page ships with Kit's answers, the grades and the recipe to recompute it. Each pack is a zip of plain files. It holds no benchmark question text and no patient-record content, and nothing in it was edited after grading.

What each pack holds

  • README.md · the benchmark and its release, the system and run dates, the settings, the grader, the score and the formula that recomputes it.
  • results.jsonl and results.csv · one row per item: the official item id, Kit's delivered answer and its grade, plus board-specific fields.
  • actions.jsonl · on the agent boards: every request or test Kit sent, in order. Server replies and patient-chart text are left out.
  • grader/ · where the board needs it: the letter-match script, the judge prompt, the case edits.
  • SHA256SUMS · a checksum for every file in the pack.

audit/INDEX.json (machine-readable index) lists each pack's slug, item count, score and zip checksum; audit/SHA256SUMS covers every zip.
