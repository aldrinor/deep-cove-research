One line crosses outward. It carries search words only.

Kit reads current medical guidance, so one controlled line reaches the internet: the Web Librarian's. It carries short search words out and public pages back.

The Web Librarian rebuilds each page as plain text before anything reaches the Core.

The line never holds patient data, and the Core never touches the internet.

A mechanism blocks identifiers, not a policy. A check inside the Core and a second, independently written check on the Web Librarian refuse any search that contains the patient's name, Personal Health Number, date of birth, address, phone or chart number.

The Web Librarian then rebuilds the query from an approved medical vocabulary, so the AI's own words are never sent as written.

Sites that want no outbound line at all choose the Sealed Library. A curated medical library is kept on site and refreshed through the Delivery Bay, so no search words leave the facility.

How the outbound check works

  • Layer 1, inside the Core. The order check knows the identifiers in the current session, supplied by the Records Librarian and the Front Door.

    It refuses any search containing them, in exact or normalized form, or any number shaped like an identifier.

  • Layer 2, on the Web Librarian, written separately. Pattern detectors for BC Personal Health Number shapes and other health-card and chart-number shapes, full dates, Canadian postal codes, phone numbers, email and street addresses; person-name detection.

    A vocabulary allow-list in which every word must belong to an approved medical and general lexicon.

    Numbers only with units (“500 mg”, “38.5 °C”).

    Shape and randomness checks that refuse encoded strings.

  • Rebuild. The Web Librarian assembles the outbound query from the canonical vocabulary forms of the accepted words.

    The AI's text is never forwarded byte for byte.

  • Rate caps. Per-session and per-site limits on searches per minute and per day keep any hidden channel to a very low, logged rate.

  • No identity on the line. One shared outbound address for the site.

    No cookies, no accounts and no user identifiers go to any search service.

    The fetching machine is rebuilt from a clean image every 24 hours.

  • Legal reading. FIPPA defines personal information as “recorded information about an identifiable individual” (Schedule 1).

    The cleaned query is built to carry none, and BC's privacy office records that determination in its PIA.

    The cleaned search words are sent to a public search service that may operate outside Canada.

    Because they are built to carry no personal information, s.33.1 is not engaged.

    BC's privacy office records that determination in its PIA, and a site that does not accept it chooses the Sealed Library.

  • Sealed Library. A per-site option.

    The library draws on open public sources (for example PubMed and Health Canada's Drug Product Database) plus references BC licenses, and its refreshes arrive on sterilized media.

    With it, the Web Librarian has no internet line.

Never used for training. BC can verify it.

The statutes, section by section

    Breach notification “without unreasonable delay” where significant harm could reasonably be expected. s.69(5.3): a public body that is not a ministry “must conduct a privacy impact assessment”.

Log design and assurance
  • Hash-chained, tamper-evident journal; the chain head goes to write-once storage.
  • Encrypted at rest, role-restricted; logs hold personal health information and get the same protection.

  • Retention set by BC (minimum per FIPPA s.31 or PIPA s.35), followed by scheduled crypto-shredding, with a deletion record.

    Each retention period's entries are encrypted under their own key.

    Destroying the key at the end of the period deletes the content, while the chain of hashes still verifies.

  • Whether intermediate model reasoning is kept is a BC setting.
  • Alarms (gate rejections, integrity checks, tamper) go one way to BC's security operations over a one-way link that carries no patient text.
  • Break-glass administration needs several named custodians, sends an alarm at once and gets a review afterwards.
  • Anyone from Deep Cove Intelligence who works on the system passes a criminal record check and signs a confidentiality undertaking.

  • Controls map to NIST SP 800-53 (AU-9 audit protection, AU-10 non-repudiation, AC-3(2) dual authorization) and to BC's Defensible Security control groups.

Health Canada's four criteria

Health Canada's Software as a Medical Device (SaMD): Definition and Classification sets four criteria.

Software that meets all four is outside device regulation:

  1. “not intended to acquire, process, or analyze a medical image or a signal from an IVDD”;
  2. “intended to display, analyze, or print medical information about a patient or other medical information”;
  3. “only intended to support a health care professional, patient or non healthcare professional caregiver in making decisions”;
  4. “not intended to replace the clinical judgement of a health care professional to make a clinical diagnosis”.

Its key test: “The intended user should have access to the basis for the software's recommendation, so the user can independently review and rely on their own judgement.”

Kit's cited answers are built to meet it.

Kit stays inside these criteria because, as delivered, it does not accept medical images or signals (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, pathology slides, ECG tracings) for interpretation.

It reads documents, forms and general photographs only.

Deep Cove Intelligence offers Kit to licensed professionals as decision support: it never makes the diagnosis or the triage decision itself.

It lists possibilities, red flags and next steps with their sources, and the licensed clinician decides.

Dose output is a referenced calculation the clinician can check against its source.

The intended-use statement appears in the agreement and in the interface.

Diagnostic imaging would be a Class II or higher licence under Health Canada's Pre-market guidance for machine learning-enabled medical devices (page modified 17 September 2026), which allows a predetermined change control plan for pre-authorized model updates.

Questions from privacy officers.

Straight answers to the questions privacy offices, officials and security teams ask first.

Patient data

Does patient data leave the facility?

No. Patient records never leave the facility.

The only outbound crossing is a cleaned search query from the Web Librarian, and the requests for the public pages it returns, with no patient identifiers.

Each query is checked twice and rebuilt from a medical vocabulary before it leaves.

With the Sealed Library option, the Web Librarian has no outbound line, and no search words leave the facility.

Is patient data used to train Kit?

No. Deep Cove ships with no training system, its model is a read-only file checked against its signed fingerprint at every start, and the agreement bars training, tuning, evaluation, product improvement, analytics and market research on BC's data.

Deep Cove Intelligence never receives the data, so it has nothing to train on.

Can Deep Cove Intelligence see our data?

No. There is no remote access path.

Our engineers work on site only, escorted by BC staff, and the log records every entry and console session.

Deep Cove Intelligence holds no copy of patient data and no data key.

Who holds the keys?

BC does. BC's hardware security modules generate the data keys and disk recovery keys, and BC holds them.

Five named BC custodians each hold a share of the break-glass credentials, in sealed envelopes in separate safes, and it takes any three of them.

Deep Cove Intelligence holds only the key that signs its software releases, which proves an update is genuine and opens no data.

Does Kit record the visit?

No. Kit does not capture audio.

It drafts notes from the clinician's own summary or the clinic's existing transcript, and the clinician reviews and signs.

BC law and governance

Does BC's cross-border privacy assessment apply?

No. B.C. Reg. 294/2021 requires it only where sensitive personal information is stored outside Canada, and Deep Cove stores nothing outside Canada.

The normal privacy impact assessment still applies, and we deliver a PIA Input Pack written under the headings of BC's PIA Directions, ready to copy into your template.

How long are records and logs kept?

As long as BC decides. BC sets retention at installation: at least one year where an answer is used for a decision about a person, as FIPPA s.31 requires.

At the end of each period, the system securely deletes the data on schedule and logs the deletion.

How do we handle an access request or a breach?

From the log. BC can export every question, lookup and answer by person and date range for an access request.

For a suspected breach, the log shows exactly who saw what, and when.

Deep Cove Intelligence notifies the head of the public body immediately, as FIPPA s.30.5(2) requires.

Is Deep Cove certified?

Not by a regulator, because no Canadian privacy regulator certifies AI systems. Deep Cove is designed to align with FIPPA, the E-Health Act and the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security's guidance, and its controls map to NIST SP 800-53.

An independent penetration test before go-live and an ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 42001 programme are part of the delivery plan.

The system

Which AI model runs inside?

Deep Cove does not trust the model. The model has no network, no credentials and no write path.

It can only place schema-checked orders through the Gates, and separate machines check each one.

The buyer receives the model's identity and its evaluations under NDA in due diligence.

Is Kit a medical device?

Kit is clinical decision-support software for licensed professionals, and the clinician decides. It shows the basis for every answer, and its image and document reading is non-diagnostic.

Diagnostic imaging follows Health Canada's medical-device pathway in a later phase, when BC asks for it.

What happens if the internet line is cut?

Kit keeps answering. The Core never uses the internet, so only live web lookups pause.

Sites with the Sealed Library see no change at all.

The Core runs on hospital power, with battery backup and the hospital generator behind it.

What if Deep Cove Intelligence stops trading?

The installed system keeps running. It needs no licence server, no connection to Deep Cove Intelligence and no outside service to answer.

The agreement sets the continuity terms, including a perpetual licence to the installed version and source-code escrow.
