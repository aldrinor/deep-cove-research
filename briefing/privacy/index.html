What never happens to patient data

Each statement names the mechanism that makes it true and how BC checks it.

Records Kept in the Facility

Patient records never leave the facility. The only outbound crossing is a cleaned search query with no patient identifiers.

How BC checks itEvery outbound query is logged. BC's privacy officer receives a daily sample.

No Training on Patient Records

Patient records are never used to train any model. Deep Cove is delivered with no training system.

How BC checks itThe software inventory shipped with every release, and the model fingerprint checked at every start.

No Vendor Copy, No Remote Access

Deep Cove Intelligence holds no copy of patient data. Our engineers work on site only, escorted.

How BC checks itThe access log records every entry and every console session.

No Reporting Home

Nothing reports back to Deep Cove Intelligence. No vendor telemetry, usage analytics or licence check leaves the facility.

How BC checks itThe outbound allow-list shows every destination the system can reach.

No Action without a Gate Check

The AI never reaches the internet or a database by itself. It can only place fixed-form orders through the Gates.

How BC checks itThe independent penetration test before go-live covers the Core, the Gates and the update path.

Only Records the Clinician May Open

Kit never reads a record the signed-in clinician is not allowed to see.

How BC checks itEach record request is logged with the clinician, the purpose and the fields returned.

See every crossing in the security architecture

The one line that crosses outward

One controlled line reaches the internet: the Web Librarian's. It carries short search words out and public pages back, never patient data.

Sites that want no outbound line choose the Sealed Library.

Expert DetailHow the Outbound Check Works
  • Refused. Any search with a patient's name, health number, birth date, address or phone. Identifiers are blocked by mechanism, not by policy.
  • Two layers. A check inside the Core knows the identifiers in the current session. A second check, on the Web Librarian, is written separately: pattern detectors, person-name detection and a vocabulary allow-list.
  • Rebuild. The outbound query is assembled from approved vocabulary. The AI's text is never forwarded byte for byte.
  • No identity on the line. One shared outbound address for the site; no cookies, accounts or user identifiers. The fetching machine is rebuilt from a clean image every 24 hours.
  • Legal reading. FIPPA defines personal information as “recorded information about an identifiable individual” (Schedule 1). The cleaned query is built to carry none, and BC's privacy office records that determination in its PIA. A site that does not accept it chooses the Sealed Library.

Never used for training, and how BC verifies it

A training ban is stronger when the machine has no training system. Deep Cove has no training jobs, no training data store and a read-only model.

The contract says the same thing in plain words.

Four checks BC can run itself

  1. Read the software inventory

    Every release lists its components. BC can confirm that no training pipeline is present.

  2. Compare the fingerprint

    The model's fingerprint is written to BC's log at every start.

  3. Read the crossings

    The log lists everything that crossed the wall around the Core.

  4. Test it independently

    An independent penetration test runs before go-live, and the full report goes to BC.

Expert DetailThe Contract Clause and the Regulator's Question
  • The clause. BC's data may not be used for training, tuning, evaluation, product improvement, analytics or market research. That clause survives the end of the agreement.
  • The regulator's question. BC's Information and Privacy Commissioner tells health organizations what to ask a vendor. One question is whether it will use data “for any secondary purposes (e.g., ... training the AI model)”. For Deep Cove, the answer is no.

OIPC BC, “PIPA and AI scribes: best practices for healthcare organizations in BC”.

Expert DetailThe Statutes, Section by Section
  • FIPPA s.33.1: “A public body may disclose personal information outside of Canada only if the disclosure is in accordance with the regulations, if any, made by the minister.”
  • s.30: protect personal information “by making reasonable security arrangements against such risks as unauthorized collection, use, disclosure or disposal.”
  • s.30.5(2): a service provider's employee who knows of an unauthorized disclosure “must immediately notify the head of the public body.”
  • s.31: information used “to make a decision that directly affects the individual” is kept “for at least one year after being used”.
  • Which statute. Health authorities and hospitals fall under FIPPA; private clinics under PIPA. Each site's privacy office confirms which applies.

Audit Rights

Every question, lookup and answer is written to a log that BC owns. The log is hash-chained, so any edit shows.

BC can audit everything; Deep Cove Intelligence can audit nothing on its own.

What each log entry holds

  • Who asked, when, and from which session
  • Every order the AI placed, and the gate's decision
  • The sources returned, by reference
  • The model's fingerprint and the release in use
  • The answer the clinician saw

What BC can do with it

  • Answer access requests. Export by person and date range.
  • Scope an incident. See who saw what, and when.
  • Audit us. The agreement gives BC and the Commissioner that right.
  • Watch the outbound line. A daily sample goes to the privacy officer.
Expert DetailLog Design and Assurance
  • The log is encrypted, stored on BC's hardware and kept for as long as BC decides.
  • Reading the log is itself logged. Our engineers see the log only on site, under BC supervision.
  • Retention is set by BC, followed by scheduled crypto-shredding with a deletion record.
  • Whether intermediate model reasoning is kept is a BC setting.
  • Controls are mapped to NIST SP 800-53 (AU-9, AU-10, AC-3(2)). “Mapped to” describes our design; it is not a certification.

The clinician decides

Kit does not make clinical decisions. Licensed clinicians do.

Every answer shows its sources, so the clinician can check the basis and decide.

BC governs every change. A BC change board decides whether and when each update is installed.

Image and document reading is non-diagnostic. Diagnostic imaging follows Health Canada's medical-device pathway, in a later phase, when BC asks for it.

Medical-Device Pathway

Diagnostic imaging, later, under a Health Canada licence.

Expert DetailHealth Canada's Four Criteria
  • Health Canada's guidance on Software as a Medical Device sets four criteria. Software that meets all four is outside device regulation:
  • “not intended to acquire, process, or analyze a medical image or a signal from an IVDD”;
  • “intended to display, analyze, or print medical information about a patient or other medical information”;
  • “only intended to support a health care professional, patient or non healthcare professional caregiver in making decisions”;
  • “not intended to replace the clinical judgement of a health care professional to make a clinical diagnosis”.
  • As delivered, Kit does not accept medical images or signals for interpretation. The intended-use statement is written into the agreement and shown in the interface.

First Nations Data Governance

First Nations hold rights over data about First Nations people, and those rights come first. Before any site handles such data, we will engage the First Nations Health Authority and the communities concerned. We follow the governance they set.

The data stays in BC facilities, under BC's keys and log. The First Nations principles of OCAP® (ownership, control, access and possession) can be applied there.

Questions from Privacy Officers

Does patient data leave the facility?

No. The only outbound crossing is a cleaned search query with no patient identifiers. With the Sealed Library option, no search words leave either.

Can Deep Cove Intelligence see our data?

No. There is no remote access path. Our engineers work on site only, escorted by BC staff.

Who holds the keys?

BC does. Deep Cove Intelligence holds only the key that signs its software releases, which opens no data.

Does Kit record the visit?

No. Kit does not capture audio. It drafts notes from the clinician's own summary or the clinic's existing transcript.

Does BC's cross-border privacy assessment apply?

No. B.C. Reg. 294/2021 requires it only where sensitive personal information is stored outside Canada. The normal privacy impact assessment still applies.

How long are records and logs kept?

As long as BC decides. FIPPA s.31 sets a minimum of one year where an answer is used for a decision about a person.

Is Deep Cove certified?

Not by a regulator, because no Canadian privacy regulator certifies AI systems. An independent penetration test and an ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 42001 programme are part of the delivery plan.

Which AI model runs inside?

Deep Cove does not trust the model. It has no network, no credentials and no write path. Its identity is provided to the buyer under NDA in due diligence.

What if Deep Cove Intelligence stops trading?

The installed system keeps running. It needs no licence server and no connection to Deep Cove Intelligence. The agreement includes a perpetual licence and source-code escrow.
