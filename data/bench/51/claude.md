# Japan’s Elderly (65+) Consumer Market, 2020–2050: Population, Spending Baseline, and Market-Size Model

Japan’s 65+ population will stay at roughly 36–40 million throughout 2020–2050, peaking at 39.53 million in 2043. [ipss +3](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-zenkoku/e/zenkoku_e2023/pp2023e_Summary.pdf) My bottom-up model shows elderly-headed households’ private consumption holding at about ¥59–64 trillion a year in 2025 prices (≈US$390–430 billion), peaking around 2040–2045. Adding public-insurance medical and long-term-care spending on the elderly gives a broad “silver market” that grows from about ¥97 trillion in 2025 to about ¥113–115 trillion in 2040–2050 (≈US$650–765 billion). The market’s size is close to locked in by demographics. Its make-up is not: it is shifting from active 65–74 consumers toward 75+, solo-living, health-and-care-dependent households.

## TL;DR

- **Population:**  Under the IPSS 2023 medium projection, the 65+ population goes from 36.03 million (28.6%) in 2020 to 39.28 million (34.8%) in 2040, then 38.88 million (37.1%) in 2050, while total population falls from 126.15 million to 104.69 million. [ipss](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-zenkoku/e/zenkoku_e2023/pp2023e_Summary.pdf)[ipss](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-zenkoku/e/zenkoku_e2023/pp2023e_Summary.pdf) The 75+ group passes 21.5 million in 2025 and reaches about 24.3 million in 2050. Households headed by someone 65+ rise from 20.97 million to a 24.31 million peak in 2045. Single-person elderly households rise from 7.38 million to 10.84 million. [ipss](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-ajsetai/j/HPRJ2024/hprj2024_gaiyo_20240412.pdf)
- **Market size (author’s model, 2025 prices):**  Private consumption by 65+-headed households is about ¥58.7tn in 2020, ¥59.5tn in 2025, ¥60.2tn in 2030, ¥64.0tn in 2040 and ¥62.6tn in 2050. Adding public medical and care spending (net of overlap) gives about ¥97tn in 2025, ¥113tn in 2040 and ¥115tn in 2050. These figures match Mizuho Bank’s ¥96.4tn (2023) and ¥114.7tn (2040, real) estimates. [mizuhobank](https://www.mizuhobank.co.jp/corporate/industry/pdf/msif_241.pdf)
- **Where the growth is:**  Private per-household spending is flat to falling with age, so headline consumption grows only about 0.2%/yr in real terms. Real growth sits in public medical care (~1.3%/yr) and long-term care (~1.5%/yr), and in services for 75+ and solo seniors: meal delivery, mobility substitutes, home monitoring, renovation and end-of-life services. Apparel and private-car spending will stagnate or shrink.

## Definitions and Assumptions

- **Elderly**  = age 65+ (Japan’s statistical standard). The report splits out **65–74 (“early elderly”, 前期高齢者)**  and **75+ (“late elderly”, 後期高齢者)** , plus 85+ where data allows.
- **Currency:**  trillions of yen (兆円). USD conversions use an illustrative **¥150 = US$1**  and are only for orientation.
- **Real vs nominal:**  The model is in **constant 2025 prices** : 2025 Family Income and Expenditure Survey (FIES / 家計調査) spending per household, held constant in real terms in the baseline. Source figures are nominal unless labelled otherwise. Mizuho’s 2040 numbers are given in both real and price-adjusted terms.
- **Consumption unit:**  households whose **head**  is 65+ (IPSS household projections × FIES spending). This includes some spending by younger members of multi-generation households and excludes elderly people living in younger-headed households or in institutions.
- **Source data vs my estimates:**  Every figure taken from a source is attributed to it. Figures labelled “model” or “author’s estimate” are my own calculations.

**Key milestones**

- **The “2025 problem”:**  The Cabinet Office notes that 65+ reached a projected 36.53 million in 2025, the year the dankai (1947–49) baby boomers all turned 75. [cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/zenbun/s1_1_1.html) Actual 2025 estimates show 75+ (21.27 million) well ahead of 65–74 (14.95 million) (Statistics Bureau Population Estimates, Oct 1, 2025). [cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei///whitepaper/w-2026/html/zenbun/s1_1_1.html)
- **Peak elderly population:**  IPSS projects 65+ to peak at **39.53 million in 2043** , after the second baby-boom cohort (born 1971–74) has entered old age. [cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/zenbun/s1_1_1.html)[cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/zenbun/pdf/1s1s_01.pdf) The previous projection had a peak of 39.35 million in 2042. [ipss](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-zenkoku/j/zenkoku2023/pp2023_gaiyou.pdf)
- **Aging rate:**  It crosses 33.3% (one in three) in 2037, reaches 37.1% in 2050 and 38.7% in 2070. [ipss](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-zenkoku/j/zenkoku2023/pp2023_gaiyou.pdf)[cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/zenbun/s1_1_1.html)
- **The 65–74 group**  shrinks to about 14.4 million around 2030, rebounds to 17.36 million in 2041 as the dankai juniors arrive, then falls. The **75+ group**  peaks only in 2055. [cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/zenbun/s1_1_1.html)
- **Total population**  falls from 126.15 million (2020) to 104.69 million (2050), drops below 100 million in 2056 and reaches 87.0 million in 2070. [ipss +2](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-zenkoku/e/zenkoku_e2023/pp2023e_Summary.pdf)
- **Deaths**  keep rising until about 2040 and then fall, which matters for funeral and inheritance markets (Cabinet Office). [cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/zenbun/s1_1_1.html)

These come from third-party compilations of the WPP 2024 dataset (Worldometer / PopulationPyramid.net), not the UN tables directly. [populationpyramid +2](https://www.populationpyramid.net/japan/2050/) The UN figure is within about 1% of IPSS, so the demographic base is robust across sources.

**Interpretation**

- The pension-debate model couple spends about **¥264,000/month**  against about **¥222,000**  of disposable income. [jili](https://www.jili.or.jp/lifeplan/lifesecurity/1130.html)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf) The roughly ¥42,000 monthly gap is covered by drawing down savings. Pensions and other social-security benefits provide about 90% of income. [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf)
- Elderly singles spend about **¥148,000/month** , with a ¥30,000 monthly deficit and an average propensity to consume of 125%. [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf)[jili](https://www.jili.or.jp/lifeplan/lifesecurity/1130.html)
- **Engel coefficient:**  Food takes **29.9%**  of the elderly couple’s spending, above the all-household average of **28.6%**  in 2025, which rose 0.3 points year on year (FIES 2025). [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/2025np/pdf/summary.pdf) After the 2022–25 food-price inflation, the elderly Engel coefficient is near 30%.
- **Age gradient (two-or-more-person households, 2025):**  Monthly spending is ¥327,405 for heads aged 60–69, ¥264,332 for 70+ and ¥275,635 for 65+. [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/2025np/pdf/summary.pdf) Among non-working 65+ households, 65–69 spend the most (¥296,997) and spending falls with each older age band. The 75+ group’s disposable income is ¥221,235. [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/2025np/pdf/summary.pdf)
- **Homeownership:**  For two-or-more-person households, ownership is **94.8%**  for heads aged 65+ and 95.1% for 70+ (FIES 2025). [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/2025np/pdf/summary.pdf) Elderly housing spending is therefore low, mostly repairs and maintenance rather than rent. The exception is single renters.

### 2.2 Share of national consumption

Mizuho Bank estimates elderly-headed household consumption at **¥55.7tn out of about ¥157tn**  of household consumption in 2023, about **35%** , rising to **¥60.5tn of ¥151tn (~40%) in 2040**  in real terms. [mizuhobank](https://www.mizuhobank.co.jp/corporate/industry/pdf/msif_241.pdf) That is the more defensible figure for private consumption. The often-quoted “40–50%” is only reached if public medical and care spending on the elderly is also counted.

### 2.3 Wealth and income capacity

- **Financial assets:**  Bank of Japan Flow of Funds put household financial assets at **¥2,351tn at end-2025**  (+5.3% y/y), and Nikkei reports ¥2,519tn in the April–June 2026 preliminary data. [nikkei +2](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGKKZO91408770Z10C25A9EE9000/) Nikkei estimates that **households headed by people aged 60+ hold around 60%** . It also reports polarization: according to J-FLEC’s 2025 Survey of Household Financial Behavior (家計の金融行動に関する世論調査2025年, fielded June 20–July 2, 2025, as reported by Kabutan/Moneiro), **30.4% of single households in their 60s hold no financial assets** , against 12.8% of 60s households with two or more people.
- **Employment (Cabinet Office FY2026 Ageing Society White Paper, 2025 data):**  Employment rates are **54.5% for 65–69** , 36.2% for 70–74 and 12.6% for 75+. About **9.42 million**  people aged 65+ are employed, the 22nd straight annual increase. [hojyokin-portal](https://hojyokin-portal.jp/columns/koreisha-koyou)[cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2026/zenbun/pdf/1s2s_01.pdf) Employment rates for 65–69 rose 13.5 points over the decade to 2024. [cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/gaiyou/s1_1.html)[cao](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/zenbun/s1_2_1.html) Working elderly households spend much more: two-or-more-person worker households with a 60+ head spend ¥335,910/month (FIES 2025). [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/2025np/pdf/summary.pdf) This is the main lever for raising elderly spending.
- **The “¥20 million problem” (老後2,000万円問題):**  The 2019 Financial Services Agency working-group figure came from multiplying the then-current monthly deficit of about ¥55,000 over 30 years (≈¥19.6m on 2017 data). On 2024 FIES data the same calculation gives about **¥12.3 million**  (¥34,058 × 12 × 30), according to FP research firm FPI-J. [fpi-j](https://fpi-j.com/column/column5664/) On the 2025 deficit (¥42,434) it gives about ¥15.3 million (my calculation). The gap is shrinking, but the public debate has entrenched precautionary saving.

## Part 3 — Willingness to Spend and Behavioral Shifts

**Wealth without spending.**  Elderly households run deficits (propensity to consume above 115%), yet aggregate household assets keep reaching records, driven by market gains and new NISA inflows. [nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUB179IQ0X10C25A9000000/)[yutaka-trusty](https://www.yutaka-trusty.co.jp/market/blog_oogo/%E8%B3%87%E9%87%91%E5%BE%AA%E7%92%B0%E7%B5%B1%E8%A8%88%EF%BC%9A2025%E5%B9%B4%E6%9C%AB%E6%99%82%E7%82%B9%E3%81%AE%E5%AE%B6%E8%A8%88%E3%81%AE%E9%87%91%E8%9E%8D%E8%B3%87%E7%94%A3%E3%80%8112%E5%9B%9B/) The Statistics Bureau’s FIES savings and liabilities data, as analysed by Dai-ichi Life Research Institute, show that drawdown has now become visible among elderly non-working households: their savings fell by ¥660,000 (−2.6%) to ¥24.94 million in 2025, the first fall in six years, while savings of all two-or-more-person households rose 3.8% to a record ¥20.59 million. Mizuho’s 2025 study argues that the health and employment profile of seniors has “become about 5 years younger” over 15 years. It calculates that if 65–79-year-olds in 2040 behave like people five years younger, **elderly income rises by ¥22tn but consumption by only ¥3tn** , while medical and care costs fall by ¥5tn. [mizuhobank](https://www.mizuhobank.co.jp/corporate/industry/pdf/msif_241.pdf) In other words, more of the extra income from longer working lives goes into saving than into spending.

**Digital adoption.**  In Ministry of Internal Affairs (MIC) data, the share of people using smartphones to access the internet rose from 15.7% (2014) to **78.8% (2024) for people in their 60s** , from 5.3% to **53.0% for those in their 70s** , and from 1.2% to just 18.7% for those 80+ (2025 Information and Communications White Paper). [soumu](https://www.soumu.go.jp/johotsusintokei/whitepaper/ja/r07/html/nd111110.html)[insection](https://insection.co.jp/slide/article/elderly-smartphone-trends-157pct-788pct/) In 2024, online travel spending by households headed by someone 70+ grew 17.8%, the fastest of any age group (Statistics Bureau, Survey of Household Economy). [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/joukyou/2024ar/gaikyou/pdf/gk01.pdf) The 65–74 group is now digitally reachable. The 80+ group, the fastest-growing segment, mostly is not, and it needs assisted channels (phone, catalog, in-person, family-mediated).

**Goods → services/experiences.**  In 2025, spending categories in FIES that rose in real terms included recreational services (+3.6%), eating out (+1.8%) and transport (+6.0%), while clothing fell 1.8%. [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/2025np/pdf/summary.pdf) Elderly couples spent more on transport and communication (+¥3,557/month in 2025) and recreation (+¥1,161). [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2024.pdf)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf) Spending by younger seniors is moving toward travel, dining and leisure.

**Health and long-term care.**  Long-term care insurance benefits reached a record **¥11.18tn in FY2024** . Total care costs including co-payments were **¥12.10tn**  (MHLW, Report on the Status of LTC Insurance), and 7.21 million people were certified as needing care or support. [nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUA274VF0X20C26A8000000/)[seiyakuonline](https://www.seiyakuonline.com/zarticles/54) Certification rates rise steeply with age: under 3% at 65–69, about 11.5% at 75–79, 46.9% at 85–89 and 74.2% at 90+ (Mizuho, from MHLW data). [mizuhobank](https://www.mizuhobank.co.jp/corporate/industry/pdf/msif_241.pdf) The government’s 2018 joint projection put **LTC benefits at ¥25.8tn in FY2040** , up from ¥10.7tn in FY2018, in nominal plan-based terms. [jmedj](https://www.jmedj.co.jp/blogs/product/product_9958) Per-capita medical costs also rise steeply with age. MHLW’s FY2023 per-capita medical costs by age band (medical insurance) are ¥937,000 at 80–84, ¥1.087 million at 85–89, ¥1.202 million at 90–94 and ¥1.278 million at 95–99.

**Mobility.**  **435,067**  driver’s licenses were voluntarily surrendered in 2025, **260,915 (60%) of them by people 75+**  (National Police Agency). Meanwhile, 75+ license holders reached **8.35 million** , so the surrender rate per holder is falling. [yahoo +2](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/articles/ed573e482b03e684eae28c3db6635104011773d8) Many late-elderly people keep driving, especially in rural areas, but each cohort that gives up driving creates demand for taxis, on-demand transport, delivery and mobility aids.

**“Shopping refugees.”**  MAFF’s Policy Research Institute estimates that **9.04 million people aged 65+ (25.6%)**  had difficulty accessing food in 2020, defined as living more than 500m from a store and unable to use a car. Of these, 5.66 million were 75+ (31.0% of that age group). The figure rose 9.7% from 2015, [maff](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/press/kanbo/kihyo01/240227.html)[maff](https://www.maff.go.jp/primaff/seika/pickup/pu24/24_03.html) and the problem is now spreading to suburban areas of Greater Tokyo as well as depopulated regions. This is the structural base for meal delivery, mobile supermarkets, convenience-store delivery and online grocery.

**Housing and end of life.**  The housing renovation market was **¥7.35tn in 2024**  and is projected flat at ¥7.3tn in 2025 (Yano Research Institute). [ibnewsnet +2](https://online.ibnewsnet.com/sp/gy250820-02.html) Barrier-free and energy-efficiency remodeling by elderly owners is a stable core of that market. The funeral services market was **¥1.83tn in 2024**  (+8.2%; Yano), excluding temple fees, graves and Buddhist altars. [yano](https://www.yano.co.jp/press-release/show/press_id/3891) Growth comes from the number of deaths, which rises until about 2040, while prices per funeral remain under pressure from small “family funerals.” [nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZRSP695911_X20C25A8000000/)[nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXLRSP519560_Q9A920C1000000/)

**Generational change: dankai vs dankai juniors.**  The 1971–74 cohort turns 65 between 2036 and 2039 and drives the 2043 peak. [ipss](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-zenkoku/j/zenkoku2023/pp2023_gaiyou.pdf) Compared with the dankai, it has more never-married members (hence the 2050 never-married shares among elderly singles above), more single households, flatter lifetime earnings, and pensions that the macro-economic slide will cut in real terms. It also has much higher digital literacy and higher female labor-force participation. My judgment is that 2040–2050 per-household spending is more likely to fall below today’s age profile than above it, outside the employment-driven upside case.

**Inflation and pensions.**  Consumer prices rose 3.7% in 2025 (FIES deflator). [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/2025np/pdf/summary.pdf) Pensions are indexed with the macro-economic slide, so they rise by less than prices. That widened the elderly couple’s deficit from ¥34,058 to ¥42,434 a month in one year. [stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2024.pdf)[stat](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/fies_gaikyo2025.pdf) Real purchasing power of pension-dependent households is eroding, and they protect spending on essentials (food, utilities) at the expense of discretionary goods.

## Part 5 — Market-Size Model, 2020–2050 (author’s estimates)

### 5.1 Method

1. **Households:**  IPSS 2024 household projection, split into four cells: 65–74-headed multi-person, 75+-headed multi-person, 65–74 single, and 75+ single. Multi-person = total − single in each age band.
2. **Annual spending per household (2025 prices, from FIES 2025):**
    - Multi-person 65–74: **¥298,000/month (¥3.576m/yr)** . Multi-person 75+: **¥258,000/month (¥3.096m/yr)** . These are calibrated so the 2025 household-weighted average equals the FIES 65+ two-or-more-person figure of ¥275,635, and they fit between the 65–69 non-working figure (¥296,997) and the 70+ figure (¥264,332).
    - Single 65–74: **¥160,000/month (¥1.92m/yr)** . Single 75+: **¥140,000/month (¥1.68m/yr)** . Calibrated to the non-working single average of ¥148,445. This is conservative because working singles spend more.
3. **Category split:**  elderly couple 2025 shares applied to multi-person households, and elderly single 2025 shares applied to single households.
4. **Public layer:**  Mizuho’s 65+ medical (¥29.0tn) and LTC (¥11.7tn) for FY2023, grown at Mizuho’s real CAGRs (1.7%/yr each) to 2040. [mizuhobank](https://www.mizuhobank.co.jp/corporate/industry/pdf/msif_241.pdf) After 2040 I assume +0.5%/yr for medical and +1.0%/yr for LTC, because the 65+ population shrinks slightly while 95+ keeps growing. To remove double counting, I subtract 10% of medical + LTC.

**Worked example, 2025:**  5.990m × ¥3.576m = ¥21.4tn; 7.641m × ¥3.096m = ¥23.7tn; 2.969m × ¥1.92m = ¥5.7tn; 5.186m × ¥1.68m = ¥8.7tn. **Total ¥59.5tn.**

### 5.5 Composition shift the model understates

The model holds category shares constant within each household type, so it misses the shift within categories as the elderly population ages. The underlying evidence points one way: 75+ households spend less in total, certification rates for care climb past 45% after 85, licenses are surrendered, and smartphone use drops to 19% among the 80+. My judgment is that by 2040–2050, compared with this baseline:

- **Clothing, private car costs (purchase, fuel, insurance) and travel**  will underperform. Clothing could plausibly be 15–25% below baseline.
- **Health, care, meal delivery, housekeeping and monitoring services, taxis and on-demand transport, and renovation**  will outperform. Much of this shows up in “other”, “medical” and the public layer.

### 5.6 Cross-check and reconciliation

- **vs. Mizuho 2012 (¥101.3tn for 2025):**  My 2025 total is ¥97.4tn. The difference comes from my overlap deduction (−¥4.2tn) and from Mizuho 2012 using older population and medical-cost paths. My lifestyle component (¥59.5tn) is higher than Mizuho’s ¥51.1tn because it is in 2025 prices.
- **vs. Mizuho 2025 (¥96.4tn for 2023; ¥114.7tn for 2040 real):**  My 2040 figure is ¥112.9tn. Most of the gap is my overlap deduction. Mizuho’s lifestyle figure for 2040 (¥60.5tn, based on the 2019 National Survey of Family Income, Consumption and Wealth) [mizuhobank](https://www.mizuhobank.co.jp/corporate/industry/pdf/msif_241.pdf) is close to my ¥64.0tn. The remaining gap reflects my 2025 price base and the IPSS 2024 household projection, which has more single households.
- **Key lesson:**  “¥100 trillion” headlines are mostly public money. About **40–45% of the silver market is publicly funded medical and care** , which is limited by fiscal policy, not by consumer demand.

## Part 6 — Implications

**Highest-growth segments (2025–2050)**

1. **Long-term care and home-care services:**  fastest real growth (~1.5%/yr in the public layer). Labor supply, not demand, is the constraint.
2. **Solo-senior services:**  monitoring (見守り), meal delivery (e.g., Watami, Nichirei, Yoshikei), housekeeping, guarantor and end-of-life planning (終活) services, driven by single 65+ households rising to 10.8 million and the large never-married share.
3. **Mobility substitutes and grocery access:**  for 9 million people with difficulty accessing food and about 0.43 million license surrenders a year: on-demand transport, mobile supermarkets, convenience-store and e-commerce delivery.
4. **Health maintenance for 65–74:**  fitness, functional foods and preventive services. This is where the “minus-5-years” upside comes from, and it extends the high-spending years.
5. **Housing adaptation and asset conversion:**  barrier-free renovation within a ¥7.3tn renovation market, moves into serviced senior housing (サ高住) and private nursing homes (有料老人ホーム), and akiya (vacant-house) and inheritance services as deaths peak around 2040.

**Stagnating or declining segments:**  apparel (2% of elderly spending and falling in real terms), private car ownership in metro areas, large durables, and products aimed at mass-market dankai buyers once that cohort is past 85.

**Risks**

- **Care labor shortage:**  Demand for LTC (721万 certified, rising) will outgrow the workforce. [nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUA274VF0X20C26A8000000/)
- **Public-finance constraints:**  Co-payment increases and benefit cuts would move medical and care spending from the public layer into private budgets and squeeze other categories.
- **Regional divergence:**  IPSS projects that 26 prefectures will have fewer 65+ residents in 2050 than in 2020. [ippjapan](https://ippjapan.org/en_ichi/en/archives/494)[e-solar](https://www.e-solar.co.jp/en/column/post1230) Elderly-headed households grow 37.3% in Tokyo but fall 13.9% in Akita. [ipss](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-pjsetai/j/hpjp2024/gaiyo/gaiyo.pdf) Volume growth is concentrated in Greater Tokyo, Aichi, Okinawa and similar areas. [e-solar](https://www.e-solar.co.jp/en/column/post1230) Rural areas need fewer, more efficient service points.
- **Polarization:**  Record aggregate assets coexist with large numbers of elderly households holding no financial assets. [hidekun](https://hidekun.blog/%E5%AE%B6%E8%A8%88%E9%87%91%E8%9E%8D%E8%B3%87%E7%94%A3%E3%81%AF2239%E5%85%86%E5%86%86%E3%81%B8%E3%80%8260%E4%BB%A3%E3%81%A7%E9%80%B2%E3%82%80%E4%BA%8C%E6%A5%B5%E5%8C%96%E3%81%A8%E4%B8%89%E5%89%B2/) Average-based market sizing overstates the mass market and understates both the premium and the low-cost segments.

**Recommendations**

- Size businesses on **households and frailty stages** , not headcount: build separate offers for 65–74 working couples, 75–84 independent singles and 85+ care-dependent people.
- Treat the **public layer as policy risk** . Prefer businesses that can sell private add-ons on top of insured care rather than depend on reimbursement rates.
- Design for **solo, non-digital 80+ customers**  (phone, in-person, family-mediated channels) while using digital channels for the 65–74 group.
- In the 2040s, plan for a **dankai-junior cohort that is more digital but has less money** : value-oriented, subscription-based and online-first offers.

## Caveats

- The model uses FIES spending for **household heads aged 65+** . It includes younger co-resident members and excludes about 3.9 million institutionalized people at the 2045 peak (IPSS) [ipss](https://www.ipss.go.jp/pp-ajsetai/j/HPRJ2024/hprj2024_gaiyo_20240412.pdf) and elderly people living in younger-headed households. Category shares are borrowed from non-working households.
- The 2020 figures are counterfactual (constant 2025 spending). Actual 2020 spending was depressed by COVID-19.
- Projections of medical and care costs after 2040 are my own extrapolations. Mizuho’s projections stop at 2040.
- UN WPP figures were taken from secondary compilations of the dataset.
- The 60% share of financial assets held by 60+ households is a Nikkei estimate, not an official BOJ breakdown.
- Data on specific product sub-markets (adult diapers, hearing aids, senior apparel, home-delivered meals) was not independently verified in this research and is discussed only qualitatively.
- All projections beyond 2030 carry substantial uncertainty in behavior, prices and policy. The demographic inputs are by far the most reliable part of the analysis.
