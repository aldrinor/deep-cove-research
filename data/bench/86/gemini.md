# Manufacturing Technology Options for Hollow Motor Shafts in New Energy Vehicle Electric Drive Units

## 1. Architectural Drivers and Functional Requirements of NEV Hollow Rotor Shafts

The rapid evolution of New Energy Vehicle (NEV) electric drive units (EDUs) toward higher power density, multi-function system integration, and elevated rotational speeds has fundamentally transformed the design and manufacturing requirements of the traction motor rotor shaft [1](https://www.scribd.com/document/639349178/electric-vehicle-transmissions). Serving as the primary mechanical link that supports the laminated rotor core and transmits electromagnetic torque to the reduction gearbox, the motor shaft has transitioned from a simple solid bar into a highly engineered, multi-functional structural and thermal component [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). Currently, the market is segmented into solid shafts, hollow shafts, and semi-hollow (half-hollow) shafts [7](https://mogrinding.com/news/industry-news/new-energy-vehicles-motor-shafts.html). Driven by global vehicle electrification, annual sales of electric vehicle rotor shafts reached approximately 21 million units in 2025 at an average unit value of $22, establishing a $475 million global market projected to reach $1.06 billion by 2032 at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 11.8%, led by Asia Pacific at 14.4% and Latin America at 12.4% [9](https://www.marketresearchreports.com/reports/global-ev-rotor-shaft-market-strategic-research-report).

The first major engineering imperative driving the transition from solid to hollow motor shafts is the optimization of rotational dynamics, mass reduction, and stiffness-to-weight ratio. Whereas early-generation electric vehicles—such as 2010-era passenger models equipped with 16 to 24 kWh batteries and 80 kW traction motors—operated at moderate speeds using solid forged or turned bar shafts, contemporary flat-wire permanent magnet synchronous motors (PMSMs) routinely operate between 20,000 and 30,000 RPM [2](https://www.allpcb.com/allelectrohub/oil-cooling-systems-for-ev-drive-motors). Under pure torsion and bending, the elastic stress distribution in a cylindrical shaft is maximal at the outer surface and approaches zero at the central axis [12](https://dl.asminternational.org/books/chapter-pdf/592495/a0006810.pdf). Removing the central core mass increases the specific bending and torsional section modulus (ZZ) for a given cross-sectional area (AA), governed by the relationship:

Z=π(D4−d4)32DZ = \frac{\pi (D^4 - d^4)}{32 D}

where DD is the outer diameter and dd is the inner bore diameter [13](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/89/2026/). Converting a solid shaft to a hollow geometry reduces component mass by 20% to 40%—and up to 50% in specialized thin-walled profiles, or up to 9 kg per shaft in heavy-duty 140 to 360 kW electric truck drives—while simultaneously increasing the shaft's natural bending frequency (critical speed), lowering rotational inertia, and reducing radial bearing loads [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). However, structural hollowing introduces an electromagnetic trade-off: in rotor topologies where magnetic flux traverses the shaft, a thin-walled hollow shaft or non-magnetic retaining sleeve can increase the effective air-gap length and reduce air-gap flux density, requiring careful co-optimization of the minimum wall thickness underneath the rotor lamination stack [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts).

A second architectural driver is coaxial drivetrain and hybrid module packaging. To minimize axial package length and achieve symmetric vehicle weight distribution, modern coaxial electric rear axles position the electric motor and the planetary differential concentrically [1](https://www.scribd.com/document/639349178/electric-vehicle-transmissions). In this arrangement, the extension output shaft from one side of the differential must pass completely through the center bore of the hollow motor shaft to reach the opposite wheel drive flange [1](https://www.scribd.com/document/639349178/electric-vehicle-transmissions). Similarly, in P2 hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) modules and multi-speed electric transmissions, hollow rotor shafts and forged rotor carriers provide the internal radial volume needed to nest disconnect clutches, triple-clutch packs, or synchronizing shift actuation rods directly inside the electromagnetic rotor envelope [1](https://www.scribd.com/document/639349178/electric-vehicle-transmissions).

The third and most critical driver is internal rotor thermal management. As flat-wire stator windings—which pack 20% to 30% more conductor into the same slot space, increasing slot fill by roughly 20% and peak motor efficiency by approximately 2%—push continuous power densities higher, thermal dissipation inside the rotor becomes a primary operational bottleneck [2](https://www.allpcb.com/allelectrohub/oil-cooling-systems-for-ev-drive-motors). Excessive rotor temperatures induce irreversible thermal demagnetization in Neodymium-Iron-Boron (NdFeB) permanent magnets and degrade bearing grease life [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). Because external housing water jackets cannot efficiently extract heat across the rotor-stator air gap, and full immersion oil cooling introduces severe hydrodynamic churning losses at high RPM, internal cooling via the hollow rotor shaft has become the industry standard [2](https://www.allpcb.com/allelectrohub/oil-cooling-systems-for-ev-drive-motors). In indirect shaft cooling or direct centrifugal spray architectures, transmission oil or specialized low-viscosity water-based lubricants are pumped into the hollow shaft bore at typical flow rates of 5 L/min and 60∘C60^\circ\text{C} inlet temperature [2](https://www.allpcb.com/allelectrohub/oil-cooling-systems-for-ev-drive-motors). Internal dams, weirs, or helical grooves distribute the coolant film along the inner bore wall to absorb rotor core heat via forced convection, while radial cross-drilled orifices utilize centrifugal force to fling cooling oil directly onto the stator end-windings [2](https://www.allpcb.com/allelectrohub/oil-cooling-systems-for-ev-drive-motors). Empirical thermal testing confirms that hollow shaft cooling achieves convective heat transfer coefficients (HTC) of 600 to 1,200 W/m2⋅K1,200\ \text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}, lowers permanent magnet operating temperatures to 32∘C32^\circ\text{C}, and increases high-speed continuous power output by 4% to 16% [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394303489_A_Review_and_Case_of_Study_of_Cooling_Methods_Integrating_Modeling_Simulation_and_Thermal_Analysis_for_a_Model_Based_on_a_Commercial_Electric_Permanent_Magnet_Synchronous_Motor). Replacing conventional transmission oil with advanced water-based lubricants (WBL) inside hollow rotor shafts further lowers steady-state shaft temperatures by 58% and stator winding temperatures by 16% [23](https://lubricants.totalenergies.com/news-press-releases/water-based-lubricant-provides-new-pathway-electric-vehicle-lubrication).

## 2. Comprehensive Evaluation of Current Metal Forming and Joining Technologies

Manufacturing a hollow rotor shaft that satisfies stringent coaxial runout tolerances, high torsional fatigue limits, and complex internal bore geometries requires selecting from seven distinct metalworking and joining routes [5](https://www.hirschvogel.com/en/products/electric-motor/rotor-shaft/). While early designs relied on subtractive drilling, modern series production utilizes near-net-shape plastic deformation and solid-state or high-energy beam welding to preserve continuous grain flow and maximize material yield [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf).

### 2.1 Solid Forging or Bar Stock with Deep-Hole Drilling (Subtractive Baseline)

When automotive OEMs initially introduced internally cooled rotor shafts to prevent permanent magnet demagnetization, the standard manufacturing route involved adding a deep-hole drilling operation to a conventional solid hot-forged or bar-turned shaft [4](https://proleantech.com/motor-shaft-manufacturing/). In this process, the external stepped contour is formed via closed-die hot forging or rough CNC turning from solid round bar stock, after which Gun Drilling or Boring and Trepanning Association (BTA) deep-hole drilling is used to core out the central axial passage [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf).

While this subtractive baseline requires minimal specialized forming capital and easily accommodates thick walls or blind bores (semi-hollow shafts), it presents severe economic and functional drawbacks at high NEV volumes [7](https://mogrinding.com/news/industry-news/new-energy-vehicles-motor-shafts.html). Coring out the interior of a solid shaft wastes 30% to 50% of high-grade alloy steel as machining chips, drastically inflating raw material and chip-handling costs [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf). Furthermore, deep-hole drilling exhibits long cycle times and is susceptible to drill drift (bore eccentricity relative to the outer diameter), which induces rotational mass imbalance and degrades Noise, Vibration, and Harshness (NVH) performance at 20,000+ RPM [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). Critically, straight axial drilling cannot produce "bottle-shaped" internal cavities—where the central bore underneath the rotor lamination stack has a large diameter for maximum weight reduction and cooling volume, while the bearing journal ends have smaller internal diameters—without resorting to complex, expensive internal boring bars [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube).

### 2.2 Rotary Swaging and Recursive Axial Forming (Incremental Cold/Warm Forming)

Rotary swaging, also termed incremental radial forging, has emerged as the premier cold-forming technology for high-volume monolithic hollow EV rotor shafts, championed by equipment and component specialists such as Felss Systems, GFU, and Hirschvogel [17](https://felss.com/en/anwendung/hollow-shaft/). The process begins with a cut-to-length precision seamless or welded steel tube supplied by mills such as Benteler with tight wall-thickness eccentricity and minimal surface decarburization [10](https://www.benteler.com/en/products-services/steel-tube-products/tubes-for-automotive-applications/). Inside the swaging head, two to four radially opposing dies oscillate at high frequency as they rotate around or against the workpiece, incrementally reducing the outer diameter of the tube in small steps within a 5 to 20 second cycle time [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube).

Inserting a precision-contoured internal mandrel into the tube during swaging arrests radial inward metal flow directly against the mandrel surface, unlocking several critical geometric capabilities [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube). As the outer diameter is reduced at the bearing journals and spline ends, the wall thickness locally increases within a very short process time, providing sufficient radial cross-section for subsequent bearing seat grinding and spline teeth while keeping the central rotor-support section thin-walled and lightweight [35](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380864639_Effect_of_Rotary_Swaging_on_Mechanical_Behaviors_of_Axle_Steel_Rod). Synchronized axial feed and radial die kinematics allow the formation of sharp 90∘90^\circ bearing shoulders and large diameter step reductions with IT8 to IT9 geometric repeatability [17](https://felss.com/en/anwendung/hollow-shaft/). Furthermore, in modern integrated transfer lines, rotary swaging is directly coupled with Recursive Axial Forming [37](https://felss.com/en/blog/from-cost-factor-to-competitive-advantage-an-efficient-process-chain-for-rotor-shafts/). In axial forming, an external or internal toothed die is pushed axially over the swaged shaft end with a superimposed oscillatory stroke, cold-forming ready-for-assembly internal or external involute splines and helical gear geometries without chip removal [38](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/sheet-bulk-metal-forming/18555796).

From a metallurgical standpoint, cold rotary swaging induces intense plastic strain hardening [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube). Empirical testing on 42CrMo4 shaft steel demonstrates that cold rotary swaging increases dislocation density (ρ\rho, following the Taylor relationship Rp0.2∝ρR_{p0.2} \propto \sqrt{\rho}), boosting tensile yield strength by 16.6% (from 749 MPa to 873 MPa), ultimate tensile strength by 32.5% (from 977 MPa to 1,295 MPa), and average section Vickers hardness by 8.57% (from 290.41 HV to 315.3 HV, peaking at 323 HV in near-surface and central zones) [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube). Because metal fibers are compressed rather than severed, rotational bending and torsional fatigue life are substantially improved, and separate heat-treatment hardening of formed splines can often be eliminated [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). However, engineers must account for two second-order metallurgical phenomena: first, elongation drops by approximately 31.5% (from 0.127 to 0.087), which increases fatigue crack propagation rates if surface defects are present [35](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380864639_Effect_of_Rotary_Swaging_on_Mechanical_Behaviors_of_Axle_Steel_Rod); second, severe axial elongation and directional dislocation pile-ups induce a Bauschinger effect, where uniaxial compression yield strength increases by only 6.8% (841 to 898 MPa) and exhibits compression softening at strains exceeding 0.25 [41](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/11/2525). Consequently, high-reduction swaging routes frequently incorporate an intermediate or post-forming stress-relief recovery annealing step around 700∘C700^\circ\text{C} to restore ductility while retaining the majority of the yield strength enhancement [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube).

### 2.3 Flow Forming, Shear Spinning, and High-Speed End-Forming (Cold, Warm, and Hot)

Flow forming—along with its related variants of shear spinning, necking-in, splitting, and high-speed end-forming—is an incremental rotational forming process extensively deployed for EV rotor shafts and hybrid clutch carriers by companies such as Leifeld Metal Spinning, Nihon Spindle, and GFU [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf). In a CNC flow-forming center, a thick-walled tubular preform, a forged cup, or a welded ring is clamped axially against a rotating mandrel while three or four symmetrically arranged CNC rollers apply localized compressive force axially and radially [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf). In backward flow forming (such as Leifeld's FFM series), the material flows in the opposite direction of the roller feed; in biconical forward flow forming (such as the 4-roller RSC/RC series), the material elongates in the same direction as the rollers, ensuring symmetrical force distribution, high concentricity, and zero excess length cutoff [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf). While the shear-forming roller moves parallel to the spinning chuck on conical contours, the wall thins in relation to the cone angle (α\alpha) according to the sine law (S1=S0sin⁡αS_1 = S_0 \sin \alpha), producing narrow tolerances and polishable surfaces [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf).

Flow forming offers exceptional versatility across cold, warm, and hot process regimes for NEV rotor shafts [29](https://www.spindle.co.jp/en/technology/electriccar/tech_electriccar.html). Starting from standard pipe stock under cold or warm conditions, rollers thin the central wall section—creating a large internal cooling cavity while work-hardening the shaft wall—and "neck-in" both ends without an internal mandrel to form stepped bearing journals in cycle times of approximately 12 seconds [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf). Dimensional tolerances of ±0.05 mm\pm 0.05\text{ mm} to ±0.1 mm\pm 0.1\text{ mm} are consistently achieved [43](https://ems-metalworking.com/aerospace-engine-component-spinning-machines/). For heavy-duty rotor shafts utilizing high-carbon and high-hardenability quenching steels that resist cold deformation, localized induction heating is integrated directly into the CNC flow-forming enclosure [29](https://www.spindle.co.jp/en/technology/electriccar/tech_electriccar.html). Hot flow forming shapes hard alloy steels with a fraction of the tonnage required by conventional multi-thousand-ton forging presses and can upset an external protruding flange overhanging up to 5 mm on one side beyond the original tube outer diameter in a single chucking setup [29](https://www.spindle.co.jp/en/technology/electriccar/tech_electriccar.html). Additionally, when specialized non-standard tube diameters are prohibitively expensive or unavailable, a flat circular steel disc can be split radially by a roller to create a bifurcated flange and subsequently flow-formed into a seamless hollow stepped shaft, yielding substantial raw material cost savings over seamless tubing [29](https://www.spindle.co.jp/en/technology/electriccar/tech_electriccar.html). Forward flow forming over a toothed mandrel also allows internal gear splines to be formed simultaneously during wall reduction [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf). Because flow forming relies on sequential localized roller contact rather than full-surface die closure, equipment capital investment is modest, mandrels are inexpensive, and geometry modifications for high-mix, low-to-medium volume production are executed primarily through CNC toolpath adjustments, though CNC programs must actively over-form to compensate for material elastic springback [29](https://www.spindle.co.jp/en/technology/electriccar/tech_electriccar.html).

### 2.4 Multi-Stage Cold, Warm, and Hot Hollow Extrusion/Forging

Bulk press forging and extrusion remain foundational pillars in the automotive shaft supply chain, utilized by global tier-1 suppliers including Hirschvogel, Aichi Steel, Chongqing Chuangjing Warm Forging, Thyssenkrupp, and FCC [8](https://www.marklines.com/en/top500/aichi-steel). Cold extrusion is conducted at room temperature on multi-stage transfer presses or mechanical servo presses operating from 630 kN up to 25,000 kN with programmable slide velocity near bottom dead center [45](https://www.scribd.com/document/711653646/PreliminaryProceedings-Ankara2023). In this route, cold backward extrusion punches a solid billet into a thick-walled cup, followed by forward hollow extrusion over a mandrel to create stepped outer and inner diameters [45](https://www.scribd.com/document/711653646/PreliminaryProceedings-Ankara2023). Specialized sheet-bulk and press methods—such as FCC's FAD process—form variable wall thicknesses across different zones in a single press stroke [38](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/sheet-bulk-metal-forming/18555796). While cold extrusion delivers high dimensional precision, superior surface quality, and strain-hardened splines, high tribological tool loads and insufficient die filling limit the achievable length-to-diameter (L/DL/D) ratio of monolithic through-bored shafts [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). Consequently, cold extrusion is ideally suited for semi-hollow (half-hollow) shafts or for producing the symmetric hollow halves of a two-piece welded shaft [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf).

To expand geometric limits, warm forging heats the billet to an intermediate temperature below the recrystallization threshold, lowering flow stress and press tonnage while avoiding heavy oxide scale [45](https://www.scribd.com/document/711653646/PreliminaryProceedings-Ankara2023). Pairing warm extrusion with a subsequent cold calibration stroke achieves near-net-shape tolerances on complex stepped transmission shafts, reducer shafts, and hub-shafts [24](https://www.marklines.com/en/top500/hirschvogel-automotive-group). For heavy-section shafts or integrated P2 hybrid rotor carriers, perforated billets are hot-forged over a mandrel, often combined with cold forging to introduce ready-for-assembly internal lamellae splines [21](https://www.hirschvogel.com/en/products/electric-motor/rotor-carrier/). While hot hollow forging accommodates large diameter jumps, axial material elongation is harder to constrain than radial deformation, necessitating larger axial machining surcharges alongside material losses from oxidation scale (~1%), billet perforation (~2%), and end-block crop waste (~15%) [13](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/89/2026/). A major metallurgical innovation in hot hollow forging is the adoption of Air-Hardening Ductile (AHD) steels (such as grade 1.5132), which achieve high strength via controlled air cooling directly from the forging heat [13](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/89/2026/). This eliminates the entire post-forging quench-and-temper (QT) heat treatment cycle—which normally accounts for ~20% of operating energy costs and induces thermal distortion—bringing the Global Warming Potential (GWP) and cost structure of hot hollow forging in line with casting while cutting component mass by up to 37% [13](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/89/2026/).

### 2.5 Cross-Wedge Rolling (CWR) and Three-/Four-Roll Skew Rolling

To achieve high-speed continuous hot forming of stepped hollow shafts with reduced press tonnage, Cross-Wedge Rolling (CWR) and CNC Skew Rolling have seen intensive industrial development [28](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/19/12/2570). Both processes start from a heated hollow tube billet of constant wall thickness [51](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/14/6/702). In Cross-Wedge Rolling, two, three, or four rolls equipped with wedge-shaped tooling segments rotate to radially compress and axially elongate the hollow billet into stepped diameters, often utilizing an internal mandrel to control inner hole reduction and prevent bore collapse [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube). Multi-wedge synchrostep CWR simultaneously forms multiple shaft steps, offering high productivity and material utilization compared to conventional flash die forging [41](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/11/2525). However, CWR tooling is geometry-specific and expensive, and rolling hollow billets without a mandrel risks ovalization, necking defects, or internal central cracking [51](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/14/6/702).

Three-Roll and Four-Roll CNC Skew Rolling overcomes the tooling inflexibility of CWR by using three or four universal conical rolls inclined at a skew offset angle (−12∘≤β≤+12∘-12^\circ \le \beta \le +12^\circ) with a cone forming angle (0∘≤α≤30∘0^\circ \le \alpha \le 30^\circ) [51](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/14/6/702). As the rolls rotate in the same direction to spin the heated tube blank, a CNC-controlled axial chuck grips one end and pulls the billet forward (for instance, at an axial traction velocity of 20 mm/s) while the rolls execute synchronized radial in-feeds to generate arbitrary stepped profiles [51](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/14/6/702). Because the hollow bore allows metal to flow radially inward rather than forcing circumferential flow, effective plastic strains in hollow skew rolling are 2.5 times lower than in solid shaft rolling, and the damage function inside the walls remains below 0.8 (more than two-fold lower than the critical cracking threshold of 1.777 at 1,100∘C1,100^\circ\text{C}) [53](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7412509/). Mean roll torque drops by 45% to 49% (averaging 8,460 Nm for 3-roll and 4,982 Nm for 4-roll mills), and maximum radial roll loads are 40% lower (351.9 kN to 400.4 kN) [53](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7412509/). Furthermore, maintaining a high axial traction velocity keeps the workpiece temperature field within a 100∘C100^\circ\text{C} band (exceeding 1,070∘C1,070^\circ\text{C}), allowing the rolled hollow shaft to be water-quenched immediately upon exiting the mill to lock in a hardened microstructure [51](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/14/6/702). Nevertheless, while mild reductions can maintain constant wall thickness, heavy diameter reductions (~40% step reduction) cause noticeable wall thinning at the end steps—typically 3 to 4 mm in 3-roll mills and 4 to 6 mm in 4-roll mills due to higher feed per revolution—and leave spiral tool marks on tapered transition zones that must be removed by subsequent turning [51](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/14/6/702).

### 2.6 Multi-Part Composite Forming with Solid-State or High-Energy Beam Welding

When an NEV electric drive requires an extreme internal "bottle-shaped" cavity—such as a large internal diameter under the rotor stack to maximize cooling surface area and reduce mass, paired with small-diameter bearing journals and solid or thick-walled splined ends—single-piece plastic forming hits physical limits of wall reduction and mandrel extraction [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). To solve this, leading manufacturers including Hirschvogel, Linamar, Schaeffler, and Sona Comstar utilize composite (assembled) hollow shafts manufactured by joining two or three separately formed sub-components [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). In two-piece designs, cup-shaped half-shafts are cold-extruded to high internal and external precision and welded together at a central circumferential seam [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). In three-piece modular architectures, a precision seamless steel center tube is joined at both ends to cold- or warm-forged end stubs containing the bearing seats and transmission splines [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). As demonstrated by Hirschvogel's modular rotor shaft platform for electric trucks (140 to 360 kW, in series production since late 2022), this architecture allows OEMs to scale motor power ratings simply by cutting the center tube to different lengths while utilizing identical forged end-pieces across an entire motor family, slashing tooling investment and development lead times while saving up to 9 kg per shaft [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf).

The structural integrity and concentricity of composite shafts depend entirely on the joining method. In Rotary and Inertia Friction Welding (RFW / IFW), one shaft sub-component is rotated at high speed and pressed axially against the stationary mating part under high forge pressure (up to 360 MPa), generating frictional heat that plasticizes the interface into a solid-state bond without melting [65](https://eureka.patsnap.com/report-how-to-inspect-propeller-shaft-welds-for-structural-integrity). RFW eliminates fusion defects, achieves parent-metal fatigue strength, and permits bimetallic joining [65](https://eureka.patsnap.com/report-how-to-inspect-propeller-shaft-welds-for-structural-integrity). However, RFW extrudes a ring-shaped upset flash on both the outer and inner diameters [12](https://dl.asminternational.org/books/chapter-pdf/592495/a0006810.pdf). While external flash is easily turned off, internal flash inside a closed hollow shaft is inaccessible and must either be engineered to function as an internal oil-retaining dam/weir or contained via flash-trap geometries [12](https://dl.asminternational.org/books/chapter-pdf/592495/a0006810.pdf). Moreover, air trapped inside a sealed hollow cavity during RFW can cause internal surface decarburization during subsequent high-temperature heat treatment, and post-weld tempering or stress relief is required to normalize the thermo-mechanically affected zone (TMAZ) [12](https://dl.asminternational.org/books/chapter-pdf/592495/a0006810.pdf). Alternatively, Laser Beam Welding (LBW) and Electron Beam Welding (EBW) utilize deep-penetration keyhole welding to produce a narrow weld seam with a high depth-to-width ratio, minimal heat-affected zone, and virtually zero macroscopic upset flash [65](https://eureka.patsnap.com/report-how-to-inspect-propeller-shaft-welds-for-structural-integrity). While conventional single-pass circumferential beam welding can induce thermal asymmetry—generating radial runout deflections averaging 0.06 mm0.06\text{ mm} with a 3σ3\sigma spread of 0.14 mm0.14\text{ mm}—modern assembly lines press-fit the finish-machined shaft stub into a precision cylindrical boss hole and execute simultaneous multi-point weld sequences, holding post-weld runout within micron-level tolerances and drastically reducing downstream dynamic balancing corrections [70](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1134358B1/en?authuser=1).

### 2.7 Niche and Hybrid Forming Processes (Tube Hydroforming and Powder Metallurgy)

Two additional forming techniques occupy specialized niches in NEV rotor assemblies. Tube hydroforming, particularly when combined with axial mechanical upsetting, uses internal fluid pressure to expand a tubular blank outward against a closed die cavity to create bulged central sections with minimally thinned walls, or to hydroform a positive-locking interference connection between a hollow shaft and a rotor flange or hub [71](https://www.globalspec.com/industrial-directory/hydroforming_camshaft). While highly effective for lightweight hollow camshafts and expanded cooling chambers, hydroforming alone cannot produce thick-walled bearing shoulders or high-torque gear splines, restricting its use to center-tube preforms or shaft-hub joining operations [71](https://www.globalspec.com/industrial-directory/hydroforming_camshaft). Similarly, sintered powder metal (PM) components and additive manufacturing are referenced in patent literature for creating intricate internal cooling inserts, annular fluid distributors, or rotor magnet retention covers, and PM forging is utilized in select gear applications [75](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20210394600). However, due to residual micro-porosity and lower impact and torsional fatigue resistance compared to wrought steel, standalone sintered shafts are unsuitable for primary high-speed NEV traction drives.

### 3.1 Suitable Materials and Metallurgical Compatibility

Material selection for NEV hollow motor shafts is governed by the method used to transmit torque to the reduction gearbox—splined coupling versus an integrated drive pinion gear—and the plastic formability limits of the chosen manufacturing route [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). Quench-and-temper (QT) and induction-hardenable alloy steels, specifically 42CrMo4 (AISI 4140), C45 (AISI 1045), and S355, represent the primary materials for hollow rotor shafts featuring internal or external splines [13](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/89/2026/). In cold rotary swaging and cold flow forming, 42CrMo4 and modified carbon steels respond exceptionally well to cold work hardening, gaining over 30% in ultimate tensile strength and improving torsional fatigue resistance [10](https://www.benteler.com/en/products-services/steel-tube-products/tubes-for-automotive-applications/). However, because high pre-hardened strength increases cold forming loads and reduces ductility, tube feedstock for cold swaging and cold extrusion must be supplied in a spheroidized annealed or normalized condition with a homogeneous microstructure and minimal surface decarburization [10](https://www.benteler.com/en/products-services/steel-tube-products/tubes-for-automotive-applications/). For multi-part welded shafts, 42CrMo4 requires pre-heating or immediate post-weld stress relief and tempering during Rotary Friction Welding or Laser Beam Welding to prevent brittle martensitic cracking in the weld zone [65](https://eureka.patsnap.com/report-how-to-inspect-propeller-shaft-welds-for-structural-integrity).

When the rotor shaft incorporates a direct-machined or rolled helical pinion gear that meshes directly with the first-stage reduction gear, low-carbon case-carburizing alloy steels such as 20MnCr5 (or 16MnCr5) are mandatory to achieve a wear-resistant martensitic case over a tough, impact-resistant core [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). In the unhardened state, 20MnCr5 exhibits excellent cold ductility, making it highly compatible with cold rotary swaging, recursive axial spline forming, and multi-stage cold extrusion [17](https://felss.com/en/anwendung/hollow-shaft/). Conversely, high-carbon quenching grades that resist cold deformation require warm extrusion, hot flow forming with induction heating, or hot skew rolling [29](https://www.spindle.co.jp/en/technology/electriccar/tech_electriccar.html).

For hot hollow forging and hot rolling routes, Air-Hardening Ductile (AHD) micro-alloyed forging steels (such as grade 1.5132) transform into a high-strength microstructure during controlled air cooling directly off the forging line [13](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/89/2026/). Unlike standard 42CrMo4 QT steels—which require rough machining prior to austenitizing, quenching, and tempering, risking severe thermal warpage on thin-walled hollow sections—AHD steels eliminate heat-treatment distortion and furnace emissions while achieving superior strength-to-weight ratios [13](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/89/2026/). Finally, in composite friction- or laser-welded shafts, dissimilar materials can be joined to optimize local properties, such as welding a case-hardened 20MnCr5 gear stub to a lower-cost C45 or S355 structural center tube, or integrating stainless steel, titanium (TA19/TC4), or aluminum (6061A/7075) elements in specialized aerospace and high-speed rotor designs [15](https://welleshaft.com/en/manufacturer/shafts-hollow-shafts-misumi-and-custom-specifications-welleshaft/).

### 3.2 Cost-Effectiveness, Material Utilization, and Capital Economics

The unit cost of an NEV hollow rotor shaft—averaging approximately $22 globally across passenger and commercial segments—is predominantly dictated by raw material weight, which accounts for roughly 50% of total manufacturing cost in forged shafts, alongside energy and operating expenses (~40%, split evenly between forming and heat treatment) and tooling or post-processing costs (~10%) [9](https://www.marketresearchreports.com/reports/global-ev-rotor-shaft-market-strategic-research-report). A fundamental economic trade-off exists between feedstock cost and forming conversion cost. Precision seamless or drawn-over-mandrel (DOM) welded steel tubing commands a higher per-kilogram price than hot-rolled solid bar stock [10](https://www.benteler.com/en/products-services/steel-tube-products/tubes-for-automotive-applications/). However, rotary swaging and flow forming achieve material utilization rates of 85% to 95%, completely eliminating the deep-hole drilling bottleneck and saving 20% to 40% in raw steel mass per shaft [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf). When axial forming is integrated to cold-form the drive splines, chip-making machining is restricted solely to functional bearing journals and seal seats, making rotary swaging combined with axial forming the lowest total-cost route at high annual volumes exceeding 100,000 units per year [17](https://felss.com/en/anwendung/hollow-shaft/).

To bypass the price premium of seamless precision tubes, Nihon Spindle and Leifeld utilize disc splitting or welded ring preforms in flow forming, achieving pipe-equivalent hollow geometries from economical flat plate stock [29](https://www.spindle.co.jp/en/technology/electriccar/tech_electriccar.html). Similarly, Three-Roll CNC Skew Rolling uses thick-walled hot-rolled hollow billets and universal conical rolls; because a single roll set can form diverse shaft geometries via CNC programming, skew rolling eliminates expensive closed forging dies and provides exceptional cost-effectiveness for medium-volume or large commercial vehicle shafts [51](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/14/6/702). For electric commercial vehicles and multi-platform passenger EVs where production volumes per variant are lower, three-piece composite shafts joined by friction or laser welding deliver unmatched platform economics [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). By standardizing the cold- or warm-forged end stubs across an entire powertrain portfolio and varying only the length of the inexpensive center tube, manufacturers amortize forging die costs over millions of common end-pieces while cutting development lead times [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf).

A critical manufacturing dependency emerges across the downstream processing chain regarding the interaction between heat-treatment distortion, datum correction, and high-speed grinding. Because hollow shafts have thin walls (often 2.5 to 5 mm), thermal stresses during case carburizing or induction hardening inevitably cause bore ovality and axial bending distortion [30](https://gfu-forming.de/en/high-speed-end-former-machine/). To prevent this distortion from translating into severe rotor imbalance at 25,000 RPM, best-in-class process chains execute post-heat-treatment center-hole grinding, which corrects bore datum distortion to within 3 μm3\ \mu\text{m} [31](https://www.deepholedrillinghub.com/post/316). Using these precision-ground center chamfers as the rotational datum, the shaft is transferred to high-speed Cubic Boron Nitride (CBN) cylindrical grinding centers operating at linear wheel speeds up to 140 m/s140\text{ m/s} [7](https://mogrinding.com/news/industry-news/new-energy-vehicles-motor-shafts.html). Straight-wheel or angled CBN grinding finishes both the bearing journal diameters and the 90∘90^\circ thrust end-faces in a single clamping without B-axis rotation or wheel changes, slashing base changeover times by 70% while guaranteeing micron-level coaxiality between the rotor core seat and the bearing journals [7](https://mogrinding.com/news/industry-news/new-energy-vehicles-motor-shafts.html). Furthermore, when the shaft integrates a drive gear, hard gear skiving or dressable CBN gear grinding is paired with nitriding—which creates a 0.01 mm0.01\text{ mm} hard skin with negligible thermal distortion—to eliminate high-pitch tonal gear whine in the vehicle cabin [1](https://www.scribd.com/document/639349178/electric-vehicle-transmissions).

## 4. Optimal Manufacturing Route Selection for NEV Electric Drive Applications

No single forming technology is universally optimal across all NEV segments; rather, the ideal manufacturing process chain is determined by the specific intersection of internal cooling cavity geometry, rotational speed, torque load, and annual production volume. Based on the comparative engineering and economic evidence, four primary manufacturing routes represent the most suitable industry solutions.

### 4.1 Route A: Precision Tube + Cold Rotary Swaging (over Mandrel) + Recursive Axial Forming

For high-volume passenger battery electric vehicle (BEV) and plug-in hybrid electric vehicle (PHEV) primary traction motors (80 to 250 kW, 16,000 to 22,000 RPM) featuring stepped through-bores for indirect or centrifugal radial-spray oil cooling and internal or external splines, the optimal manufacturing route combines cold rotary swaging over a stepped internal mandrel with recursive axial forming [2](https://www.allpcb.com/allelectrohub/oil-cooling-systems-for-ev-drive-motors). Starting from a precision seamless or DOM welded steel tube (42CrMo4, modified C45, or 20MnCr5), multi-stage cold rotary swaging forms the stepped outer and inner diameters alongside sharp 90∘90^\circ bearing shoulders in a 5 to 20 second cycle [10](https://www.benteler.com/en/products-services/steel-tube-products/tubes-for-automotive-applications/). Where severe diameter reductions are required, an intermediate stress-relief anneal at approximately 700∘C700^\circ\text{C} restores elongation prior to recursive axial forming, which cold-forms net-shape internal or external splines [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube). Subsequent processing is minimized to skim-turning the bearing seats, cross-drilling and deburring radial cooling orifices, localized induction hardening, center-hole grinding to within 3 μm3\ \mu\text{m}, and single-setup 140 m/s140\text{ m/s} CBN cylindrical and face grinding [7](https://mogrinding.com/news/industry-news/new-energy-vehicles-motor-shafts.html). This route delivers the lowest total unit cost at scale (>100,000 units/year) by achieving ~90% material utilization, eliminating deep-hole drilling, and increasing ultimate tensile strength by up to 32.5% through work hardening, which frequently renders separate spline hardening unnecessary [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf).

### 4.2 Route B: Two-Piece Cold Extrusion (or Tube + Forged Stubs) + Laser Beam Welding

For ultra-high-speed performance EV motors (22,000 to 30,000 RPM) and compact coaxial drives requiring an extreme "bottle-shaped" internal cooling cavity—characterized by a large central inner diameter under the rotor stack tapering to small-bore bearing journals—with integrated internal oil weirs and dams, a multi-part composite route is optimal [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). Monolithic swaging or flow forming cannot economically extract a large-diameter mandrel through narrow end-journal bores or machine complex internal oil baffles inside a nearly closed cavity [27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384463607_Effect_of_rotary_swaging_and_annealing_on_mechanical_properties_and_microstructure_evolution_of_aeroengine_high-temperature_alloy_steel_tube). By multi-stage cold extruding two hollow half-shafts (or cold-forging two end stubs to mate with a precision center tube), internal oil grooves, centrifugal dams, and press-fit alignment bosses can be pre-formed or finish-machined with unobstructed access prior to assembly [3](https://www.thevoltbridge.com/learn/rotor-shafts). While Rotary Friction Welding is widely used, deep-penetration Laser Beam Welding (or multi-spot Electron Beam Welding) paired with a precision press-fit boss joint is superior for 25,000+ RPM shafts because it avoids internal upset flash—which otherwise traps furnace gases during heat treatment or disturbs oil film flow—and minimizes thermal runout distortion prior to center-hole datum grinding, CBN finish grinding, and 100% ultrasonic weld inspection [12](https://dl.asminternational.org/books/chapter-pdf/592495/a0006810.pdf).

### 4.3 Route C: Warm/Hot CNC Flow Forming (or Disc Splitting + Flow Forming)

For medium-volume or high-mix EV motor shafts (10,000 to 80,000 units/year), thin-walled coaxial pass-through shafts where the differential output shaft runs concentrically through the motor bore, and P2 hybrid integrated rotor carriers, CNC flow forming provides the highest technical and economic agility [1](https://www.scribd.com/document/639349178/electric-vehicle-transmissions). Starting from a thick-walled tube preform, a forged cup, or a radially split flat steel disc, induction-assisted hot or warm 4-roller flow forming and necking-in produce ultra-thin, highly concentric central walls, internal gear teeth, and protruding external flanges up to 5 mm in roughly 12 seconds [11](https://www.spindle.co.jp/img/technology/pdf/TechnicalReport_No58.pdf). Following CNC journal skim-turning, nitriding or localized induction hardening, and CBN grinding, the finished shaft exhibits exceptional rotational accuracy and enhanced fatigue life [1](https://www.scribd.com/document/639349178/electric-vehicle-transmissions). Flow forming requires substantially lower capital investment and cheaper tooling than multi-stage cold extrusion or heavy forging presses, while CNC roller path control allows rapid changeovers across multiple shaft lengths and wall thicknesses without dedicated die sets [29](https://www.spindle.co.jp/en/technology/electriccar/tech_electriccar.html).

### 4.4 Route D: Three-Part Modular Welded Assembly or Three-Roll CNC Skew Rolling

For heavy-duty commercial NEV and electric truck traction motors (140 to 360+ kW) requiring large shaft lengths, thick walls, and high torque capacity across fragmented vehicle production volumes, two specialized routes stand out [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). The first is Hirschvogel's three-part modular composite architecture, which joins standardized warm- or cold-forged splined end stubs to a variable-length seamless steel center tube via Rotary Friction Welding, followed by external flash turning, post-weld stress relief, induction hardening, and CBN grinding [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). This modular strategy cuts component weight by up to 9 kg and allows OEMs to cover diverse power ratings simply by adjusting the center tube cutoff length [6](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). Where a monolithic heavy-duty hollow shaft is preferred, Three-Roll CNC Skew Rolling of a heated hollow billet (using 42CrMo4 or Air-Hardening Ductile steel 1.5132) with an axial traction chuck forms long stepped hollow shafts with 40% lower radial load than solid rolling, zero dedicated die cost, and direct inline water quenching or air hardening [13](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/89/2026/).
