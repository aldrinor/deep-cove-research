# Saint Seiya: An Armor-Centered Guide to Classes, Factions, Power, Techniques, Arcs, and Fates

Saint Seiya is best understood as a conditional armor system rather than a single power ladder. Athena’s 88 Cloths establish the main rank structure—48 Bronze, 24 Silver, 12 Gold, and four of unknown material—but Cosmo, the Seventh Sense, technique matchups, armor condition, environment, and continuity often matter more than rank alone. Gold Saints are the stable non-divine benchmark; Bronze Saints can temporarily reach Gold-level performance, Scales and God Robes occupy disputed Gold-adjacent territory, Surplices range from mass-produced guard armor to Judge-level equipment, and God Cloths or Kamui represent divine escalation. [1]

The classic manga remains the primary continuity, while the 1986 TV anime adds the anime-original Asgard arc and several character changes. Next Dimension is the direct sequel and prequel; Lost Canvas, Saintia Shō, Soul of Gold, Episode G, the films, and Omega are treated separately where their armor systems or fates diverge.

This report first defines the armor classes and the power language used for comparison. It then follows the principal Bronze, Silver, Gold, Marina, God Warrior, and Specter characters through their techniques, arcs, and fates, before placing Black Cloths, Steel Cloths, Saintia, divine armor, and alternate continuities in context. The final comparison focuses on technique design, environmental modifiers, arc function, survival, sacrifice, redemption, and the limits of cross-continuity ranking.

## Armor taxonomy, continuity, and comparative power language

### Corpus and continuity boundaries

**The primary story.** Masami Kurumada’s original manga ran from 1985 to 1990 and covers Sanctuary, Poseidon, and Hades. Its concluding Hades material is the baseline for Cloth evolution, the Wailing Wall sacrifice, the Elysion battle, and the classic outcome in which Hades’s sword mortally wounds Seiya. The 1986–1989 TV anime contains 114 episodes: Sanctuary in episodes 1–73, the anime-original Asgard arc in episodes 74–99, and Poseidon in episodes 100–114. [2][3]

**The Hades adaptation.** The manga’s final arc was later adapted as 31 OVAs: 13 Sanctuary episodes, 12 Inferno episodes, and six Elysion episodes, released between 2002 and 2008. The Hades OVAs adapt manga events, although their staging and some character presentations belong to the later anime continuity. [4]

**The sequel line.** Next Dimension is Kurumada’s direct sequel and prequel, combining the aftermath of Hades with the eighteenth-century Holy War. It changes the practical meaning of Seiya’s ending: the classic conclusion presents a mortal wound, while Next Dimension treats Hades’s sword as a curse producing a coma that Athena seeks to cure. It also introduces new Gold Saints, the Silver-to-Gold transformation of Ophiuchus Odysseus, and Suikyō’s Silver-to-Specter transition. [5]

**Parallel and alternate works.** Lost Canvas is a separate eighteenth-century prequel with different Gold Saints, Specters, techniques, and deaths. Saintia Shō is a parallel story in the original world centered on Athena’s personal maidens. Soul of Gold is an anime interquel tied to the Asgard anime and Hades aftermath. Episode G has debated canonicity and much more extreme scaling language; its feats should not be used to rewrite the classic baseline. The first four films form a loosely connected Movieverse, while Omega is a later anime sequel with its own developments. [6][7]

### Cloths: constellation, Cosmo, and rank

**The armor is not autonomous.** A Cloth is a constellation-linked object that becomes functional armor when its wearer burns Cosmo. It does not simply supply power to an otherwise ordinary fighter; without Cosmo, the Cloth is effectively inert. This is why Seiya’s first use of the Pegasus Cloth does not instantly solve his problems, and why a Bronze Saint who awakens the Seventh Sense can temporarily perform like a Gold Saint. [1]

**Rank is a prior, not a verdict.** Bronze, Silver, and Gold communicate expected training, equipment, and battlefield role. They do not function as immutable numerical tiers. Seiya reaches Aldebaran-level power after awakening the Seventh Sense; Shiryu defeats Krishna by reaching the same threshold; Ikki momentarily breaks through Shaka’s control; and Algol’s Silver-level Medusa Shield creates a lethal matchup against stronger opponents until Shiryu finds the correct counter.

**Repair changes the baseline.** After Sanctuary, the surviving Gold Saints repair Seiya, Shiryu, Hyoga, Shun, and Ikki’s Bronze Cloths with Gold-Saint blood. In Poseidon, the five Cloths can glow gold when their wearers burn enough Cosmo. In Hades, Athena’s blood raises the Cloths to their highest temporary state, allowing the God Cloth transformation. The repair does not permanently turn the protagonists into Gold Saints; it creates a bridge between rank, Cosmo, and transformation.

### Scales: sea authority rather than a second Gold rank

**The Scale system is functional.** Poseidon assigns seven Marine Generals to seven ocean pillars. Ordinary Mariners are described as roughly Bronze-equivalent, while the General Scales are forged from Orichalcum and are repeatedly compared with Gold Cloths in hardness or defense. Baian believes his Scale has Gold-level resistance, yet Seiya’s Comet Fist breaks it. Krishna’s Scale survives ordinary attacks but falls to Seventh-Sense Excalibur. These scenes make Scale durability an important combat variable without proving that every General has the same overall power as every Gold Saint.

**The faction is conditional.** One supplied hierarchy attributes the Generals’ relatively moderate showing to Poseidon’s incomplete awakening and their lack of experience, while projecting stronger performance after full divine awakening. That projection is best treated as a conditional ceiling. The actual Poseidon arc demonstrates specialized General abilities, pillar-defense objectives, and several Gold-adjacent matchups; it does not establish a universal Scale ranking.

### Surplices: Demon Stars, army structure, and divine exceptions

**Surplices are an army system.** Hades commands 108 Demon Stars and 108 unique Specter armors, alongside mass-produced Skeleton Surplices. A Taizen or Hypermyth explanation describes Demon Stars as souls that seek suitable hosts and instantly raise their Cosmo, but the original manga is less explicit about the possession mechanics. The useful distinction is between the broad army and the upper echelon that can actually fight Gold Saints. [8][9]

**The Judges are administrators and elite combatants.** Rhadamanthys, Minos, and Aiacos command the other 105 Specters. A source-supported distribution of 35 Specters per Judge remains an inference rather than an official structure, but their superiority over ordinary Specters is clear. Hypnos and Thanatos wear Surplices but are gods, not ordinary Specters; their divine Cosmo places them in a separate class.

### God Robes: Norse myth and manipulated loyalty

**God Robes belong to the anime.** The 1986 Asgard arc is anime-original and takes place between Sanctuary and Poseidon. Its God Warriors protect Odin’s representative, Hilda of Polaris, and wear Robes based on Norse creatures, heroes, and myths. The seven Odin Sapphires correspond to the stars of Ursa Major and are needed to summon Odin’s Robe and Balmung. [10]

**The armor’s power has two readings.** One source describes all seven official God Robes as broadly equal, able to withstand Seventh-Sense attacks and therefore far above Silver Cloth resistance, while noting that Seiya’s attack through Siegfried’s chest leaves open the possibility that they are below Gold Cloths. Another overview describes the anime God Warriors’ standard strength as Gold-comparable, with some pushing beyond it. The most defensible judgment is therefore a range: Robe resistance above Silver and possibly below Gold, with individual wearers sometimes performing at Gold-comparable levels.

### A consistent power vocabulary

This article uses five recurring criteria:

- formal rank and armor class;
- Cosmo development, especially the Seventh and Eighth Senses;
- direct feats against named opponents;
- technique counters and environmental modifiers;
- continuity and armor condition.

“High Bronze” means a Bronze Saint with strong but sub-Gold evidence. “Gold-comparable” means a stated or demonstrated matchup against a Gold benchmark. “Exceptional” means the character exceeds the ordinary expectation of the class through a specific feat or technique. “Divine” refers to God Cloth, Kamui, a god’s own Cosmo, or a deity’s armor. These are qualitative bands, not numerical scores.

A character may occupy more than one band because the series repeatedly changes the conditions. Rhadamanthys is especially dominant inside Hades Castle because the castle suppresses non-Specters; Kanon performs differently in a Scale and Gemini Cloth; Soul of Gold’s God Warriors benefit from Yggdrasil while the Gold Saints are drained; and Bronze Saints become divine only temporarily in Elysion.

## Bronze Saints and Cloths

**Bronze Saints carry the franchise’s central contradiction.** They are Athena’s lowest and most numerous rank, normally assigned supporting roles, yet the five protagonists repeatedly surpass the limits associated with Bronze Cloths. Their growth comes from Cosmo, self-sacrifice, inherited techniques, repaired Cloths, and the ability to turn an opponent’s mechanism against itself. [11]

### Principal Bronze Saints

### Pegasus Seiya

**Power.** Seiya begins as a conventional Bronze Saint but reaches Seventh-Sense parity with Taurus Aldebaran. His later Cosmo, repaired Cloth, Eighth Sense, and God Cloth place him at the top of the mortal-to-divine progression. His level is not permanently “Gold” because the Aldebaran parity depends on Seventh-Sense activation, while the God Cloth is temporary and tied to Athena’s blood.

**Techniques.** The Pegasus Meteor Fist is a rapid barrage; the Pegasus Comet Fist concentrates that output into a single attack; and the Pegasus Rolling Crush converts Seiya’s physical momentum into a close-range throw. These techniques show the characteristic Seiya pattern: repeated impact, adaptation, and increasingly concentrated Cosmo. [12]

**Arcs.** He is the principal fighter in Galaxian Wars, Black Saints, the Silver-Saint campaign, the Twelve Houses, anime Asgard, Poseidon, and Hades. His key rank breakthrough occurs against Aldebaran; his decisive divine escalation occurs against Thanatos, whose armor he shatters after awakening the Pegasus God Cloth.

**Fate.** In the classic manga and Hades anime, Hades’s sword pierces Seiya and leaves him mortally wounded as Athena and the other Bronze Saints defeat Hades. Next Dimension reframes the wound as a curse-induced coma, with Athena traveling through time to save him. The two endings are continuity-dependent rather than contradictory versions of one settled status. [13]

### Dragon Shiryu

**Power.** Shiryu is the highest-end defensive Bronze Saint before the God Cloth stage. His Dragon Cloth is associated with the strongest fist and shield, but his defining strength is the willingness to discard armor and burn Cosmo directly. He reaches Gold-comparable performance against Deathmask and Krishna, and his Excalibur feat is one of the clearest instances of a Bronze Saint defeating a superior armor through technique inheritance.

**Techniques.** Rozan Rising Dragon is his signature ascending impact technique. Shura passes Excalibur into Shiryu’s right arm, giving him a cutting attack capable of destroying Krishna’s golden lance and Chrysaor Scale. The Libra Cloth’s authorized weapons provide a separate structural function: after defeating Krishna, Shiryu uses the Libra sword to destroy the Indian Ocean pillar. [14]

**Arcs.** Shiryu is central to the Galaxian Wars and Black Saints arcs, the Algol and Deathmask fights in Sanctuary, the Krishna pillar defense in Poseidon, the Hades assault, and Next Dimension’s eighteenth-century war. His repeated loss of sight is not decorative suffering; it demonstrates that he can replace ordinary perception with Cosmo and still fight.

**Fate.** Shiryu survives the classic Hades conflict. In Next Dimension he is alive with Chunli, has adopted a child named Shoryu, travels toward the eighteenth-century war with Hyoga, and is proclaimed the future Libra Gold Saint. This is a clearer sequel endpoint than the original Hades conclusion alone provides.

### Cygnus Hyoga

**Power.** Hyoga is the most technically specialized Bronze Saint. His ice Cosmo reaches absolute-zero conditions by stopping subatomic motion, giving him an elemental ceiling beyond what his formal rank suggests. He reaches Seventh Sense in the Camus duel, though his struggle with Milo shows that awakening the threshold does not make every Gold matchup equal.

**Techniques.** Diamond Dust is his basic freezing attack; Aurora Thunder Attack adds greater area and force; Aurora Execution approaches absolute zero and represents the complete expression of Camus’s teaching. Hyoga’s defensive value is equally important: his Cloth prevents Hydra Ichi’s poison from penetrating, and his ice can immobilize opponents before the decisive attack.

**Arcs.** The manga makes Aquarius Camus his direct master. The anime inserts the Crystal Saint as Hyoga’s immediate teacher and makes Camus the Crystal Saint’s master, creating a major adaptation divergence. Hyoga’s other defining arcs are the Sanctuary confrontation with Camus, the Poseidon battle with his former companion Isaac, Hades, and Next Dimension. [15]

**Fate.** Hyoga survives the classic Hades conflict and travels with Shiryu in Next Dimension. He is proclaimed the future Aquarius Gold Saint. The anime and manga disagree about his teacher, but not about his central arc: emotional attachment becomes the source of, rather than an obstacle to, his strongest Cosmo.

### Andromeda Shun

**Power.** Shun is a control-oriented Bronze Saint whose potential is repeatedly greater than his willingness to use lethal force. His chain can attack, defend, sense danger, change form, and counter several attacks at once. He reaches Elysion and briefly wears the Virgo Cloth, establishing a route from Bronze control specialist to future Gold successor.

**Techniques.** The Andromeda Chain creates defensive and offensive forms. Nebula Stream immobilizes or restrains an opponent, while Nebula Storm converts that restraint into overwhelming output. Against Scylla Io, the chain counters every one of Io’s beast forms, making the victory a tactical demonstration rather than a simple power contest.

**Arcs.** Shun appears in the Silver-Saint campaign, Sanctuary, Poseidon, Hades, and Elysion. Hades’s necklace marks him as the god’s vessel; Ikki frees him after Hades takes control of his body. The manga names Cepheus Daidalos as his master, while the anime uses Cepheus Albiore.

**Fate.** Shun survives, helps defeat Hades, and is proclaimed the future Virgo Gold Saint in Next Dimension. His fate is defined by possession without moral surrender: Hades can use his body, but Shun’s identity remains recoverable.

### Phoenix Ikki

**Power.** Ikki is the exceptional Bronze Saint from the start. His debut as the leader of the Black Saints places him above ordinary Bronze fighters, and later feats against Shaka, Kasa, and Aiacos show that his peak Cosmo can challenge Gold-comparable or Judge-level opponents. His apparent death is also less final than for most characters because the Phoenix Cloth regenerates.

**Techniques.** Phoenix Wings Rise is a direct destructive attack. Phoenix Illusion Demon Fist attacks the mind and exposes buried trauma, making it particularly effective against opponents who depend on emotional manipulation. The Phoenix Cloth’s regeneration gives Ikki a unique endurance advantage, but it does not make him invulnerable: Shaka overwhelms him until the loss of his senses awakens the Seventh Sense.

**Arcs.** Ikki leads the Black Saints before his redemption, fights Shaka in Sanctuary, confronts Mime and Bud in anime Asgard, defeats Kasa in Poseidon, and defeats Aiacos in Hades. Against Kasa, Ikki’s emotional control is the counter; against Aiacos, adaptation prevents the same attack from defeating him twice. [16]

**Fate.** Ikki becomes a recurring ally rather than a permanent antagonist. He appears in Hades and Next Dimension, protects Shun in the eighteenth-century conflict, and is proclaimed the future Leo Gold Saint. His central outcome is redemption through continued action rather than a single apology.

**The secondary group’s function.** Jabu, Geki, Ichi, Nachi, and Ban lose the narrative contest for centrality even when they remain alive. ### Silver exceptions and adaptation differences

**Algol shows why rank is not enough.** The Medusa Shield petrifies targets who look at it, even through eyelids or a blindfold. Shiryu’s self-blinding removes the condition required for the Shield, allowing him to defeat Algol rapidly and restore the petrified Bronze Saints. Algol is not Gold-level in general; his weapon produces a near-one-hit control threat that forces a specific sacrifice. [18]

**Daidalos and Albiore must remain separate.** The manga’s Daidalos is an average Silver Saint and Shun’s master. The anime’s Albiore fills the same narrative position but is strong enough to stalemate Scorpio Milo before Aphrodite intervenes. Both are killed by Aphrodite, but their power portrayals must not be merged. This is one of the clearest examples of adaptation-specific scaling changing a character without changing the arc function. [18]

**Marin and Shaina are recurring bridges.** Marin trains Seiya and remains active in Sanctuary and later continuities. Shaina moves from enemy to ally and repeatedly protects Seiya. Their exact placement above ordinary Silver is continuity-dependent: anime filler and spin-offs suggest lower-Gold relevance, while some Star Hill interpretations are contradicted by Shion’s own later performance. The safe judgment is high Silver with occasional lower-Gold evidence, not a fixed rank promotion.

### Gold Saints as benchmark and exception

**Mu is the technical counterexample to brute hierarchy.** Mu’s defining contribution is not simply destructive power. He repairs Cloths, teleports allies, uses psychokinesis, and combines Crystal Wall with Stardust Revolution. In Hades he defeats Deathmask and Aphrodite, but the combined force of Saga, Shura, and Camus overwhelms him. His power is therefore high Gold in a support-and-defense profile rather than a simple destructive ranking. The official Soul of Gold profile emphasizes his Cloth-repair tradition and psychic fighting style. [20]

**Aldebaran is a durability test.** Great Horn is a close-range defensive counterattack that punishes an opponent for entering his space. Seiya’s ability to cut Aldebaran’s horn after awakening the Seventh Sense marks the first decisive Bronze-to-Gold breakthrough. In Hades, Niobe’s lethal attack kills Aldebaran, but the residual Great Horn destroys Niobe afterward; the scene makes Aldebaran’s fate a sacrifice rather than a clean defeat.

**Saga and Shaka define the upper Gold range.** Saga’s split personality makes the manga’s central villain an internal Sanctuary crisis; the anime instead constructs a good former Pope and an evil brother named Ares, so the villain architecture differs by continuity. Saga’s Another Dimension removes targets from the battlefield, while Galaxian Explosion supplies overwhelming direct force. His good personality returns, and Athena’s scepter kills him after he asks forgiveness. [21]

Shaka’s strength is control rather than simple impact. He closes his eyes to raise Cosmo, then opens them to seal an opponent’s senses through Tenbu Hōrin. He defeats early Seiya, Shun, and Shiryu with ease and forces Ikki to awaken the Seventh Sense through prolonged psychological and physical torment. In Hades, Shaka reaches the Eighth Sense and sacrifices himself to communicate the truth of the Underworld.

**Shura turns rank into inheritance.** Shura’s Excalibur cuts armor, flesh, and dimensional barriers. Shiryu cannot defeat him through ordinary techniques, so Shura’s final act is to transfer the technique to the Dragon Saint. Shura later returns as a Hades Specter, but his apparent betrayal is part of the plan to reach Hades. His fate is therefore death, resurrection, and a second death after the Wailing Wall.

**Camus and Aphrodite show specialized Gold combat.** Camus’s absolute-zero pursuit makes him the teacher whose defeat represents Hyoga’s emotional and technical maturation. Aphrodite’s poison roses create an area-denial battle in which Shun wins only by burning his Cosmo through a broken Cloth. Deathmask’s soul attack sends Shiryu to Yomotsu Hirasaka, but Shunrei’s prayers and Shiryu’s awakened Seventh Sense reverse the fight.

**Dohko, Milo, and Aiolos embody authority.** Dohko is one of only two survivors of the previous Holy War and possesses both Rozan Hundred Dragons and the Athena-authorized Libra weapons. Milo’s Scarlet Needle attacks fifteen nerve points and turns endurance into a countdown; he uses the technique on Kanon before accepting the repentant former Marina General. Aiolos’s defining feat is not a named attack but the protection of baby Athena, for which he dies branded a traitor and is later vindicated.

**Shion and Odysseus expand the class.** Shion’s restoration of the Bronze Cloths with Athena’s blood bridges Gold authority and Bronze evolution. In the manga he turns to dust; the anime gives him time for a final reunion with Dohko. Odysseus, by contrast, is a Next Dimension-specific transformation from Silver Saint to legendary thirteenth Gold Saint. His healing ability and resurrection show that the Silver-to-Gold boundary is narratively permeable, but the sequel’s final status leaves his endpoint open.

### Kanon’s cross-category role

**Kanon is armor-dependent.** As Sea Dragon, Kanon is the strategist who manipulates Poseidon’s awakening and leads the Marina Generals. As Gemini, he becomes an Athena-aligned Gold Saint. The Cloths reference explicitly states that Kanon can fight Rhadamanthys on equal terms while wearing the Gemini Cloth but is no match after returning it to Saga. [1]

Kanon is thus simultaneously a rank exception, a redemption story, and proof that armor state changes the practical reading of power.

### The seven Generals and their Scales

**Baian tests the Scale claim.** Baian believes his Scale is as resistant as a Gold Cloth and uses Rising Billows and God Breath to control the battlefield. Seiya studies his defense, reaches the correct Cosmo level, and destroys the Scale with the Comet Fist. The result does not prove that all Scales are weak; it shows that durability remains conditional on the attacker’s Cosmo and the armor’s specific design.

**Io is defeated by coverage.** Io’s Scylla Scale produces seven distinct attack forms, each modeled on a different creature. Shun’s Andromeda Chain can counter every form, turning a multi-form arsenal into a puzzle with a specialized answer. Io’s willingness to sacrifice himself for the South Pacific pillar makes the battle a loyalty test as much as a power test.

**Krishna is the clearest General-level technical fight.** Krishna’s golden lance penetrates Shiryu’s Dragon Shield and Cloth. Shiryu’s first Excalibur attempt fails because his Cosmo is insufficient; only after removing the Dragon Cloth and reaching the Seventh Sense can he cut the lance, the Scale, and Krishna’s chakra points. Krishna’s Maha Roshini still blinds Shiryu, so the victory is high-end but costly. [14]

**Kasa attacks identity rather than armor.** Kasa does not need to outmuscle Hyoga or Shun. He takes the form of people they love and uses their emotional weaknesses to create an opening. Ikki’s emotional discipline makes him the natural counter, and Phoenix illusion turns Kasa’s own manipulation against him. This is why Kasa’s power is difficult to place on a linear scale: he is dangerous when the opponent’s psychology supplies the opening.

**Isaac turns a General fight into a debt.** Isaac was Hyoga’s fellow Siberian apprentice and loses an eye while saving him. Their fight is not a simple test of Aurora Borealis against Diamond Dust; Hyoga must overcome guilt before he can burn his Cosmo fully. Isaac dies after recognizing Hyoga’s growth and revealing the crisis in Poseidon’s sanctuary.

**Sorrento demonstrates conditional superiority.** Sorrento’s music can reduce an opponent’s effective output and attack the senses, giving him a strong matchup against fighters who cannot interrupt the melody. His confrontation with Siegfried in Asgard is not a universal proof that Sorrento exceeds all Gold Saints; the decisive advantage is the effect on hearing. Once he learns that Kanon’s manipulation caused the conflict, Sorrento abandons him and helps Julian’s redemption.

### Thor, Hagen, and Fenrir: direct elemental and tragic combat

**Thor’s strength is physical and moral.** Thor’s twin hammers repeatedly overwhelm Shun, Hyoga, and Seiya, while Titanic Hercules badly injures the Pegasus Saint. Yet Thor notices that Hilda’s Cosmo has changed and fights because he believes defeating the Saints might restore the woman who once saved him. Seiya’s final attack kills him, but Thor’s last judgment is that the Saints can rescue Hilda.

**Hagen combines incompatible elements.** He trains in both frozen mountains and volcanic caves, allowing him to combine ice with fire. Universe Freezing makes him a natural opponent for Hyoga, but jealousy over Hyoga’s relationship with Freya turns a tactical fight into a personal collapse. Hyoga’s Aurora Execution defeats him.

**Fenrir turns environment into weapon and fate.** Fenrir’s wolves amplify his attacks and embody his rejection of human society. Shiryu’s Rising Dragon triggers an avalanche that kills Fenrir and the wolves. The result is not a clean comparison of Dragon Cloth and God Robe; the environment becomes the final weapon.

### Alberich: the God Robe as deception

**Alberich is the arc’s tactical exception.** He defeats Marin, Seiya, and Hyoga by using Amethyst Sealed, Nature Unity, acid, and the environment rather than relying on a direct power contest. Shiryu survives Nature Unity by becoming one with nature and receives Dohko’s help to defeat him. Alberich’s God Robe therefore illustrates the importance of technique mechanics: a seemingly less imposing warrior can defeat multiple higher-profile opponents through preparation and deception.

### Syd, Bud, and Mime: family and psychological combat

**The Zeta twins are a divided armor system.** Syd is the official wearer, while Bud is the abandoned twin who acts as a shadow. Their combined attack defeats Aldebaran in the anime’s opening Asgard material, but Syd’s victory is aided by Bud. When they fight separately, Shun’s Nebula Storm defeats Syd, while Bud cannot deliver the killing blow. Their fate is mutual death, but the emotional result is reconciliation.

**Mime’s harp changes the normal battle logic.** Mime’s strings restrain Shun and create illusions; Stringer Requiem provides a lethal finish. Ikki must fight without relying on the Phoenix Cloth after Mime removes it. The Phoenix Illusion Demon Fist reveals the truth about Mime’s adoptive father and transforms their battle from revenge into recognition. Mime dies wishing that he and Ikki could be friends in another life.

### Siegfried, Hilda, and the Odin Robe

**Siegfried is the armor-resistance climax.** Shiryu’s attacks initially leave Siegfried apparently unharmed. His vulnerability resembles the Dragon Saint’s: the heart’s location can be exposed at a precise moment. Siegfried uses Odin Sword and Dragon Bravest Blizzard and survives several Bronze attacks before Seiya strikes the heart. He dies after learning that Sorento’s revelation about Hilda is true.

**Hilda is an unwilling antagonist.** Her combat power comes primarily from divine authority and the Nibelung Ring rather than a standard Saint technique. She is manipulated by Poseidon, watches her warriors die, and is restored when Seiya uses Odin’s Robe and Balmung to destroy the Ring. Her fate is survival combined with political responsibility: she must atone for actions performed while controlled.

**Odin’s Robe is a resolution armor.** Once the seven Sapphires are assembled, Seiya summons Odin’s Robe and Balmung, cuts the Nibelung Ring, and restores Hilda. The Robe then removes itself from Seiya. Unlike the Bronze God Cloths, it is not a permanent evolution of Seiya’s Pegasus Cloth; it is an external divine instrument tied to Asgard’s ritual.

## Specters and Surplices: the Hades arc

**The Specters are not one power level.** Hades’s army contains Skeleton guards, ordinary Terrestrial and Celestial Star Specters, upper-echelon fighters, and three Judges. The army’s 108 count refers to the Demon-Star structure, while different reference systems count named, identified, and drawn Specters differently. A practical comparison must therefore use rank, direct combat, and the exact Surplice or environment involved.

### Rhadamanthys: power and environmental limits

**Rhadamanthys is the clearest conditional Judge.** In the manga, his victories over Mu, Aiolia, and Milo at Hades Castle occur while the castle suppresses non-Specters. Athena-blood-restored Bronze Cloths negate that advantage. The anime omits the suppression limitation and presents Rhadamanthys as more overwhelmingly dominant, so manga and anime must be separated. [27]

Greatest Caution gathers Rhadamanthys’s Cosmo and releases it through both palms as a powerful wind-type attack. Seiya’s Meteor Fist cracks the Wyvern Surplice, but Kanon is the only opponent who conclusively ends the fight. Kanon removes the Gemini Cloth, accepts a suicide attack, and destroys both himself and Rhadamanthys.

### Minos and Aiacos

**Minos meets a divine armor boundary.** Cosmic Marionette controls opponents through invisible threads, making Minos a control specialist rather than merely a stronger brawler. ### Orphée and the cost of loyalty

**Orphée is the Silver exception who belongs in Hades.** His music is powerful enough to disable opponents and he descends into the Underworld to recover Eurydice. Pharaoh manipulates the arrangement that keeps Eurydice trapped, but Orphée ultimately chooses Athena’s cause. He defeats Pharaoh, helps Seiya and Shun, and dies after confronting Rhadamanthys. His fate combines extraordinary Silver power with the franchise’s recurring pattern of love, failed rescue, and sacrificial return to duty.

### Black Saints and Black Cloths

**Black Cloths are counterfeit Athena armor.** Black Saints are renegades who use Saint power for personal gain or were stripped of proper status. On Death Queen Island they produce copies of Athena’s Cloths, but the lack of Athena’s permission and knowledge makes the copies inferior and vulnerable to Bronze-Saint attacks. The Black Four mirror Pegasus, Dragon, Andromeda, and Cygnus, turning the protagonists’ identities into hostile doubles. [29]

### God Cloths, Divine Cloths, and Kamui

**God Cloths are temporary evolutions.** In Elysion, Athena’s blood bathes the damaged Bronze Cloths, and Eighth-Sense Cosmo raises them into God Cloths. The five Bronze Saints can then damage or defeat Thanatos, Hypnos, and Hades’s true body. The transformation is a final-arc escalation, not a permanent universal promotion from Bronze to divine rank.

**Athena’s armor is distinct.** Athena’s Cloth is both the additional God Cloth outside the 88 and the only known Kamui shown in battle. It allows Athena to use divine Cosmo at full potential and withstand Hades’s attacks without visible armor damage. Poseidon uses a Scale and Hades uses a Surplice in their known incarnations, so the presence of divine wearers does not automatically convert every divine armor into Kamui. [31]

**The categories overlap in terminology but not function.** A Bronze God Cloth is a temporary human transformation enabled by Athena’s blood and extreme Cosmo. A Kamui is the original sacred armor of an Olympian god. Athena’s armor occupies both conceptual spaces because she is a goddess whose Cloth is also the divine protection of a Saint system.

**Athena is the decisive divine wearer.** Her Victory Staff, shield, divine blood, and Kamui drive the outcomes against Poseidon and Hades. She seals Poseidon, expels Hades from Shun, and destroys Hades’s true body. Her “power level” is therefore divine Cosmo rather than Saint rank; her key arc function is to convert the Saints’ sacrifices into victory.

### Next Dimension

**Next Dimension preserves the rank system while destabilizing identities.** Its eighteenth-century conflict includes Pegasus Tenma, Aries Shion, Libra Dohko, Crateris Suikyō, and Ophiuchus Odysseus. Suikyō moves from Athena’s Silver rank to Hades’s Garuda Specter role, while Odysseus moves from Silver healing specialist to the legendary thirteenth Gold Saint. These transformations prove that armor categories can describe vocation and allegiance without permanently defining the person.

**The sequel also revises fates.** Seiya’s mortal wound becomes a curse-induced coma; Shiryu, Hyoga, Shun, and Ikki receive prospective Gold successions; and the previous Holy War becomes an active continuity rather than a fixed backstory. Because Next Dimension is the direct Kurumada sequel, its fate statements should be separated from Lost Canvas even when the same names or reincarnational roles recur.

### Films, Episode G, Omega, and other armor-bearing groups

**The films use separate armor logic.** The Ghost Saints, Corona Saints, Fallen Angels, and film God Warriors belong to the Movieverse or film-specific narratives. They can add Silver-derived, solar, fallen, or Odin-related armor concepts, but their victories and deaths should not be imported into the classic manga. The first four films are loosely connected to the anime rather than a single fully integrated timeline. [7]

**Episode G has exceptional scaling language.** Fan discussions often cite Episode G’s Photon Burst and galaxy-destruction language to produce extreme Gold-Saint rankings. Those readings are continuity-dependent and disputed; Episode G’s techniques and fates should be described from its own text rather than used as a numerical measurement of classic Saga, Aiolia, or Shaka.

**Omega extends rather than stabilizes the system.** Omega promotes Seiya to Sagittarius Gold Saint and introduces new generations of Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Steel wearers. Its most relevant contribution here is the explanation that Steel Cloth performance causes burden and recoil, not a universal revision of the original Steel Saints’ power.

### Technique styles

**Impact is the easiest style to compare.** Seiya’s Meteor and Comet Fists, Thor’s hammers, Aldebaran’s Great Horn, and Aiolia’s lightning attacks can be compared through direct feats. Even here, Seiya’s victory over Aldebaran depends on Seventh Sense, while Thor’s defeat depends partly on his willingness to recognize Hilda’s corruption.

**Control often beats rank.** Algol’s Medusa Shield, Shaka’s Tenbu Hōrin, Kasa’s identity deception, Sorrento’s music, and Hypnos’s sleep do not need to overpower an opponent conventionally. They change the conditions under which power can be expressed. The counter is therefore more important than a general tier: Shiryu blinds himself, Ikki controls his emotions, Athena’s blood reverses Hypnos’s effect, and Shun’s chain counters Io’s forms.

**Inheritance is a major mechanism.** Shura’s Excalibur passes to Shiryu; Dohko’s Libra weapons destroy pillars; Athena’s blood repairs and transforms Cloths; the Phoenix Cloth renews itself; and Odin’s Sapphires summon the Odin Robe. Power in Saint Seiya is often transmitted through armor, blood, memory, or sacrifice rather than earned only by individual training.

### Power comparison across factions

**Gold remains the stable benchmark.** The Gold Saints are the only ordinary faction with a clearly defined collective threshold: Seventh Sense, twelve zodiac Cloths, and Athena Exclamation. Soul of Gold explicitly places Gold Saints above ordinary Mariners and most God Warriors, while matching them against Judges. That statement is useful as a baseline, but Yggdrasil reverses the local conditions and forces the Gold Saints into temporary God Cloths.

**Bronze can exceed Gold in moments.** Seiya equals Aldebaran after awakening the Seventh Sense; Shiryu’s Excalibur destroys Krishna’s Scale; Ikki defeats Aiacos; Shun defeats Io’s complete Scylla arsenal; and all five defeat divine opponents in God Cloths. These are not proof that Bronze rank is secretly stronger than Gold rank. They show that rank supplies a starting point, while Cosmo breakthroughs and technique counters determine the result.

**Scales occupy a conditional middle-high range.** Baian’s Scale is comparable to Gold armor until Seiya breaks it. Krishna’s Scale and lance overwhelm Shiryu until Excalibur and Seventh Sense appear. Kanon is a special case because Gemini Cloth performance places him alongside the strongest Gold benchmarks. The Generals should therefore be described as Gold-adjacent specialists rather than a uniform tier above or below Gold.

**God Robes have the widest ambiguity.** The Robes survive Seventh-Sense attacks and therefore clearly exceed ordinary Silver resistance. The source comparison allows them to be below Gold, while character-level statements and anime performances place several God Warriors around Gold level. The original Asgard arc’s home-field conditions, emotional instability, and Odin Sapphires further complicate comparison. Siegfried, Thor, Mime, and Alberich should be judged through their individual feats rather than one Robe-wide ranking.

**Specters divide sharply.** Ordinary Specters are not automatically Gold-level. Myu, Niobe, Raimi, Pharaoh, and the Judges demonstrate different kinds of danger, but only the Judges and upper echelon regularly approach Gold-Saint combat. Hades Castle makes Rhadamanthys appear stronger against weakened Gold Saints; Elysium’s dimensional pressure eliminates Minos; and divine Surplice wearers exceed the ordinary army. The category is therefore a hierarchy, not a tier.

### Arc function and armor meaning

| Arc | Armor-centered function | Principal contrast | Fate pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sanctuary | Tests rank, legitimacy, and institutional authority | Bronze protagonists challenge Gold hierarchy | Gold death, moral restoration, Bronze promotion |
| Poseidon | Converts combat into pillar destruction | Scales and specialized Generals defend a divine structure | General deaths, Kanon redemption, Poseidon sealing |
| Asgard | Tests mythic loyalty and manipulated duty | God Robes represent noble ideals distorted by the Ring | Tragic warrior deaths and Hilda’s restoration |
| Hades | Turns armor into a bridge between life and death | Surplices, Eighth Sense, and divine armor define thresholds | Resurrection, sacrifice, sealing, divine escalation |

**Sanctuary is an institutional revolution.** Bronze Saints begin as outsiders seeking the Sagittarius Gold Cloth and end by proving that Athena’s authority does not belong exclusively to the Gold hierarchy. The Gold Saints’ deaths or moral reversals establish that rank without justice is empty. Saga dies after his good nature returns; Shura passes on Excalibur; Shaka sacrifices himself after awakening the Eighth Sense; and the Bronze Saints inherit the right to continue.

**Poseidon is a structural and redemptive arc.** Each Scale is attached to a pillar, so the victory is measurable in destroyed supports rather than defeated bodies alone. Kanon moves from manipulation to repentance, Sorrento abandons a false cause, Julian is separated from Poseidon, and Athena seals the god. The armor’s oceanic symbolism is therefore administrative: the Generals are guardians of a system that collapses when its divine purpose is exposed as manipulation.

**Asgard is about loyalty under false orders.** God Robes are not intrinsically evil. Thor, Hagen, Fenrir, Mime, Syd, Bud, and Siegfried are driven by gratitude, jealousy, trauma, family, or devotion to Hilda. Poseidon’s ring turns noble protection into war. Most God Warriors die once they recognize the truth, making the arc a tragedy of manipulated virtue rather than a simple good-versus-evil contest.

**Hades is the sacrifice arc.** Gold Saints return as apparent traitors, Athena enters the Underworld, Shaka reaches the Eighth Sense, the Gold Saints destroy the Wailing Wall, and the Bronze Saints acquire temporary God Cloths. Death becomes a transport mechanism: the Saints must master the Underworld rather than merely survive earthly combat. The final victory belongs to a chain of sacrifices involving Gold Saints, Athena, the five Bronze Saints, and the divine blood that transforms their armor.

| Divine defeat or sealing | Poseidon, Hades, Hypnos, Thanatos | Gods require collective Cosmo, divine blood, or Athena’s intervention |
| Fate remains continuity-bound | many secondary Saints, Specters, film characters, and spin-off figures | A roster appearance is not the same as a universal final endpoint |

**Redemption crosses armor classes.** Ikki begins as a Black-Saint leader; Kanon begins as Sea Dragon; Saga becomes the false Pope; Sorrento serves Poseidon; Hilda is controlled by the Nibelung Ring; Shura appears as a Hades Specter; and several Asgard warriors fight Athena while believing they defend their land. In each case, the armor initially expresses allegiance but does not permanently determine moral identity.

**Death is more common among elite protectors.** Gold Saints, God Warriors, Marine Generals, and upper Specters frequently die because their armor represents a cause that must be completed through sacrifice. Bronze Saints survive more often because the narrative uses them as the next generation, though Seiya’s classic wound and Lost Canvas Tenma’s death show that protagonist survival is continuity-dependent.

**Resurrection is never free.** Hades’s Gold Saints return only to complete a hidden mission and die again. Soul of Gold’s Gold Saints are resurrected by Odin and return to death after defeating Loki. God Cloths consume time and sometimes life force. The series treats restoration as a temporary loan, not a cancellation of mortality.

### Final judgment

The most defensible cross-category conclusion is not “Gold Saints always beat God Warriors” or “Marina Generals are stronger than Gold Saints.” Gold Cloths are the stable non-divine benchmark, but Seventh-Sense Bronze feats, conditional Scales, God Robes, Judge-level Surplices, divine environments, and specialized techniques prevent a universal linear ranking.

The technique comparison is more reliable than the ranking comparison. Impact fighters test durability; elemental fighters change the environment; psychic, illusion, and musical fighters alter perception; dimensional and soul techniques bypass ordinary armor; and God Cloths or Kamui change the category of the battle itself. A lower-ranked fighter wins when the technique creates the right condition, the opponent’s armor is compromised, or Cosmo reaches a threshold that rank alone does not predict.

The armor classes also explain the franchise’s story structure. Cloths represent chosen identity and growth; Scales represent a divine administration built around pillars; Surplices represent death and predetermined stars; God Robes represent mythic loyalty and tragic manipulation; Black Cloths represent rejected or counterfeit sainthood; Steel Cloths represent technological imitation; God Cloths represent temporary human transcendence; and Kamui represent the armor of gods.

The decisive judgments would change if a primary continuity statement established a universal ceiling for Scales or God Robes, or if a later sequel definitively revised a character’s endpoint. Until then, the strongest conclusion is conditional: rank sets the prior, Cosmo determines the ceiling, technique determines the matchup, armor state determines the available defense, environment changes the result, and continuity determines the fate.

The class counts used here are inventory counts rather than power measurements: 88 Athena Cloths comprise 48 Bronze, 24 Silver, 12 Gold, and four unknown-material Cloths; the Silver overview separately identifies 16 active figures; Poseidon has seven General Scales and pillars; Hades has 108 Demon-Star Surplices plus Skeleton armor; and the original anime Asgard roster has seven official God Robes with Bud’s replica treated separately. “Gold-comparable,” “high Silver,” and similar phrases are qualitative judgments based on rank, Cosmo, direct matchups, technique counters, armor conditions, and continuity. Entries marked as bounded profiles use a sourced combat role or last-established outcome as a proxy rather than inventing a missing named move, numerical score, or unshown ending.
