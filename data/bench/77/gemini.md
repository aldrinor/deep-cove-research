# Epistemic Motivation and False Belief: The Role of Need for Cognitive Closure in Misinformation Acceptance

## Theoretical Foundations of Need for Cognitive Closure

Within the study of human judgment and belief formation, misinformation acceptance is rarely the product of passive information deficits alone [1](https://www.emc-lab.org/uploads/1/1/3/6/113627673/ecker.2022.nrp_preprint.pdf). Rather, false beliefs emerge from the dynamic interplay between cognitive architecture and epistemic motivation—the underlying psychological drives that govern how individuals seek, evaluate, and solidify knowledge [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Grounded in Lay Epistemic Theory, the **Need for Cognitive Closure (NFCC)**  represents one of the most consequential motivational determinants of how individuals process ambiguous or misleading information [5](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/the-need-for-closure-and-conservati). Formulated by Arie W. Kruglanski and Donna M. Webster, NFCC is defined as an individual's epistemic desire for a firm, definite answer to a question—any firm answer—as opposed to enduring confusion, uncertainty, or ambiguity [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure).

Crucially, NFCC does not equate to a deficit in intelligence or an inability to engage in complex thought [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Instead, it reflects a motivational orientation toward epistemic security that operates both as a stable dispositional trait and as a malleable situational state [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). At the trait level, individuals vary chronically in their baseline tolerance for uncertainty, which psychometricians assess using the original 42-item Need for Closure Scale, the 14-item Revised scale, or the widely adopted 15-item abridged scale [9](https://osf.io/download/5p4nw/). Across these instruments, dispositional NFCC is conceptualized as a multifaceted construct comprising five distinct subscales [13](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/an-attempt-to-clarify-the-link-between-cognitive-style-and-political-ideology-a-nonwestern-replication-and-extension/8C19751D82F71F5306C6096BAD487BB0):

| NFCC Subscale Dimension | Core Epistemic Preference | Primary Vulnerability Mechanism in Misinformation Processing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Preference for Order** | Desire for structure, organization, and neatness in physical and cognitive environments [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). | Fosters reliance on simplified, binary narratives and structured stereotypes over nuanced or conflicting data [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). |
| **Preference for Predictability** | Craving for secure, trans-situationally consistent knowledge that enables reliable future expectations [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). | Drives endorsement of overarching teleological or conspiratorial explanations during novel, volatile crises [15](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/5h73q9209). |
| **Decisiveness** | Urgency to reach immediate conclusions and execute rapid decisions to terminate deliberation [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). | Triggers premature seizing on initial headlines or unverified claims before fact-checking can occur [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). |
| **Discomfort with Ambiguity** | Acute aversive affective reaction when confronted with missing, equivocal, or contradictory evidence [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). | Lowers resistance to interpersonal memory conformity and false post-event suggestions when internal memory is uncertain [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full). |
| **Closed-Mindedness** | Unwillingness to have existing knowledge structures challenged by alternative opinions or inconsistent evidence [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). | Insulates established false beliefs against factual retractions, fueling belief perseverance and continued influence [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). |

Beyond chronic dispositional variance, NFCC can be acutely elevated by environmental and macro-social pressures [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Situational factors that tax cognitive energetics—such as strict decision deadlines, time pressure, chronic environmental noise, mental fatigue, or circadian troughs—systematically heighten the desire for immediate closure [4](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/motivation-and-cognition). At a macro-societal scale, periods of rapid socio-political upheaval, economic downturns, mass migration, and public health emergencies (such as the COVID-19 pandemic) induce widespread epistemic anxiety and personal significance loss [5](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/the-need-for-closure-and-conservati). Under such conditions, the motivational system shifts toward rapid uncertainty reduction, creating fertile psychological ground for simplistic falsehoods, populist rhetoric, and extremist ideologies that promise immediate certainty [5](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/the-need-for-closure-and-conservati).

## The Dual-Phase Mechanics of Misinformation Acceptance: Seizing and Freezing

To understand how NFCC governs misinformation acceptance, one must move beyond the simplistic assumption that high NFCC universally increases gullibility [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Empirical evidence demonstrates that NFCC operates as a **chronological and contextual amplifier**  governed by two sequential cognitive-motivational tendencies: the **urgency tendency**  and the **permanence tendency**  [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure).

### The Dual-Phase Epistemic Pathway of **Seizing and Freezing**  in Misinformation Processing

[figure]

Need for Cognitive Closure operates through two sequential mechanisms. During the pre-closure urgency phase, individuals confronting an epistemic vacuum rapidly seize on the first salient explanation—accepting misinformation if it arrives first or comes from an ascribed epistemic authority. Once a mental model is formed, the permanence phase locks the belief in place, causing the cognitive system to suppress competing memories and reject subsequent retractions that fail to provide a complete alternative explanation.

<!-- /figure 1 -->

When an individual high in NFCC lacks a settled conclusion regarding an event, the **urgency tendency**  dominates cognitive processing [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Experiencing ambiguity as aversive, the individual is motivated to terminate the information search as rapidly as possible [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). This urgency produces a pronounced **seizing**  effect, wherein the cognitive system latches onto early, salient, and easily accessible cues capable of providing immediate structure [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). In modern digital environments characterized by information overload, emotive false headlines and simplified rumors are frequently engineered to be highly salient and cognitively effortless to process [1](https://www.emc-lab.org/uploads/1/1/3/6/113627673/ecker.2022.nrp_preprint.pdf). Consequently, if misinformation is the first plausible explanation encountered during an uncertain event, high-NFCC individuals are disproportionately likely to seize upon it to alleviate epistemic discomfort [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure).

Immediately following the formation of an initial judgment, the **permanence tendency**  engages to protect the newly acquired closure [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). This mechanism induces **freezing** , whereby the individual crystallizes the initial interpretation and becomes closed-minded toward subsequent, competing information [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Because returning to a state of uncertainty is psychologically costly, the permanence tendency acts as a motivated gatekeeper that shields the established belief from disconfirming evidence [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure).

This two-stage architecture yields a critical second-order implication: **the effect of NFCC on misinformation acceptance is fundamentally governed by primacy and order of exposure**  [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). If an individual high in NFCC first encounters an accurate, authoritative explanation that resolves uncertainty, the permanence tendency causes them to freeze on the truth, rendering them *more resistant*  to subsequent misinformation than individuals low in NFCC [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). Conversely, if misinformation arrives first during an informational void—or if a preexisting stereotype or ideological schema is already frozen in memory—the exact same motivational machinery locks the falsehood in place and aggressively repels factual corrections [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure).

## The Epistemic Vacuum Paradox: Official Explanations and Conspiracy Endorsement

The conditional nature of NFCC is most vividly demonstrated in the domain of conspiracy theories—a virulent genre of misinformation that attributes major socio-political events to the covert schemes of malevolent groups [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). Psychological frameworks developed by Karen M. Douglas and colleagues classify the drivers of conspiracy belief into three primary clusters: epistemic motives (the desire to understand one's environment and attain certainty), existential motives (the need for safety and control), and social motives (the need to maintain positive personal and in-group esteem, often linked to collective narcissism) [17](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317401748_The_Psychology_of_Conspiracy_Theories). Within the epistemic cluster, NFCC plays a paradoxical role: conspiracy theories attract high-NFCC individuals because they offer totalizing, internally consistent explanations for complex or threatening realities, yet they do so only under specific environmental boundary conditions [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf).

Seminal experimental work by Marta Marchlewska, Aleksandra Cichocka, and Małgorzata Kossowska (2018) resolved longstanding contradictions regarding when NFCC promotes versus inhibits conspiratorial misinformation acceptance [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). Across two experiments involving Polish internet users, the researchers demonstrated that high-NFCC individuals do not spontaneously generate complex conspiratorial narratives; rather, their acceptance of such misinformation depends jointly on **situational salience**  (whether a conspiratorial explanation is explicitly provided in the environment) and **event certainty**  (whether a clear, official non-conspiratorial explanation already exists) [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf).

In Study 1 (N=245N = 245), participants read an online news report regarding European Union plans to relocate Syrian and Eritrean refugees to Poland—an ambiguous socio-political issue [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). While NFCC correlated positively with conspiratorial beliefs overall (r(243)=.22,p=.001r(243) = .22, p = .001), moderation analysis revealed that NFCC strongly predicted endorsement of an anti-Polish EU conspiracy when mock reader comments below the article explicitly primed the conspiratorial explanation (B=0.97,SE=0.25,p<.001B = 0.97, SE = 0.25, p < .001), whereas the relationship dropped to statistical non-significance in the control condition (B=0.29,SE=0.23,p=.20B = 0.29, SE = 0.23, p = .20) [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). Because fabricating an elaborate conspiracy de novo requires substantial cognitive effort, individuals high in NFCC only adopt conspiratorial misinformation when it is situationally accessible [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf).

Study 2 (N=455N = 455) introduced a critical second moderator: whether the target event possessed a verified official explanation [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). Participants were randomly assigned to read about either the unexplained disappearance of Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 (high uncertainty; official cause unknown) or the Germanwings Flight 9525 crash (high certainty; official cause confirmed and well-known), paired with either conspiratorial comments blaming covert Russian sabotage or neutral control comments [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). When the cause of the catastrophe was unknown and conspiratorial claims were salient, NFCC positively predicted conspiracy endorsement (B=0.39,SE=0.22,p=.08B = 0.39, SE = 0.22, p = .08), as participants seized on the covert plot to fill the explanatory void [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). However, when the official cause of the crash was already established and certain, the relationship inverted: NFCC **negatively and significantly**  predicted conspiracy belief (B=−0.44,SE=0.21,p=.04B = -0.44, SE = 0.21, p = .04) [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf).

This **Certainty Reversal Effect**  exposes a profound structural vulnerability in public communication during emerging crises [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). When a novel crisis erupts—such as the initial outbreak of COVID-19 or a sudden geopolitical shock—scientific and institutional authorities must gather data before issuing definitive conclusions, creating a temporary **epistemic vacuum**  [15](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/5h73q9209). Because responsible institutions communicate with probabilistic caution while purveyors of disinformation supply immediate, absolute explanations, individuals high in NFCC (particularly across the predictability, ambiguity-avoidance, and closed-mindedness dimensions) rapidly seize onto false narratives to alleviate anxiety [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf). Once they have frozen onto those early falsehoods, subsequent official explanations arriving weeks or months later are treated not as welcome answers, but as disruptive threats to hard-won cognitive closure [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure).

## Heuristic Reliance: Epistemic Authority Over Argument Quality

When evaluating novel claims, individuals can pursue an elaborate, analytical route by scrutinizing the substantive quality of the evidence, or a peripheral, heuristic route by deferring to source characteristics [3](https://www.scribd.com/document/558114090/The-psychological-drivers-of-misinformation-belief-and-its-resistance-to-correction). Lay Epistemic Theory posits that knowledge formation is heavily guided by **epistemic authority (EA)** —the degree to which an individual ascribes legitimate expertise and trustworthiness to a given source, whether an institution, a political leader, a peer group, or oneself [7](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/e0ff0074-f77e-9bac-e053-1705fe0af019/Euro%20J%20Social%20Psych%20-%202021%20-%20Pica%20-%20The%20epistemic%20bases%20of%20changes%20of%20opinion%20and%20choices.pdf).

Empirical investigations by Gennaro Pica, Maxim Milyavsky, Antonio Pierro, and Arie W. Kruglanski (2021) demonstrated how NFCC fundamentally alters the balance between source authority and argument quality during opinion formation and belief revision [7](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/e0ff0074-f77e-9bac-e053-1705fe0af019/Euro%20J%20Social%20Psych%20-%202021%20-%20Pica%20-%20The%20epistemic%20bases%20of%20changes%20of%20opinion%20and%20choices.pdf). When individuals perceive themselves as lacking self-ascribed epistemic authority in a complex domain, high NFCC intensifies their reliance on external sources [7](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/e0ff0074-f77e-9bac-e053-1705fe0af019/Euro%20J%20Social%20Psych%20-%202021%20-%20Pica%20-%20The%20epistemic%20bases%20of%20changes%20of%20opinion%20and%20choices.pdf). Crucially, across two experimental studies, Pica et al. (2021) established that individuals with high (versus low) NFCC are disproportionately influenced by the **ascribed epistemic authority of an advisor**  while remaining largely insensitive to the **actual quality or relevance of the advice**  [7](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/e0ff0074-f77e-9bac-e053-1705fe0af019/Euro%20J%20Social%20Psych%20-%202021%20-%20Pica%20-%20The%20epistemic%20bases%20of%20changes%20of%20opinion%20and%20choices.pdf). Conversely, individuals with low NFCC prefer a higher volume of diverse, heterogeneous information and adjust their opinions based on the substantive quality of the arguments presented [7](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/e0ff0074-f77e-9bac-e053-1705fe0af019/Euro%20J%20Social%20Psych%20-%202021%20-%20Pica%20-%20The%20epistemic%20bases%20of%20changes%20of%20opinion%20and%20choices.pdf).

In the context of misinformation ecosystems, this mechanism explains why high-NFCC populations can simultaneously exhibit rigid skepticism toward mainstream scientific consensus and uncritical acceptance of demonstrable falsehoods, as the determining variable is the locus of ascribed epistemic authority [7](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/e0ff0074-f77e-9bac-e053-1705fe0af019/Euro%20J%20Social%20Psych%20-%202021%20-%20Pica%20-%20The%20epistemic%20bases%20of%20changes%20of%20opinion%20and%20choices.pdf). When social and political identities become sorted into polarized "mega-identities," in-group elites and partisan media networks are granted supreme epistemic authority [39](https://www.scribd.com/document/830002950/Wrong). For high-NFCC individuals—whose cognitive style favors simplicity, order, and intuitive consonance—deference to an in-group epistemic authority provides rapid, socially validated closure without the cognitive strain of verifying underlying evidence [5](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/the-need-for-closure-and-conservati). Similarly, when trust in traditional epistemic authorities such as medical scientists or governmental agencies erodes, high-NFCC individuals gravitate toward alternative authorities that offer unambiguous certainty [5](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/the-need-for-closure-and-conservati). Paradoxically, even high trust in science can backfire when combined with heuristic processing, because individuals who defer uncritically to scientific authority cues become more vulnerable to misinformation cloaked in pseudo-scientific jargon [11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.02710). Under extreme conditions where personal significance loss or humiliation induces an acute spike in NFCC, a state of motivational imbalance can also emerge [5](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/the-need-for-closure-and-conservati). As documented by Kruglanski, Jocelyn J. Bélanger, Michele J. Gelfand, and colleagues, this heightened NFCC drives individuals toward radical, totalizing narratives validated by charismatic extremist authorities, crowding out competing cognitive constraints and cementing false ideological dogmas [5](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/the-need-for-closure-and-conservati).

## Memory Distortion, Retrieval-Induced Forgetting, and Forensic Suggestibility

Beyond the acceptance of public news and conspiracy narratives, NFCC exerts a powerful, direct influence on how episodic memories are reconstructed and distorted by post-event misinformation [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). In cognitive and forensic psychology, the **misinformation effect**  refers to the incorporation of misleading post-event suggestions into an individual's memory report of an originally witnessed event [44](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254899688_Misinformation_Effects_and_the_Suggestibility_of_Eyewitness_Memory). While early cognitive models treated memory distortions as cold, mechanical encoding or retrieval failures, research over the past decade proves that reconstructive memory is deeply regulated by epistemic motivation [48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6206073/).

### Motivated Retrieval-Induced Forgetting and Eyewitness Suggestibility

A foundational breakthrough in this area was achieved by Pica, Pierro, Bélanger, and Kruglanski (2014) in *Social Cognition* , building upon their 2013 tests of Cognitive Energetics Theory [4](https://www.kruglanskiarie.com/motivation-and-cognition). Across three studies utilizing eyewitness scenarios—such as recalling household items stolen in a burglary or characteristics of a suspect—Pica et al. (2014) examined how NFCC modulates **Retrieval-Induced Forgetting (RIF)**  and post-event **misinformation effects**  [48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6206073/). In standard memory paradigms, selectively practicing the retrieval of a subset of items from a category actively inhibits competing, unpracticed items from that same category—a phenomenon known as RIF [48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6206073/).

Pica et al. (2014) demonstrated that high-NFCC individuals exhibit significantly stronger RIF and a markedly amplified eyewitness misinformation effect compared to low-NFCC individuals [45](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820). Because competing memories create cognitive interference and interpretive ambiguity during recall, high-NFCC individuals actively suppress non-retrieved original details to achieve a clear, unambiguous memory representation [48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6206073/). In subsequent extensions (Pica et al., 2016, 2017, 2018), this same NFCC-driven RIF mechanism was shown to preserve gender and ethnic stereotypes by selectively inhibiting stereotype-inconsistent memories (such as agentic leadership qualities in female managers) while retaining stereotype-congruent traits [28](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Antonio-Pierro-3/2). Importantly, in line with Cognitive Energetics Theory, NFCC enhances RIF only when individuals possess adequate cognitive resources (driving force) to execute the active inhibitory suppression [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316716868_Multifaceted_Effects_of_Need_for_Cognitive_Closure_on_Effort_A_Cardiovascular_Response_Study). By actively suppressing competing details from the original event—and especially after a temporal delay of 24 hours when original memory traces naturally weaken—high-NFCC witnesses experience an accessibility deficit for non-practiced original details [44](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254899688_Misinformation_Effects_and_the_Suggestibility_of_Eyewitness_Memory). When subsequently exposed to misleading post-event information via leading questions presupposing false details or credible post-event narratives, high-NFCC witnesses seize upon the suggested misinformation to resolve memory ambiguity and construct a coherent account [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure).

### Co-Witness Memory Conformity and Discomfort with Ambiguity

In real-world forensic settings, misinformation is frequently encountered not just through leading interrogations, but through interpersonal discussions among co-witnesses—a phenomenon termed **memory conformity**  [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full). Extensive empirical work by Malwina Szpitalak, Magdalena Kękuś, Anna Hejniak, Romuald Polczyk, and colleagues at Jagiellonian University has mapped how NFCC governs compliance with forensic misinformation and proneness to false confession [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full).

In an investigation by Hejniak, Szpitalak, Kękuś, Chylińska, and Woźniak (2019), the researchers integrated Kassin and Kiechel's false confession paradigm with classic misinformation schemas, establishing NFCC as a significant determinant of both susceptibility to misleading suggestions and vulnerability to interrogative compliance [46](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Malwina-Szpitalak). Extending this to digital interpersonal environments, Kękuś, Szpitalak, Polczyk, and Barzykowski (2024) utilized an online version of the MORI-v technique with 72 participants (36 pairs, aged 18–54) who watched slightly different versions of a crime film (for example, a thief wearing a red cap versus a black cap) before discussing the event via video conference and completing an individual recognition test [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full).

The experiment confirmed a robust memory conformity effect, as recognition accuracy was significantly lower for discussed items where a co-witness introduced misinformation than for non-discussed control items [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full). Behavioral coding of the discussions revealed that when one witness introduced misinformation and the partner **did not dispute it** , the partner almost invariably adopted the false detail on the final test, indicating strong informational influence [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full). When Kękuś et al. (2024) analyzed the five subscales of the short Need for Cognitive Closure Scale against misinformation resistance, a highly specific psychometric pattern emerged: **Discomfort with Ambiguity**  was the sole NFCC subscale that correlated significantly with **lower resistance to misinformation**  (greater memory conformity), whereas the remaining four subscales (Preference for Order, Predictability, Decisiveness, and Closed-Mindedness) showed no statistically significant correlation with co-witness conformity [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full). When a co-witness asserts a contradictory detail during discussion, it induces immediate epistemic conflict and memory distrust [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full). For individuals with acute discomfort with ambiguity, the psychological strain of holding two conflicting memories (their own recollection versus their partner's assertion) is intolerable, prompting them to resolve the discrepancy by capitulating to the partner's confident false claim [14](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239139/full). Related forensic interventions by Szpitalak and colleagues demonstrate that **Reinforced Self-Affirmation (RSA)** —which bolsters self-competence and reduces state anxiety and memory distrust via positive feedback—can effectively buffer witnesses against this compliance [19](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10829763/).

## Boundary Conditions: Where Need for Closure Does Not Moderate Susceptibility

While NFCC strongly modulates misinformation acceptance when uncertainty reduction, source authority, or memory inhibition are engaged, empirical research reveals critical boundary conditions where NFCC exerts **virtually zero moderating effect**  [57](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31179863/). Specifically, when false belief formation is driven by passive, low-level perceptual fluency or when individuals perform decontextualized headline evaluations without prior framing, other cognitive, demographic, and affective mechanisms dominate [27](https://scholarsarchive.library.albany.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3830&context=legacy-etd).

### Invariance in the Illusory Truth Effect

One of the most pervasive drivers of misinformation acceptance is the **illusory truth effect** : repeated exposure to false claims increases their perceived accuracy relative to novel claims, even when the falsehoods contradict prior knowledge [3](https://www.scribd.com/document/558114090/The-psychological-drivers-of-misinformation-belief-and-its-resistance-to-correction). Repetition enhances processing fluency and strengthens encoding in memory, generating a peripheral metacognitive cue of familiarity that the brain misattributes to truth [3](https://www.scribd.com/document/558114090/The-psychological-drivers-of-misinformation-belief-and-its-resistance-to-correction). Signal-detection and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) reanalyses of news evaluation studies demonstrate that headline familiarity exerts a massive positive effect on response bias (B=1.03,95% CI [0.67,1.34]B = 1.03, 95\% \text{ CI } [0.67, 1.34]), powerfully shifting individuals toward labeling familiar false headlines as true [60](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2409329121). By contrast, demographic and cognitive covariates exert much smaller shifts on baseline response tendencies: formal education produces a slight true-news response bias (B=0.10,95% CI [0.05,0.14]B = 0.10, 95\% \text{ CI } [0.05, 0.14]) that increases accuracy for true news but lowers accuracy for false news, whereas increasing age (B=−0.19,95% CI [−0.24,−0.14]B = -0.19, 95\% \text{ CI } [-0.24, -0.14]) and higher analytical thinking (B=−0.19,95% CI [−0.26,−0.12]B = -0.19, 95\% \text{ CI } [-0.26, -0.12]) induce a cautious false-news bias [60](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2409329121). Notably, despite older adults exhibiting greater baseline caution and discrimination on novel headlines, prior exposure effects increase with age and are strongest in the oldest cohort (aged 60+), particularly for false news, due to age-related impairments in strategic source recollection [59](https://osf.io/download/z78xm).

In a multi-study investigation published in *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* , Jonas De keersmaecker, David Dunning, Gordon Pennycook, David G. Rand, Carmen Sanchez, Christian Unkelbach, and Arne Roets (2020) tested whether the illusory truth effect is moderated by individual differences in **need for cognitive closure** , cognitive ability (verbal and fluid intelligence), or analytic cognitive style (Cognitive Reflection Test, CRT) [9](https://osf.io/download/5p4nw/). Their findings were unequivocal: **the magnitude of the repetition-induced truth effect is completely invariant across NFCC, intelligence, and analytic thinking**  [9](https://osf.io/download/5p4nw/). Similarly, individuals high and low in Need for Cognition exhibit comparable illusory truth effects, and those high in Need for Cognition are even more susceptible when not forewarned about false information [59](https://osf.io/download/z78xm). Because processing fluency operates as an automatic, low-level cognitive mechanism relatively outside volitional cognitive control, high-NFCC and low-NFCC individuals are equally vulnerable to repetition-induced truth [9](https://osf.io/download/5p4nw/).

### Analytical Reflection, Affective Arousal, and Active Fact-Checking

A second boundary condition emerges in decontextualized fake news discernment tasks, where participants evaluate isolated headlines or social media posts without a prior freezing event or salient authority cue [58](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.790848/full). Under these conditions, classical dual-process theories (System 1 intuitive versus System 2 analytical processing) and personality traits account for the bulk of individual variance [27](https://scholarsarchive.library.albany.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3830&context=legacy-etd). Stronger performance on the Cognitive Reflection Test and Actively Open-Minded Thinking consistently predict superior truth discernment and cautious evaluation [11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.02710). In active crowdworker misinformation assessment studies (Kaufman & Broukhim, 2022, 2024), where participants evaluated COVID-19 tweets covering vaccines, masks, and hydroxychloroquine (with nearly 10 out of 24 tweets mislabeled on average), Big Five **Conscientiousness**  accounted for 43.2%43.2\% of the variance in detection accuracy among MTurk workers, followed by **CRT analytical reflection**  at 24.1%24.1\% (together explaining 67.3%67.3\% of the variance) [62](https://escholarship.org/content/qt71p35602/qt71p35602.pdf). Recruitment platform also drove major performance disparities, with university SONA participants achieving substantially higher mean coding accuracy (2.172.17, representing 27.82%27.82\% of top performers) than MTurk crowdworkers (−4.58-4.58, representing 8.33%8.33\% of top performers, p<.001p < .001) [62](https://escholarship.org/content/qt71p35602/qt71p35602.pdf). When controlling for CRT, Conscientiousness, Political Orientation, and Trust in Medical Scientists, baseline trait NFCC frequently exhibits non-significant direct associations with raw headline discernment or misinformation warning tag attitudes [11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.02710).

Simultaneously, emotional intensity directly undermines analytical scrutiny in decontextualized news evaluation by taxing working memory and triggering amygdala-mediated prioritization of affective stimuli [27](https://scholarsarchive.library.albany.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3830&context=legacy-etd). Experimental evidence confirms that experiencing heightened positive emotions (r=.14,p<.001r = .14, p < .001) or negative emotions (r=.17,p<.001r = .17, p < .001)—and specifically high **emotional arousal/intensity**  rather than valence alone—significantly inflates both belief in fake news headlines and metacognitive confidence in those false judgments [27](https://scholarsarchive.library.albany.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3830&context=legacy-etd). Similarly, developmentally heightened neuroticism and emotional reactivity during adolescence increase susceptibility to misinformation belief and sharing [41](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-devpsych-010923-093547?crawler=true&mimetype=application/pdf).

## Post-Acceptance Entrenchment: The Continued Influence Effect and Debunking Architecture

The most consequential role of NFCC in the misinformation lifecycle occurs *after*  a falsehood has been accepted and subsequently debunked [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Across psychological literature, simple retractions rarely eliminate the impact of false information [1](https://www.emc-lab.org/uploads/1/1/3/6/113627673/ecker.2022.nrp_preprint.pdf). Instead, individuals exhibit **Belief Perseverance Bias (BPB)** —maintaining biased attitudes after the evidentiary basis is falsified—and the **Continued Influence Effect (CIE)** , wherein retracted misinformation continues to drive inferential thought and causal judgments about an event [3](https://www.scribd.com/document/558114090/The-psychological-drivers-of-misinformation-belief-and-its-resistance-to-correction).

### Mental Model Completeness Versus Epistemic Discomfort

Two primary theoretical frameworks explain the Continued Influence Effect: dual-process retrieval failure and mental model theory [3](https://www.scribd.com/document/558114090/The-psychological-drivers-of-misinformation-belief-and-its-resistance-to-correction). Under the dual-process account, retractions often fail because misinformation is retrieved automatically via fast, acontextual familiarity processes (or carries a stronger initial affective charge), whereas recalling a retraction tag requires effortful, strategic recollection [3](https://www.scribd.com/document/558114090/The-psychological-drivers-of-misinformation-belief-and-its-resistance-to-correction). Consequently, deficits in working memory capacity, short-term memory, and general cognitive ability severely impair attitude adjustment after correction—as demonstrated by De keersmaecker and Roets (2017), who showed that individuals with lower cognitive ability fail to reset negative social impressions after learning that accusations of theft and drug dealing were fabricated, even when controlling for open-mindedness and authoritarianism [69](https://www.psypost.org/fake-news-study-finds-incorrect-information-cant-corrected-simply-pointing-false/). Conversely, the mental model theory—formulated by Hollyn M. Johnson and Colleen M. Seifert (1994) and expanded by Ullrich K. H. Ecker and Stephan Lewandowsky—posits that individuals construct integrated causal models of unfolding events [3](https://www.scribd.com/document/558114090/The-psychological-drivers-of-misinformation-belief-and-its-resistance-to-correction). When a central piece of misinformation is simply negated without providing a replacement cause, the retraction threatens the internal coherence of the model and leaves an explanatory **gap**  [64](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/social-media-and-democracy/misinformation-and-its-correction/61FA7FD743784A723BA234533012E810).

It is precisely at this mental-model gap that **Need for Cognitive Closure**  acts as an epistemic lock [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). As demonstrated in research examining trait moderators of belief revision (including Leding & Antonio, 2019; Wittenberg & Berinsky, 2020; and Susmann & Wegener, 2022), invalidating a causal link in a mental model induces psychological discomfort [67](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343996617_Misinformation_and_Its_Correction). Because individuals high in NFCC possess an acute aversion to ambiguity and incomplete knowledge structures, they prefer a **complete, coherent, albeit inaccurate mental model**  over an incomplete model containing an unresolved causal vacuum [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Driven by the permanence tendency ("freezing"), high-NFCC individuals continue to rely on retracted misinformation during subsequent inferences—and rely on self-generated causal explanations that become independent of the original claim in memory—specifically to avoid reverting to epistemic uncertainty [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). Furthermore, in the case of conspiracy theories, the narrative structure itself is self-insulating: because conspirators are assumed to engage in covert cover-ups, authoritative retractions are easily assimilated by closed-minded believers as further proof of the conspiracy [29](https://pure.eur.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/211143956/Conspiracy_Theories_Resistance_to_Evidence_and_Propaganda.pdf).

## Conclusions and Strategic Implications for Misinformation Mitigation

Synthesizing the empirical literature demonstrates that the Need for Cognitive Closure is neither a simple measure of gullibility nor a uniform cognitive deficit; rather, it is a directional epistemic motive that regulates when information search terminates and how fiercely existing mental models are defended [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). When an informational environment is characterized by causal uncertainty, time pressure, or an absence of official explanations, the urgency tendency compels high-NFCC individuals to seize upon salient falsehoods, conspiratorial schemas, or pronouncements from high-epistemic-authority leaders without scrutinizing argument quality [7](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/e0ff0074-f77e-9bac-e053-1705fe0af019/Euro%20J%20Social%20Psych%20-%202021%20-%20Pica%20-%20The%20epistemic%20bases%20of%20changes%20of%20opinion%20and%20choices.pdf). Once an explanation is encoded, the permanence tendency freezes the mental model, driving the active inhibition of competing memories via retrieval-induced forgetting and insulating the false belief against simple retractions that threaten to leave causal gaps [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure). At the same time, low-level perceptual biases such as the repetition-induced illusory truth effect bypass epistemic motivation entirely, operating automatically across all levels of NFCC [9](https://osf.io/download/5p4nw/).

Because NFCC is a motivational drive for epistemic structure, interventions that merely exhort the public to "think harder" or present bare negations consistently fail among high-NFCC audiences [1](https://www.emc-lab.org/uploads/1/1/3/6/113627673/ecker.2022.nrp_preprint.pdf). Effective mitigation requires aligning communication architecture with the mechanics of lay epistemics [1](https://www.emc-lab.org/uploads/1/1/3/6/113627673/ecker.2022.nrp_preprint.pdf). In reactive debunking, simple retractions must be replaced with comprehensive **refutations**  that supply a plausible, factual **alternative explanation**  [20](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369091178_Effective_mitigation_of_the_belief_perseverance_bias_after_the_retraction_of_misinformation_Awareness_training_and_counter-speech). To satisfy high-NFCC recipients and prevent belief regression over time, the alternative account must possess the same explanatory relevance as the retracted myth while remaining clear and simple enough to be grasped effortlessly, seamlessly filling the gap in the recipient's mental model without sacrificing cognitive closure [20](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369091178_Effective_mitigation_of_the_belief_perseverance_bias_after_the_retraction_of_misinformation_Awareness_training_and_counter-speech). When beliefs are entrenched by self-generated causal schemas, applying the **counter-explanation debiasing technique** —which prompts individuals to actively generate or write causal explanations supporting the validity of an alternative hypothesis—builds a competing mental model capable of displacing the frozen belief [20](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369091178_Effective_mitigation_of_the_belief_perseverance_bias_after_the_retraction_of_misinformation_Awareness_training_and_counter-speech). More broadly, scientific and public health communications should match their delivery format to the recipient's epistemic style, framing verified conclusions in structured, definitive language so that high-NFCC individuals do not reject the message out of frustration with open-ended ambiguity [2](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2026/Epistemic_motivation_and_the_need_for_cognitive_closure).

Finally, because the urgency and permanence tendencies heavily favor the *first*  explanation encoded in memory, pre-empting misinformation before freezing occurs is far more effective than retroactive correction [1](https://www.emc-lab.org/uploads/1/1/3/6/113627673/ecker.2022.nrp_preprint.pdf). Up-front **prebunking**  and inoculation forewarnings that explicitly explain the mechanisms of the Continued Influence Effect, or expose manipulative disinformation tactics such as "fake experts," false-balance coverage, and emotional arousal, provide metacognitive cues that prompt recipients to scrutinize incoming claims critically before closure is reached [20](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369091178_Effective_mitigation_of_the_belief_perseverance_bias_after_the_retraction_of_misinformation_Awareness_training_and_counter-speech). Above all, during emerging crises, institutional authorities must avoid prolonged informational vacuums; providing rapid, transparent, and structured interim explanations satisfies the public's need for closure early, harnessing the freezing mechanism to anchor society on verified facts rather than opportunistic falsehoods [12](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/61588/1/Marchlewska%20et%20al.%20Addicted%20to%20Answers%20FINAL.pdf).
