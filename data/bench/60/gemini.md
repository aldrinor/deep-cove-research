# Comprehensive Cislunar Space Situational Awareness and Short-Term Target Tracking Architecture

## The Cislunar Dynamical and Observational Paradigm

Expanding human, commercial, and defense operations beyond Geosynchronous Earth Orbit (GEO) into the cislunar volume requires a fundamental restructuring of Space Situational Awareness (SSA) and Space Domain Awareness (SDA) architectures [1](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/bde4d870-0a09-4f85-a928-4e5879cdf3be/downloads/62edb211-24fb-4432-bab6-1794335f3d9c/A%20Comprehensive%20review%20on%20Cislunar%20Expansion.pdf?ver=1779074152037). Over the coming decade, the cislunar regime will host more than 30 lunar and deep-space missions focused on trajectory testing, lunar surface mapping, regolith resource harvesting, and the establishment of sustained human infrastructure [1](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/bde4d870-0a09-4f85-a928-4e5879cdf3be/downloads/62edb211-24fb-4432-bab6-1794335f3d9c/A%20Comprehensive%20review%20on%20Cislunar%20Expansion.pdf?ver=1779074152037). Whereas traditional SSA focuses primarily on cataloging orbital states, cislunar SDA encompasses characterizing non-cooperative resident space objects (RSOs), inferring operational intent, and maintaining uninterrupted custody across an expansive volume governed by simultaneously competing gravitational fields [1](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/bde4d870-0a09-4f85-a928-4e5879cdf3be/downloads/62edb211-24fb-4432-bab6-1794335f3d9c/A%20Comprehensive%20review%20on%20Cislunar%20Expansion.pdf?ver=1779074152037). Legacy near-Earth tracking infrastructure and estimation algorithms—engineered under the assumption of Keplerian two-body dynamics and dense ground-based radar or optical coverage—fail when extended to cislunar regimes [2](https://www.kbr.com/en/document/16811).

### Multi-Body Astrodynamics and Chaotic Divergence

Within the cislunar domain, the simultaneous gravitational perturbations of the Earth, the Moon, and the Sun invalidate planar conic-section approximations [1](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/bde4d870-0a09-4f85-a928-4e5879cdf3be/downloads/62edb211-24fb-4432-bab6-1794335f3d9c/A%20Comprehensive%20review%20on%20Cislunar%20Expansion.pdf?ver=1779074152037). The foundational baseline for modeling target motion in this regime is the Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem (CR3BP), formulated in a rotating (synodic) barycentric coordinate frame that co-rotates with the Earth and Moon [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D). By normalizing the Earth-Moon distance to 1 n.d.1\text{ n.d.} (non-dimensional unit), the characteristic time scale to 4.342 days4.342\text{ days}, and defining the mass ratio parameter as μ=mMoonmEarth+mMoon≈0.012150582\mu = \frac{m_{\text{Moon}}}{m_{\text{Earth}} + m_{\text{Moon}}} \approx 0.012150582, the Earth and Moon are fixed along the rotating xx-axis at (−μ,0,0)(-\mu, 0, 0) and (1−μ,0,0)(1-\mu, 0, 0), respectively [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18058).

For a target with synodic state vector x=[x,y,z,x˙,y˙,z˙]⊤∈R6\mathbf{x} = [x, y, z, \dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6, the equations of motion incorporating potential unknown thrust or disturbance accelerations w(t,τ)=[wx,wy,wz]⊤\mathbf{w}(t, \tau) = [w_x, w_y, w_z]^\top are governed by coupled, non-linear second-order differential equations [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18058):

x¨−2y˙=∂U∂x+wx(t,τ)=x−(1−μ)(x+μ)r13−μ(x−1+μ)r23+wx(t,τ)\ddot{x} - 2\dot{y} = \frac{\partial U}{\partial x} + w_x(t, \tau) = x - \frac{(1-\mu)(x+\mu)}{r_1^3} - \frac{\mu(x - 1 + \mu)}{r_2^3} + w_x(t, \tau)

y¨+2x˙=∂U∂y+wy(t,τ)=y−(1−μ)yr13−μyr23+wy(t,τ)\ddot{y} + 2\dot{x} = \frac{\partial U}{\partial y} + w_y(t, \tau) = y - \frac{(1-\mu)y}{r_1^3} - \frac{\mu y}{r_2^3} + w_y(t, \tau)

z¨=∂U∂z+wz(t,τ)=−(1−μ)zr13−μzr23+wz(t,τ)\ddot{z} = \frac{\partial U}{\partial z} + w_z(t, \tau) = -\frac{(1-\mu)z}{r_1^3} - \frac{\mu z}{r_2^3} + w_z(t, \tau)

where r1=(x+μ)2+y2+z2r_1 = \sqrt{(x+\mu)^2 + y^2 + z^2} and r2=(x−1+μ)2+y2+z2r_2 = \sqrt{(x-1+\mu)^2 + y^2 + z^2} represent the radial distances from the target to the centers of mass of the Earth and Moon, respectively, and U(r)U(\mathbf{r}) is the effective three-body pseudo-potential [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18058):

U(r)=1−μr1+μr2+x2+y22U(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{1-\mu}{r_1} + \frac{\mu}{r_2} + \frac{x^2 + y^2}{2}

Unlike the two-body problem, which admits six independent integrals of motion (the classical Keplerian orbital elements) and closed-form analytical propagation via ff and gg series, the ballistic CR3BP (w=0\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{0}) admits only a single scalar integral of motion: the Jacobi constant C(r,v)C(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v}) [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf):

C(r,v)=2U(r)−v2=2(1−μr1+μr2)+(x2+y2)−(x˙2+y˙2+z˙2)C(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v}) = 2U(\mathbf{r}) - v^2 = 2\left(\frac{1-\mu}{r_1} + \frac{\mu}{r_2}\right) + (x^2 + y^2) - (\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 + \dot{z}^2)

This scarcity of invariants has profound consequences for target tracking [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf). All periodic and quasi-periodic trajectories in the cislunar domain exhibit varying degrees of dynamical instability and chaos [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D). Small initial state estimation errors or unmodeled perturbations are amplified exponentially over short time horizons, particularly during close lunar passages or along unstable invariant manifolds near the collinear Lagrange points (L1,L2L_1, L_2) [11](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2021_AAS_FruHowDeMBha.pdf). While long-term evolutions of chaotic trajectories are partially bounded by dynamical barriers formed by invariant curves of quasi-periodic trajectories, short-term trajectory dispersion remains severe [11](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2021_AAS_FruHowDeMBha.pdf). Furthermore, simulations utilizing the NASA Standard Break-Up Model (SBM) within the CR3BP reveal that when catastrophic collisions or break-ups occur along cislunar trajectories, the resulting debris clouds do not remain confined to narrow orbital shells as in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf). Instead, multi-body gravitational folding disperses the majority of fragments throughout the entire cislunar volume within three months, threatening lunar surface assets, cislunar transfer corridors, and the terrestrial GEO region simultaneously [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf). Specifically, break-ups in Southern and Northern L2L_2 Halo orbits result in 34.3%34.3\% and 28.5%28.5\% of fragments impacting the lunar surface within 90 days, while break-ups in L1L_1 Lyapunov and L1L_1 Northern Halo orbits result in 2.7%2.7\% and 2.6%2.6\% of fragments impacting the Earth [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf).

### Radiometric, Geometric, and Exclusion Bottlenecks

The astrodynamic complexity of cislunar space is compounded by severe radiometric and geometric sensing constraints [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D). Distances spanning 384,400 km384,400\text{ km}—and extending up to 550,000 km550,000\text{ km} for distant retrograde and halo orbits—induce severe signal attenuation, making targets substantially fainter than GEO assets [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D). For passive Electro-Optical (EO) sensors, a space object is typically considered detectable only if its apparent visual magnitude satisfies msc<20m_{\text{sc}} < 20 and its signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) exceeds sensor-specific thresholds [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf). Apparent visual magnitude is governed by solar irradiance, target range dd, and the Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF) integrated across NfacetN_{\text{facet}} visible spacecraft facets [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf):

msc=msun−2.5log⁡10(∑i=1NfacetIiAicos⁡θid2Isun)m_{\text{sc}} = m_{\text{sun}} - 2.5 \log_{10} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{facet}}} \frac{I_i A_i \cos\theta_i}{d^2 I_{\text{sun}}} \right)

where msunm_{\text{sun}} is the apparent magnitude of the Sun, IiI_i is the reflected radiance of the ii-th facet of area AiA_i, θi\theta_i is the observer phase angle relative to the facet normal, and IsunI_{\text{sun}} is incident solar irradiance [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf). While box-wing satellites occasionally produce high-irradiance specular glints off solar arrays or antennas, these glints are non-continuous and occur only during narrow geometric windows over an orbital period; continuous tracking must rely on diffuse reflection, which frequently falls below the limiting magnitude of terrestrial apertures [11](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2021_AAS_FruHowDeMBha.pdf).

Beyond flux attenuation, optical sensors are severely degraded by celestial glare and eclipse geometries [11](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2021_AAS_FruHowDeMBha.pdf). A target is only optically observable if it resides outside the umbral and penumbral shadows of the Earth and Moon and if the angular separation between the observer-to-target line-of-sight vector and the target-to-celestial-body vector exceeds strict exclusion thresholds: 50∘50^\circ for the Sun, 35∘35^\circ for the Earth, and 30∘30^\circ for the Moon [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf). High lunar albedo washes out optical detectors attempting to track objects passing near the lunar disk—a volume commonly designated in architectural trade studies as the "Cone of Shame" [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D). Furthermore, because cislunar orbital periods span days to weeks, apparent angular velocities across the celestial sphere are exceptionally slow, requiring extended observation cadences to observe a dynamically meaningful fraction of an orbit while simultaneously complicating target discrimination against dense, unfamiliar stellar backgrounds [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D).

## Multi-Tiered Sensing Architectures for Comprehensive Coverage

Because no single sensor location—whether terrestrial, in near-Earth orbit, in cislunar orbit, or on the lunar surface—can overcome solar and lunar exclusion cones, weather, and 1/r21/r^2 radiometric losses simultaneously, comprehensive cislunar situational awareness requires a federated, multi-phenomenology "Cislunar Custody Chain" [11](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2021_AAS_FruHowDeMBha.pdf). This architecture integrates five distinct sensing tiers optimized through Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) and mathematical graph coverage frameworks [19](https://hanspeterschaub.info/Papers/grads/MichaelKlonowski.pdf).

### Space-Based Cislunar Constellations and Mobile Patrol Sentinels

Moving optical sensors into cislunar space eliminates atmospheric attenuation and decouples viewing geometry from terrestrial rotation [16](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/02/04/afrl-oracle-program-for-cislunar-space-situational-awareness-ssa/). Comprehensive architectural studies demonstrate that placing observers across nine periodic CR3BP orbit families—specifically selecting three stable orbits of differing periods per family to form a 28-orbit global network—enables observers to sweep wide volumes of cislunar space while maintaining focused custody around the Lagrange points and the lunar surface [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf). Within these constellations, phasing observers into synodic resonances (such as 3:2, 1:2, and 5:3 resonances with the Earth-Moon synodic period) ensures repeating geometric coverage across 60-period cycles [19](https://hanspeterschaub.info/Papers/grads/MichaelKlonowski.pdf). Distant Retrograde Orbits (DROs) provide high-stability wide-area surveillance, whereas Near-Rectilinear Halo Orbits (NRHOs) and L1/L2L_1/L_2 Lyapunov and Halo orbits provide close-in custody of lunar transfers [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf).

However, purely ballistic constellations remain vulnerable to predictable phase-angle blind spots [16](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/02/04/afrl-oracle-program-for-cislunar-space-situational-awareness-ssa/). Consequently, operational pathfinders such as the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) *Oracle*  program (formerly the Cislunar Highway Patrol System, or CHPS, including the *Oracle-Prime*  spacecraft bus developed with Terran Orbital) and the *Defense Deep Space Sentinel (D2S2)*  (equipped with a 20 cm20\text{ cm} optical aperture) introduce active maneuverability into the sensing tier [16](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/02/04/afrl-oracle-program-for-cislunar-space-situational-awareness-ssa/). Utilizing high-thrust, non-toxic ("green") chemical and modular multimode propulsion systems alongside radiation-hardened, fault-tolerant computing architectures, *Oracle*  operates near the L1L_1 (uninterrupted lunar near-side view) and L2L_2 (lunar far-side coverage) points [16](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/02/04/afrl-oracle-program-for-cislunar-space-situational-awareness-ssa/). Rather than drifting passively, a patrol platform expends Δv\Delta v to adjust its orbital phase relative to the Sun-Earth-Moon line, actively optimizing solar illumination angles so that its optical payload is never blinded for extended durations by full-Earth or full-Moon albedo [16](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/02/04/afrl-oracle-program-for-cislunar-space-situational-awareness-ssa/).

### Out-of-Plane Pole-Sitters and Lunar Surface Stations

To resolve the planar ambiguity and celestial exclusion zones inherent to in-plane CR3BP orbits, out-of-plane pole-sitter platforms and lunar surface optical stations provide orthogonal observation geometries [2](https://www.kbr.com/en/document/16811). Positioned at altitudes between 1.0 Mkm1.0\text{ Mkm} and 2.5 Mkm2.5\text{ Mkm} directly above the Earth-Moon orbital plane in a Sun-Centered Inertial Frame, a pole-sitter looks down vertically onto the entire cislunar arena [2](https://www.kbr.com/en/document/16811). Because the platform must continuously thrust along the ZZ-axis to counteract the combined pull-down gravitational forces of the Earth (which remains constant) and the Sun (which varies sinusoidally with season), fuel expenditure trades inversely with altitude: higher altitudes (2.5 Mkm2.5\text{ Mkm}) reduce required thrust acceleration, decrease vehicle wet mass, and extend operational lifetime, achieving ATP+10 years\text{ATP} + 10\text{ years} if refueled [2](https://www.kbr.com/en/document/16811). Autonomous navigation for the pole-sitter is maintained via inverted Positioning, Navigation, and Timing (iPNT) and optical triangulation against known GEO RSOs and background stars [2](https://www.kbr.com/en/document/16811). Downlink of processed one-minute tracking snapshots (approximately 1 Kb1\text{ Kb} per frame) requires only 10 to 100 bps10\text{ to }100\text{ bps} via compact RF links, while raw full-frame optical imagery is supported at up to 10 Gbps10\text{ Gbps} from 2.5 Mkm2.5\text{ Mkm} using a 5 W5\text{ W}, 1 μm1\,\mu\text{m} (300 THz300\text{ THz}) Deep Space Optical Communication (DSOC) laser terminal [2](https://www.kbr.com/en/document/16811).

Complementing high-altitude vertical observation, deploying optical telescopes directly on the lunar surface—such as proposed stations at Shackleton Crater on the lunar south pole—eliminates the lunar exclusion cone entirely for objects approaching the Moon [11](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2021_AAS_FruHowDeMBha.pdf). Coupling lunar ground-based observers with space-based cislunar telescopes significantly improves state observability during perilune passages, provides a rock-solid rotational baseline free of spacecraft attitude jitter, and supports anthropogenic change detection on and near the lunar regolith [28](https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/mec_aereng_facwork/5894/).

### Terrestrial Active Radar and Passive RF Aperture Synthesis

While space-based EO sensors form the backbone of deep cislunar custody, terrestrial non-optical systems are essential for all-weather, daylight-independent monitoring of the cislunar entry gateway and for tracking targets inside solar or lunar optical exclusion zones [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D). Legacy ground-based optical networks (such as GEODSS) are incapacitated by cloud cover and daylight [30](https://migflug.com/afterburner/darc-deep-space-advanced-radar-texas-lake-kickapoo-2026/). To secure the GEO and xGEO entry corridors (35,786 km35,786\text{ km} and beyond), the United States Space Force, in a trilateral partnership with Australia and the United Kingdom and prime contractor Northrop Grumman, is deploying the Deep Space Advanced Radar Capability (DARC) network [30](https://migflug.com/afterburner/darc-deep-space-advanced-radar-texas-lake-kickapoo-2026/). Comprising three globally distributed sites—Site 1 in Exmouth, Western Australia (construction completed December 2024, initial multi-satellite tracking demonstrated in August 2025, working toward initial operational capability in 2026), Site 2 at Cawdor Barracks in Pembrokeshire, Wales (scheduled for 2030), and Site 3 at Lake Kickapoo, Texas (selected in September 2026 for operational acceptance in 2030–2032)—DARC utilizes 27 parabolic antennas across the network in a distributed X-band transmit/receive array [30](https://migflug.com/afterburner/darc-deep-space-advanced-radar-texas-lake-kickapoo-2026/). By coherently combining signals to synthesize a massive effective aperture, DARC tracks objects as small as 10 cm10\text{ cm} at geostationary ranges 24/7 in all weather, providing immediate early warning of trans-lunar injections (TLI) and GEO-to-cislunar maneuvers [30](https://migflug.com/afterburner/darc-deep-space-advanced-radar-texas-lake-kickapoo-2026/).

Because active radar suffers from severe two-way free-space path loss beyond the GEO belt, passive Radio-Frequency (RF) sensors exploit the one-way propagation of a spacecraft's own Telemetry, Tracking, and Command (TTC) downlinks to track targets across the full lunar distance [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D). Academic and operational surveys demonstrate that even a modest 0.6 m0.6\text{ m} S-band parabolic reflector sampling at 20-second intervals can detect and track cislunar targets via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) waterfall processing [36](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2023/Poster/Joyce.pdf). When scaled to long-baseline aperture synthesis using 12 m12\text{ m} parabolic antennas, cross-correlation of received TTC waveforms yields high-precision Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA) and Frequency Difference of Arrival (FDOA) observables [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18058). Passive RF is immune to solar glare, lunar albedo, and eclipse shadows, providing an indispensable hybrid custody bridge whenever an emitting spacecraft transits an optical exclusion zone [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D).

### Persistent Detection Corridors and Facility Location Optimization

Integrating these disparate tiers into a cohesive architecture requires formal mathematical optimization [19](https://hanspeterschaub.info/Papers/grads/MichaelKlonowski.pdf). Because constellation design and sensor scheduling are coupled—the realized performance of an observer network depends on its dynamic tasking schedule—the architecture trade space can be formulated as a Lagrangian Facility Location Problem (FLP) [20](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/1.A36361). By modeling static volumetric coverage demands (such as the lunar "Cone of Shame") and dynamic traffic demands across discrete epochs, Lagrangian relaxation rapidly identifies near-optimal observer placements [19](https://hanspeterschaub.info/Papers/grads/MichaelKlonowski.pdf). Alongside capacity-based architecture optimization, Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) frameworks employ open-loop Linear Covariance (LinCov) analysis—which executes the covariance propagation portion of an Extended Kalman Filter to obtain statistical navigation performance in a single run rather than thousands of Monte Carlo trials—to rapidly hypertune observer motion models, telescope quality, and autonomous cislunar navigation links [23](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393975299_System_Design_and_Analysis_for_Cislunar_Space_Domain_Awareness_Through_Distributed_Sensors).

Furthermore, for protecting high-value assets such as crewed spacecraft executing trans-lunar injection (TLI) or L1/L2L_1/L_2 transfers, the time-varying detectability volume of a multi-sensor architecture can be encoded into a directed mathematical graph [19](https://hanspeterschaub.info/Papers/grads/MichaelKlonowski.pdf). Refining continuous paths through this graph via direct collocation yields **persistent detection corridors** : three-dimensional trajectory tubes within which a spacecraft is guaranteed to remain continuously visible and observable by the sensor network, even when subjected to small trajectory perturbations, with defined Δv\Delta v bounds required to remain inside the corridor [5](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2025/Cislunar_SDA/Evans.pdf).

## Short-Term Tracking Mechanics: Initial Orbit Determination and Uncertainty Realism

Supporting short-term cislunar tracking and monitoring tasks requires overcoming a critical operational vulnerability: observations of cislunar targets typically consist of sparse, short-arc, angles-only optical tracks (azimuth AZAZ and elevation ELEL, or right ascension α\alpha and declination δ\delta) separated by multi-day observation gaps [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). In a topocentric or sensor-centric coordinate frame T\mathcal{T}, a single optical measurement provides only the unit line-of-sight vector [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18058):

AZ=tan⁡−1(yTxT),EL=π2−cos⁡−1(zTxT2+yT2+zT2)AZ = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y_{\mathcal{T}}}{x_{\mathcal{T}}}\right), \quad EL = \frac{\pi}{2} - \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{z_{\mathcal{T}}}{\sqrt{x_{\mathcal{T}}^2 + y_{\mathcal{T}}^2 + z_{\mathcal{T}}^2}}\right)

leaving range ρ\rho and range-rate ρ˙\dot{\rho} completely unobservable from a single epoch [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01428v1).

### Cislunar Initial Orbit Determination (IOD)

In near-Earth space, classical Gauss or Laplace IOD algorithms resolve the unknown range vector ρ=[ρ1,ρ2,ρ3]⊤\boldsymbol{\rho} = [\rho_1, \rho_2, \rho_3]^\top from three angles-only observations by exploiting two assumptions: Keplerian motion is strictly coplanar (r2=c1r1+c3r3\mathbf{r}_2 = c_1\mathbf{r}_1 + c_3\mathbf{r}_3), and position at any epoch can be expressed as a linear combination of initial position and velocity via two-body ff and gg functions, r(t)=f(ρ0,t−t0)r(t0)+g(ρ0,t−t0)r˙(t0)\mathbf{r}(t) = f(\rho_0, t-t_0)\mathbf{r}(t_0) + g(\rho_0, t-t_0)\dot{\mathbf{r}}(t_0) [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18058). In the three-body cislunar regime, out-of-plane gravitational acceleration and Coriolis forces (2y˙,−2x˙2\dot{y}, -2\dot{x}) violate coplanarity and invalidate two-body ff and gg closures [7](https://repository.gatech.edu/bitstreams/bccc0a76-3964-4d92-aa43-aa35aa95c9e9/download).

To execute reliable short-term IOD in cislunar space, modern architectures first bound the unmeasured phase space using Admissible Region (AR) theory [39](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2025/Space-Debris/Popplewell.pdf). Given an optical attributable vector A=(α,δ,α˙,δ˙)\mathcal{A} = (\alpha, \delta, \dot{\alpha}, \dot{\delta}) extracted from a short optical tracklet, or a radar attributable (ρ,ρ˙,α,δ)(\rho, \dot{\rho}, \alpha, \delta) leaving angular rates (α˙,δ˙)(\dot{\alpha}, \dot{\delta}) undetermined, physical constraints define the set of feasible orbits [39](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2025/Space-Debris/Popplewell.pdf). While near-Earth ARs constrain two-body specific orbital energy (ϵ≤0\epsilon \le 0) and periapsis/apoapsis radii [39](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2025/Space-Debris/Popplewell.pdf), cislunar IOD replaces or augments these with **Jacobi Constant Pruning**  [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf). By hypothesizing bounds on the target's Jacobi constant C(r,v)C(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v}) corresponding to stable cislunar families or transfer corridors, non-physical ranges and range-rates are systematically eliminated [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf). Recent extensions further tighten this initial state volume using **Sensor- and Measurement-Centric Admissible Regions** —which incorporate sensor field-of-view boundaries, limiting magnitude (msc<20m_{\text{sc}} < 20), and SNR detection limits directly into the (ρ,ρ˙)(\rho, \dot{\rho}) constraint set [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf)—as well as **Periodic Orbit Manifold Admissible Regions** , which project invariant manifold structures of CR3BP periodic orbits onto the range/range-rate plane [37](https://amostech.space/track/cislunar-sda/). The resulting Probabilistic Admissible Region (PAR) is then discretized into a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to initialize sequential Bayesian filters without premature convergence to false roots [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01428v1).

For short-arc angles-only tracks where point-estimate initialization is desired, the classical Gauss algorithm has been reformulated directly within the CR3BP framework by expanding the three-body equations of motion into generalized ff and gg Taylor series [41](https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/Kyle-T-Alfriend-77961836). When applied to short-arc optical observations and paired with track-to-track association logic across observation gaps of up to 10 days, this cislunar Gauss formulation achieves an overall association accuracy of 99.92%99.92\%, a true positive sensitivity of 92.43%92.43\%, a true negative sensitivity of 100%100\%, a false negative sensitivity of 7.57%7.57\%, and a 0%0\% false positive rate [41](https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/Kyle-T-Alfriend-77961836). When observation arcs span strong three-body nonlinearities where Taylor series truncate poorly, universal angles-only cislunar IOD is executed using Sparse Grid Collocation, Multi-Order Single Shooting differential correction schemes, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), or Nonlinear Programming (NLP) solved directly against full ephemeris dynamics [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01428v1).

To bridge admissible region sampling with continuous trajectory estimation, Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) architectures utilize Extreme Learning Machines (ELM) to model the unknown target trajectory over a short observation window via a single-layer neural network with non-trainable hidden weights w\mathbf{w}, biases b\mathbf{b}, and activation functions σ(wnt+bn)\sigma(\mathbf{w}_n t + \mathbf{b}_n) [14](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/1.A36313). Rather than solving for deterministic output weights β\boldsymbol{\beta}, the PIML framework treats β\boldsymbol{\beta} as a multivariate Gaussian distribution [45](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2025/Machine-Learning-for-SDA-Applications/Badura.pdf). By enforcing the CR3BP equations of motion via Lagrange multipliers with a tunable dynamical acceleration covariance Rd=σd2I3×3\mathbf{R}_d = \sigma_d^2 \mathbf{I}_{3\times 3}, the network converges rapidly from perturbed admissible region states while outputting a complete state covariance matrix σβ=diag(Pβ1/2)\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} = \text{diag}(\mathbf{P}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{1/2}) [14](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/1.A36313).

### Non-Linear Uncertainty Propagation and Adaptive Gaussian Mixture Filtering

Once an initial state estimate is generated, maintaining short-term custody requires propagating state uncertainty across observation gaps and assimilating sparse follow-up measurements [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). In near-Earth regimes, uncertainty is routinely approximated as a single multivariate Gaussian distribution propagated via an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) or Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). In the cislunar domain, however, Monte Carlo analyses confirm that initially Gaussian error ellipsoids rapidly deform into highly asymmetric, curved, and multimodal probability density functions (PDFs) within a matter of days [11](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2021_AAS_FruHowDeMBha.pdf). While Distant Retrograde Orbits (DROs) exhibit comparatively mild curvature during uncertainty propagation, trajectories in Lyapunov or Halo orbits—or transfer orbits undergoing close lunar passage—experience severe non-linear stretching that causes single-Gaussian filters (such as EKF, UKF, or Square-Root UKF) to diverge and lose track custody [11](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2021_AAS_FruHowDeMBha.pdf).

To maintain **uncertainty realism** —which is mandatory for downstream conjunction assessment, object search, and multi-sensor fusion—cislunar tracking architectures represent arbitrary non-Gaussian state PDFs p(x)p(\mathbf{x}) as a weighted Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf):

p(x)=∑m=1NωmN(x;μm,Pm),where ∑m=1Nωm=1p(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{m=1}^{N} \omega_m \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}; \boldsymbol{\mu}_m, \mathbf{P}_m), \quad \text{where } \sum_{m=1}^{N} \omega_m = 1

In a **Hybrid Probabilistic Gaussian Mixture (PGM)**  filter designed for cold-start short-term tracking, the prior p0−(x)p_0^-(\mathbf{x}) can be initialized as an uninformative multivariate uniform distribution spanning the entire cislunar volume (±550,000 km\pm 550,000\text{ km} in position and ±100 km/s\pm 100\text{ km/s} in velocity) [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01428v1). Upon receiving angles-only observations zk\mathbf{z}_k from terrestrial or space-based telescopes, the filter samples the posterior P(x)=pk−(x)p(zk∣x)P(\mathbf{x}) = p_k^-(\mathbf{x})p(\mathbf{z}_k \mid \mathbf{x}) via Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), clusters the resulting chains into a GMM ensemble pi+(x)=∑m=1Niωm∗N(x;μm+,Pm+)p_i^+(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{m=1}^{N_i} \omega_m^* \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}; \boldsymbol{\mu}_m^+, \mathbf{P}_m^+), and executes sequential Bayesian updates [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01428v1). For each mixture component ii at time step nn, the state mean μi\boldsymbol{\mu}_i and covariance Pi\mathbf{P}_i are updated via [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18058):

μi+(n)=μi−(n)+Pi,zx⊤(n)Pi,zz−1(n)(z(n)−Ei[h(X)])\boldsymbol{\mu}_i^+(n) = \boldsymbol{\mu}_i^-(n) + \mathbf{P}_{i,zx}^\top(n)\mathbf{P}_{i,zz}^{-1}(n)\left(\mathbf{z}(n) - \mathbb{E}_i[h(\mathbf{X})]\right)

Pi+(n)=Pi−(n)−Pi,zx⊤(n)Pi,zz−1(n)Pi,zx(n)\mathbf{P}_i^+(n) = \mathbf{P}_i^-(n) - \mathbf{P}_{i,zx}^\top(n)\mathbf{P}_{i,zz}^{-1}(n)\mathbf{P}_{i,zx}(n)

where h(X)h(\mathbf{X}) is the non-linear observation model [9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18058). Although both standard Kalman-Filter PGM (KF-PGM) and Hybrid PGM filters experience a brief initial upward tick in differential entropy during the first one to two measurement steps due to explosive velocity-space uncertainty growth prior to multi-epoch observability, utilizing a broad uniform prior and MCMC clustering during the critical first pass allows the Hybrid PGM filter to assimilate significantly more measurements without divergence [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01428v1). Consequently, while KF-PGM converges from prior velocity uncertainties of 54.047 km/s54.047\text{ km/s} (x˙\dot{x}), 78.993 km/s78.993\text{ km/s} (y˙\dot{y}), and 88.644 km/s88.644\text{ km/s} (z˙\dot{z}) to posterior errors of 0.32216 km/s0.32216\text{ km/s}, 1.2984 km/s1.2984\text{ km/s}, and 1.9414 km/s1.9414\text{ km/s}, the Hybrid PGM filter converges from even larger prior uncertainties of 85.836 km/s85.836\text{ km/s}, 88.528 km/s88.528\text{ km/s}, and 97.135 km/s97.135\text{ km/s} down to posterior errors of 0.01063 km/s0.01063\text{ km/s} in x˙\dot{x}, 0.014715 km/s0.014715\text{ km/s} in y˙\dot{y}, and 0.025009 km/s0.025009\text{ km/s} in z˙\dot{z} [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01428v1).

During continuous tracking across Earth-Moon transfers, NRHOs, and DROs, **Adaptive Gaussian Mixture Models (AGMM)**  dynamically manage component resolution through nonlinearity- and uncertainty-informed moment-matching **splitting**  and **merging**  [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). Component splitting is automatically triggered along directions of high dynamical nonlinearity, during non-linear angles-only measurement updates, and when assimilating **negative information** —such as when a target fails to appear within a sensor's field of view or passes behind the lunar disk or inside an exclusion cone [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). Splitting carves away the zero-likelihood volume of the PDF while preserving non-Gaussian curvature around the boundary of the occlusion [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). To prevent exponential growth in the number of mixture components over time, a cluster-based merging algorithm consolidates closely spaced Gaussian kernels, preserving computational tractability [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf).

Propagating hundreds of sigma points or Monte Carlo particles per GMM component through numerical CR3BP integrators is computationally expensive for large catalogs [23](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393975299_System_Design_and_Analysis_for_Cislunar_Space_Domain_Awareness_Through_Distributed_Sensors). To accelerate short-term uncertainty propagation, **Differential Algebra (DA)**  and **State Transition Tensors (STTs)**  replace floating-point arithmetic in the ordinary differential equation integrator with an algebra of Taylor polynomials expanded around the nominal state xˉ0\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0, alongside geometry-informed Edgeworth series expansions [17](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/D-Scheeres). A single integration of the free CR3BP dynamics in the DA framework generates a high-order polynomial flow map that propagates initial state deviations and analytical moments forward in time algebraically within a local neighborhood of the expansion point [48](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1307424/1/MAPEM01-26.pdf). Similarly, **Arbitrary Polynomial Chaos (aPC)**  and **Multi-Element Polynomial Chaos Expansions (ME-PCE)**  project the stochastic state onto orthogonal polynomial bases, yielding higher accuracy and computational efficiency than standard Monte Carlo sampling when propagating short-arc admissible region uncertainties [46](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/6.2024-0203).

### Maneuver Detection, Jump Markov Filtering, and Reachability Search

Detecting and tracking an unknown maneuver in cislunar space is complicated by the fact that natural three-body accelerations vary rapidly with position, masking small Δv\Delta v burns [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D). To maintain custody through unannounced thrusting phases—such as trans-lunar injection, perilune burns, or orbit insertion along an Artemis I-like trajectory—cislunar target motion is modeled as a discrete-time non-linear **Jump Markov System (JMS)**  [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). The target's operational modality τk∈{1,…,M}\tau_k \in \{1, \dots, M\} (representing ballistic flight versus various active thrusting directions, durations, and magnitudes w(t,τ)\mathbf{w}(t, \tau)) evolves according to a homogeneous Markov chain governed by mode transition probabilities [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf):

πij=Pr⁡(τk=j∣τk−1=i)\pi_{ij} = \Pr(\tau_k = j \mid \tau_{k-1} = i)

The **Adaptive Gaussian Mixture Interacting Multiple Model (AGMIMM)**  filter recursively estimates the joint state-and-modality distribution pk(xk,τk∣Z0:k)p_k(\mathbf{x}_k, \tau_k \mid \mathbf{Z}_{0:k}) conditioned on multi-sensor angles-only measurement histories Z0:k\mathbf{Z}_{0:k} [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). As mode hypotheses branch at each epoch, the filter applies multi-criterion Gaussian mixture splitting to capture non-linear maneuver plumes while executing cluster-based merging across mode histories to prevent combinatorial explosion [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf).

Because the Jacobi constant C(r,v)=2U(r)−v2C(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v}) = 2U(\mathbf{r}) - v^2 is strictly invariant along any ballistic trajectory in the CR3BP, it serves as a highly sensitive energy-based invariant for maneuver detection and pattern-of-life violation analysis [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf). By computing the **Jacobi constant variance**  associated with each Gaussian mixture component (ωm,μm,Pm)(\omega_m, \boldsymbol{\mu}_m, \mathbf{P}_m) across sequential optical or passive RF tracks, operators can immediately detect statistically significant deviations from ballistic energy conservation [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). Similarly, when attempting to correlate two uncorrelated optical tracklets separated by a multi-day gap, a **minimum-energy correlation metric**  formulated in the three-body framework quantifies the minimum energy expenditure required to bridge the two boundary states, cleanly discriminating between natural ballistic motion, maneuvering behavior, and unrelated space objects [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf).

When a target executes a large maneuver during a long observation gap and breaks track custody, blindly scanning the surrounding cislunar volume is infeasible [15](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/6.2022-0853). Instead, **Directional Reachability Analysis**  computes the three-dimensional position Reachable Set (RS) over a specified time horizon for a bounded maximum Δv\Delta v or low-thrust acceleration budget [15](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/6.2022-0853). By solving a General Independent Parameter Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) partial differential equation or propagating high-order State Transition Tensors (STTs), the reachable boundary volume is rapidly generated, even for highly sensitive regimes such as Near-Rectilinear Halo Orbits (NRHOs) or trajectories passing near the lunar singularity [15](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/6.2022-0853). Furthermore, for monitoring small-maneuver Earth-return or lunar-transfer trajectories from ground-based sensors, projecting the dynamical flow onto **Poincaré maps**  isolates the specific phase-space intersections where a maneuvering target must cross, drastically contracting the sky search area required to re-acquire custody [37](https://amostech.space/track/cislunar-sda/).

### Non-Resolved Object Characterization via Light Curve Inversion

Because cislunar targets at ranges of 100,000 to 400,000 km100,000\text{ to }400,000\text{ km} appear as unresolved point sources even in 20 cm20\text{ cm} space-based cameras or large terrestrial telescopes, physical characterization and intent inference rely on **temporally resolved photometry (light curves)** , multi-spectral unmixing, event camera photometry, and spectropolarimetry [21](https://breakingdefense.com/tag/cislunar-highway-patrol-system-chps/). For rotating rocket bodies, space debris, or spin-stabilized spacecraft, the apparent (synodic) rotation period is extracted using Fourier analysis or Phase Dispersion Minimization [58](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10550v1). Once folded at the synodic period, the light curve is fitted with a Fourier series [58](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10550v1). Feeding the single-period Fourier series coefficients into a 3-hidden-layer (256 neurons per layer) Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) trained on large photometric repositories—such as the Russian Mini-MegaTORTORA (MMT) database (15,82515,825 objects and 534,604534,604 tracks as of January 2025) and the Comenius University Space Debris Light Curve Database (SDLCD, containing 2,2242,224 light curves of 791791 objects and dedicating >20 minutes>20\text{ minutes} per track to capture slowly rotating high-altitude objects)—classifies targets into distinct structural categories and detects rotational anomalies, such as slow angular deceleration in H-2A rocket bodies (0.1∘/s0.1^\circ/\text{s} over two months) or sudden angular acceleration jumps in CZ-3B upper stages from 2.7∘/s2.7^\circ/\text{s} to 3.9∘/s3.9^\circ/\text{s} [58](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10550v1). In high-eccentricity cislunar capture scenarios, such as the temporarily captured object 2020 SO (modeled as a 2,274.3 kg2,274.3\text{ kg}, 12.68×3.05 m12.68 \times 3.05\text{ m} cylindrical shell approaching within 50,000 km50,000\text{ km} of Earth), running 35,00035,000-iteration Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) photometric inversions reliably bounds posterior attitude and shape distributions [60](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2021/Poster/Campbell.pdf).

For active three-axis stabilized or maneuvering cislunar spacecraft, light curve inversion reconstructs the full 3D attitude quaternion qk\mathbf{q}_k and angular velocity vector [18](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16165). Standard Unscented Kalman Filters (UKFs) and Rao-Blackwellized particle filters that rely on rigid facet-BRDF measurement equations degrade severely when the target's true diffuse reflectance ρtrue\rho_{\text{true}} or specular Phong exponent differs from assumed filter priors (such as a fixed ρukf=0.5\rho_{\text{ukf}} = 0.5) [18](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16165). To overcome unknown surface properties, the **Gaussian Process Unscented Kalman Filter (GPUKF)**  replaces the parametric BRDF observation equation with a non-parametric Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) model [18](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16165). By conditioning the GPR on both the current estimated attitude qk\mathbf{q}_k and a rolling average of past apparent magnitudes mapp,past,km_{\text{app,past},k}, the filter implicitly learns the target's true surface reflectance in real time [18](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16165). Furthermore, when tracking multi-surface spacecraft exhibiting transient specular glints, an adaptive multi-surface attitude filter incorporating an interacting mixing step increases attitude convergence rates from 10%10\% (single-surface filter) and 40%40\% (unmixed filter) up to 73%73\% across Monte Carlo trials with initial attitude errors of up to 80∘80^\circ [57](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245433715_Attitude_Determination_from_Light_Curves). Coupling these photometric attitude estimators with orbital state filters via a **Multiplicative Unscented Kalman Filter (MUKF)**  enables unified orbit-attitude estimation, allowing operators to detect attitude slew maneuvers and infer active control modes before an orbital burn is executed [56](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/1.G002223).

### Closed-Loop Predictive Sensor Tasking

In short-term cislunar monitoring campaigns, the number of cataloged and newly discovered targets surpasses the number of available terrestrial and space-based observers, and each optical sensor is constrained by slewing rates, integration times, and exclusion cones [28](https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/mec_aereng_facwork/5894/). Consequently, sensor pointing directions cannot be scheduled statically; they must be governed by a closed-loop **receding-horizon predictive sensor tasking**  framework that adapts to dynamic demand uncertainty [50](https://research.tudelft.nl/files/210930636/1-s2.0-S0376042124000435-main.pdf). Formally, for a network of NsN_s sensors over a receding discrete look-ahead window [k:k+n][k : k+n], the sensor tasking controller determines the optimal control vector u∗={u1,u2,…,uNs}\mathbf{u}^* = \{u_1, u_2, \dots, u_{N_s}\} by maximizing a scalar information-theoretic objective function E(u)E(\mathbf{u}) [50](https://research.tudelft.nl/files/210930636/1-s2.0-S0376042124000435-main.pdf):

u∗=arg⁡max⁡u∈UE(u∣pk(xk∣Z0:k))\mathbf{u}^* = \arg\max_{\mathbf{u} \in \mathcal{U}} E\left(\mathbf{u} \mid p_k(\mathbf{x}_k \mid \mathbf{Z}_{0:k})\right)

The primary information-theoretic metric driving cislunar tracking is **Mutual Information (MI) Gain** , which quantifies the expected reduction in Shannon entropy (uncertainty) of the target state distribution H(X)H(\mathbf{X}) conditioned on the predicted measurement vector Z\mathbf{Z} over the receding horizon [61](https://etda.libraries.psu.edu/catalog/27824vpd5086):

I(X;Z)=H(X)−H(X∣Z)=∫∫p(x,z)log⁡(p(x,z)p(x)p(z))dx dzI(\mathbf{X}; \mathbf{Z}) = H(\mathbf{X}) - H(\mathbf{X} \mid \mathbf{Z}) = \int \int p(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z}) \log \left(\frac{p(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z})}{p(\mathbf{x})p(\mathbf{z})}\right) d\mathbf{x}\,d\mathbf{z}

Unlike trace-of-covariance metrics, Mutual Information evaluates the full non-Gaussian GMM density, naturally prioritizing targets whose probability distributions are splitting or stretching along unstable CR3BP manifolds [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). Crucially, because Mutual Information satisfies the mathematical property of **submodularity**  (diminishing marginal returns as additional sensors observe the same target), multi-sensor scheduling across distributed cislunar constellations can be solved using **distributed game-theoretic algorithms**  that guarantee tractable, performance-bounded solutions with high scalability and robustness [63](https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/bitstreams/87d230ae-820c-4c3c-a746-10a4700bedaf/download).

For rapid onboard evaluation or large-scale constellation planning, **Fisher Information Gain (FIG)**  and analytical cislunar information gain formulations measure the curvature of the log-likelihood function to maximize precision along the least-observable state directions, combining Fisher information with entropic measures and local Lyapunov exponents to instruct sensors to observe targets immediately prior to perilune passage or Lagrange-point manifold bifurcations [5](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2025/Cislunar_SDA/Evans.pdf). Additionally, when tracking non-cooperative or evasive targets near L1L_1 and L2L_2, sensor scheduling can be paired with a limited-Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) observation surface, judicial evidential reasoning for anomaly detection, or formulated as a **Bayesian Game**  [37](https://amostech.space/track/cislunar-sda/). In a Bayesian game framework, the sensor network optimizes its observation schedule against an intelligent target executing minimum-Δv\Delta v transfers, maximizing detection likelihood across coupled space-based and lunar ground-based optical observers even under adversarial maneuvering [28](https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/mec_aereng_facwork/5894/).

## Strategic Synthesis and Architectural Recommendations

Conducting comprehensive, accurate situational awareness across the cislunar volume while ensuring high-effectiveness short-term tracking requires uniting multi-phenomenology sensor placement with non-Gaussian, three-body-native estimation theory [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). Legacy near-Earth paradigms that treat sensor architecture design, orbit determination, and catalog tasking as decoupled, linear two-body processes cannot survive the chaotic dynamics and severe radiometric exclusions of the Earth-Moon corridor [6](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/RV/A%20Primer%20on%20Cislunar%20Space_Dist%20A_PA2021-1271.pdf?ver=vs6e0sE4PuJ51QC-15DEfg%3D%3D).

To operationalize a resilient cislunar SDA enterprise, architectural implementation must first deploy a hybrid, geometry-diverse Cislunar Custody Chain [22](https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/221205-apl-cislunar-traffic-management). Anchoring the near-Earth entry boundary with the trilateral 27-antenna DARC X-band radar network guarantees 24/7 all-weather detection of objects departing the GEO belt [30](https://migflug.com/afterburner/darc-deep-space-advanced-radar-texas-lake-kickapoo-2026/). In deep cislunar space, pairing maneuverable Lagrange-point optical sentinels—such as AFRL's *Oracle*  and *D2S2*  platforms operating in L1/L2L_1/L_2 and resonant periodic orbits—with lunar-surface optical telescopes and out-of-plane pole-sitters (1.0 to 2.5 Mkm1.0\text{ to }2.5\text{ Mkm}) provides the orthogonal lines of sight required to eliminate the lunar albedo exclusion zone [2](https://www.kbr.com/en/document/16811). Simultaneously, integrating 12 m12\text{ m} long-baseline passive RF TDOA/FDOA interferometric arrays ensures uninterrupted tracking of emitting spacecraft whenever trajectories cross solar or lunar optical exclusion cones [10](https://conference.sdo.esoc.esa.int/proceedings/sdc9/paper/72/SDC9-paper72.pdf).

On the algorithmic front, operational centers must standardize on three-body-native Initial Orbit Determination and adaptive mixture estimation [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf). Replacing Keplerian Gauss/Laplace initializers with CR3BP Taylor-series Gauss algorithms, Physics-Informed Extreme Learning Machines, and Sensor/Manifold-Constrained Probabilistic Admissible Regions pruned via the Jacobi constant eliminates initial track divergence [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf). Transitioning cataloging filters from single-Gaussian EKFs and UKFs to Hybrid and Adaptive Gaussian Mixture Interacting Multiple Model (AGMIMM) frameworks—accelerated via Differential Algebra and State Transition Tensors and explicitly assimilating negative information from field-of-view non-detections and lunar occultations—preserves uncertainty realism across multi-day observation gaps [12](http://keithlegrand.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Iannamorelli-2024-Adaptive-Gaussian-Mixture-Filtering-for-Multi-Sensor-Maneuvering-Cislunar-Space-Object-Tracking.pdf).

Finally, short-term tactical responsiveness depends on institutionalizing invariant-based maneuver monitoring, non-parametric photometry, and closed-loop submodular sensor tasking [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf). Continuously monitoring Jacobi constant variance and three-body minimum-energy correlation metrics across all tracked mixture components provides automated tipping-and-cueing of unannounced orbital burns, while STT/HJB directional reachability sets and Poincaré surface-of-section maps bound recovery searches when custody is disrupted [8](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a0b8bb71-1aa8-4df8-817d-89b990c564e7/PhD_Thesis_Alessia_De-Riz.pdf). Processing unresolved optical tracks through Gaussian Process Unscented Kalman Filters (GPUKF) and multi-surface mixing estimators extracts 3-axis attitude and detects active control mode shifts prior to thruster ignition [18](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16165). By coupling this AGMIMM state estimation engine directly to a receding-horizon sensor scheduler optimizing Mutual Information and Fisher Information Gain via distributed game-theoretic optimization and onboard quantized neural network image processing, cislunar observer constellations can autonomously coordinate pointing vectors in real time—preemptively tracking unstable manifold departures and guaranteeing custody across persistent detection corridors [19](https://hanspeterschaub.info/Papers/grads/MichaelKlonowski.pdf).
