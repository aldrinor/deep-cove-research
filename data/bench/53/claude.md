# How the World’s Wealthiest Governments Invest: Sovereign Wealth Funds and State Investors in 2026

Sovereign wealth funds now manage a record of roughly $15 trillion, and they are sending a growing share of it into US technology, AI infrastructure and private markets. The money is split between two models. The Norwegian and Singaporean tradition is transparent, return-seeking and globally diversified. The Gulf and emerging-market “strategic” funds are increasingly used as tools of industrial policy and geopolitics. The second model is growing faster, and the first is under political strain.

## TL;DR

- **Scale and concentration:**  Global SWF counted sovereign wealth fund assets above US$15 trillion for the first time in December 2025 ($15.2tn), within US$60tn held by all state-owned investors. The top 15 funds hold 81% of SWF assets, per Global SWF’s 2026 Annual Report. Norway’s GPFG (about $2.3tn) is the largest, followed by China’s SAFE Investment Company and CIC, Singapore’s GIC, ADIA, KIA and PIF. Asia-Pacific and the Middle East together hold about 79% of the total.
- **Where the money goes:**  In 2025, 47% ($132bn) of all sovereign investment went to the United States. Capital concentrated in AI, data centers, digital infrastructure and private credit, while emerging markets (including China and India) fell to 16% of flows. Gulf funds (the “Gulf 7”) deployed $119bn in 2025 and a record $53.9bn across 108 transactions in the first half of 2026, per Global SWF’s 2026 GSR Scoreboard, despite the Iran war and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.
- **The tension to watch:**  Strategic and domestic mandates (PIF, Danantara, QIA’s new Doha Investment unit, the US government’s ad-hoc equity stakes) are blurring the line between investing and industrial policy. Even the governance benchmark, Norway, suspended its ethics-based exclusions in November 2025 and is now weighing whether to allow defense and nuclear-weapons makers. [irishtimes](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/11/05/norway-suspends-21tn-oil-funds-ethics-rules-to-avoid-selling-big-tech-stakes/) Returns are solid but uneven: GIC’s 20-year real return fell to 3.4%, while Australia’s Future Fund returned 14.8% and NBIM posted a record first half of 2026. [capitalbrief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/future-fund-delivers-annual-returns-of-148-a5e733dc-1cf0-467f-b440-e7d8f460f228/)

## Key Findings

1. **The industry is bigger and more concentrated than ever.**  Global SWF’s year-end 2025 report puts SWF assets at $15.2tn, part of $60tn across sovereign funds, public pension funds and central banks, and projects about $80tn for all state-owned investors by 2030. [amazonaws](https://argaamplus.s3.amazonaws.com/3ffe681b-beab-49ba-9c30-bfe8ca8bd0bb.pdf) The IE University/ICEX Sovereign Wealth Funds Report 2026 independently counts 109 funds with $15.1tn in April 2026, against 104 funds and $13.2tn in its previous edition, an increase of almost $2tn, or 14%, in eighteen months. Global SWF’s September 2026 ranking puts the top 50 at about $15.7tn. [Substack](https://marianogrosmanipo.substack.com/p/the-50-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds)
2. **Market beta, not oil, is now the main growth engine.**  Global SWF says the 2025 equity rally “is way more important than oil prices at this point.” The S&P Global 1200 rose 21.5% in 2025, while Brent averaged $68, against $80 in 2024. [amazonaws](https://argaamplus.s3.amazonaws.com/3ffe681b-beab-49ba-9c30-bfe8ca8bd0bb.pdf) This favors equity-heavy funds (NBIM, GIC, the Future Fund) over those dependent on fresh hydrocarbon inflows.
3. **The US is the overwhelming destination.**  Sovereign investors put $132bn, or 47% of all deals, into the US in 2025. That figure excludes their $2.2tn of holdings in Magnificent 7 stocks (as of 30 September 2025). Emerging-market flows fell 26%. [amazonaws](https://argaamplus.s3.amazonaws.com/3ffe681b-beab-49ba-9c30-bfe8ca8bd0bb.pdf)
4. **AI is the dominant theme, and the Gulf is its most aggressive financier.**  Abu Dhabi’s MGX closed a $49bn AI fund, helped buy 100% of Aligned Data Centers from Macquarie-managed funds at an enterprise value of approximately $40bn (closed 21 July 2026, per the consortium’s release), and sits in the cap tables of OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI. QIA, GIC, Temasek and Mubadala are repeat backers of frontier labs and AI infrastructure. [temasek +2](https://www.temasek.com.sg/en/news-and-resources/news-room/speeches/2026/temasek-review-2026-media-briefing)
5. **Strategic and development funds are where governance risk is rising.**  PIF’s AUM fell for the first time this decade in 2025 (to about SAR 3.4tn / $906bn) and 76% of it is domestic. [asiaasset](https://www.asiaasset.com/sovereign-wealth-funds/saudi-arabias-pif-assets-under-management-fell-38-billion-riyals-in-2025/) Indonesia’s Danantara reports a $900bn figure that is roughly four times Global SWF’s estimate, [Substack](https://marianogrosmanipo.substack.com/p/the-50-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds) and it raises money through “Patriot Bonds” that come with legal protections for buyers. [medium](https://medium.com/@zaidanhabibikhalilullah/addressing-regulatory-vulnerabilities-in-the-protection-of-danantara-special-bond-investor-funds-77bff1e4b3ff)[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danantara) The US has invested roughly $26.7bn across 30 equity or quasi-equity deals without creating a formal fund, according to Fortune.
6. **Governance scores are flat; sustainability and resilience are rising.**  Global SWF’s 2026 GSR Scoreboard (200 funds, $34.0tn) shows an average score of 60%. Governance and transparency did not improve. [bernama](https://www.bernama.com/misc/rss/news.php?id=2575335) Nine funds scored 100%: NBIM, Temasek, Future Fund, NZ Super, NSIA, La Caisse, BCI, OTPP and Rest. [beehiiv](https://globalswf.beehiiv.com/p/global-swf-2026-gsr-scoreboard) None is a Gulf fund.

**Sources of capital.**  There are four main sources, and they shape behavior:

- **Commodity revenue:**  Norway, ADIA, KIA, QIA, Alaska. These are long-horizon savings funds with global portfolios.
- **FX reserves and trade surpluses:**  SAFE, CIC, GIC, KIC, HKMA. These lean toward liquidity, though CIC and GIC have pushed hard into alternatives.
- **Transferred state assets and privatizations:**  Temasek, PIF, Türkiye Wealth Fund, Danantara, ICD. These start life as holding companies with heavy domestic concentration.
- **Fiscal surpluses and budget allocations:**  Australia’s Future Fund, the Ireland funds, NZ Super.

## 2. Asset Allocation and Investment Models

Four models stand out.

**Model A: the transparent near-index fund (Norway).**  NBIM runs the GPFG close to a Ministry of Finance benchmark. Equities were 72.1% and fixed income 25.8% at mid-2026, with only 2.1% in unlisted real estate and renewable infrastructure. [nbim](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/the-press/press-releases/2026/record-high-krone-return-in-the-first-half-of-the-year/) The mandate caps those at 7% and 2%, and it allows no private equity or private credit. [Substack](https://marianogrosmanipo.substack.com/p/the-50-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds) Withdrawals are governed by Norway’s fiscal rule, under which the budget may draw roughly the fund’s expected real return (3%). Active management adds small increments: +0.22 percentage points against the benchmark in the first half of 2026. [nbim](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/reports/2026/half-year-report-2026/) The model is cheap, liquid and fully transparent, but it is now heavily exposed to US mega-cap technology through the index. In Q1 2026 the fund lost 1.89% as large US tech stocks fell. [nbim](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/reports/2026/half-year-report-2026/web-report-half-year-report-2026/)[marketsgroup](https://www.marketsgroup.org/news/nbim-reports-negative-first-quarter-return-as-stocks-weigh-on-results)

**Model B: the long-horizon, diversified reserve manager (GIC, ADIA, CIC, KIC).**  These funds hold substantial private-market and alternative books and judge themselves over 20–30 years.

- **GIC**  moved on 1 April 2026 to a new framework. Its strategic portfolio now uses three return-driver groups (equities, fixed income, real assets) instead of six asset classes, and it no longer sets separate ranges for public and private equity. The aim is more flexibility amid geopolitical fragmentation and AI disruption. [pressreader +2](https://www.pressreader.com/singapore/the-business-times/20260724/281526527836357)
- **ADIA**  sets its allocation as ranges. Its 2025 Review raised private equity to 15–20% (from 12–17%) and financial alternatives (hedge funds) to 7–12% (from 5–10%), and cut real estate to 2–7% (from 5–10%). [hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/adia-raises-hedge-fund-and-pe-allocation-targets/) ADIA is often described as heavily externally managed, but its latest figures show 63% managed internally and 37% externally. [ua](https://ua.news/en/finansi/fond-adia-v-oae-zberezhe-tekhnologiyi-sered-golovnikh-napriamiv-investitsii) That model is changing.
- **CIC**  puts 48.5% of its overseas portfolio in alternatives and outsources 62.5% to external managers. [caproasia](https://www.caproasia.com/2025/12/11/china-sovereign-wealth-fund-china-investment-corporation-cic-reports-1-5-trillion-assets-1-3-trillion-net-assets-in-2024-and-6-9-annualized-return-in-last-10-years-2015-to-2024-global-invest/)

**Model C: the active owner (Temasek, Mubadala).**  Temasek owns concentrated, often controlling stakes. Singapore-headquartered portfolio companies such as Singapore Airlines, DBS and Singtel make up 43% of its portfolio, and it does not trade those stakes. [temasek](https://www.temasek.com.sg/en/news-and-resources/news-room/speeches/2026/temasek-review-2026-media-briefing)[temasek](https://www.temasek.com.sg/en/news-and-resources/news-room/speeches/2026/ceo-address-video-transcript-temasek-review-2026-media-briefing) About half of the portfolio is unlisted. [Substack](https://marianogrosmanipo.substack.com/p/the-50-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds) Temasek reorganized into three segments (Singapore portfolio companies, global direct investments, and partnerships/funds/asset managers). Global SWF flagged this as a 2026 “restructuring.” [amazonaws](https://argaamplus.s3.amazonaws.com/3ffe681b-beab-49ba-9c30-bfe8ca8bd0bb.pdf)[temasek](https://www.temasek.com.sg/en/news-and-resources/news-room/speeches/2026/temasek-review-2026-media-briefing) From FY2026 Temasek reports performance on a mark-to-market basis, and it targets increasing private credit from 2% to 5% of the portfolio by 2031. [temasekreview](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)[Substack](https://marianogrosmanipo.substack.com/p/the-50-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds) Mubadala combines direct private investing (42% private) with an asset-management business: Mubadala Capital manages more than $430bn for clients after its C$12.1bn take-private of CI Financial. [agbi](https://www.agbi.com/finance/2026/04/mubadala-assets-under-management-rise-to-385bn/)[zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/strong-performance-by-uae-portfolio-drives-mubadalas-growth-in-2025-349997)

**Model D: the strategic or development fund (PIF, Danantara, Türkiye Wealth Fund, QIA’s domestic arm, Ireland’s ISIF, the UK NWF).**  Here returns are only one objective. PIF reports by “pools”: Saudi equity holdings 33%, Saudi sector development 29%, international strategic investments 10%. It publishes no listed/unlisted split. [Substack](https://marianogrosmanipo.substack.com/p/the-50-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds) Its 2026–2030 strategy, approved in April 2026, reorganizes the fund into a “Vision Portfolio” of six domestic economic ecosystems and a “Strategic Portfolio” that manages flagship assets such as Ma’aden and ACWA Power. It emphasizes “investment efficiency” after a decade of giga-project spending. [spa](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2562977) The Vision 2030 annual report nonetheless raised PIF’s AUM target to SAR 10 trillion by 2030. [maaal](https://maaal.com/en/news/details/vision-2030-annual-report/) That target is roughly triple the current size and should be treated as aspirational.

**Hybrid: the Future Fund.**  Australia’s fund uses a total-portfolio approach. It has low bond exposure, large private equity and infrastructure books, and 14.9% in hedge-fund-style alternatives. [Substack](https://marianogrosmanipo.substack.com/p/the-50-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds) A 2024 mandate update added “national priorities” such as energy transition and housing, with A$3.5bn committed to new direct investments since then. Its returns have risen since the change (12.2% in FY2025, 14.8% in FY2026). [treasury](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/jim-chalmers-2022/media-releases/future-funds-strong-returns-continue-under-modernised) Politicians have cited this as vindication, but strong markets explain most of it. [capitalbrief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/future-fund-tops-250bn-after-double-digit-annual-return-87e0249b-500e-4d6e-b048-fe288531454c/)

**Industry-wide allocation trends.**  The 2026 Invesco Global Sovereign Asset Management Study surveyed 144 institutions (90 SWFs, 54 central banks, $29tn). [invesco](https://www.invesco.com/au/en/insights/media/2026/sovereign-investors-prioritise-resilience-diversification-amid-heightened-global-uncertainty.html)[rankiapro](https://rankiapro.com/en/insights/sovereign-investors-shield-their-portfolios-against-a-more-uncertain-world-and-prioritize-innovation-and-diversification/) It finds:

- **Resilience:**  54% of SWFs and 71% of central banks say resilience now matters as much as return. [invesco +2](https://www.invesco.com/au/en/insights/media/2026/sovereign-investors-prioritise-resilience-diversification-amid-heightened-global-uncertainty.html)
- **Equities:**  net allocation intentions show “a significant turn against equities,” with capital moving toward infrastructure (the fastest-growing alternative asset class over five years) and private credit. [invesco +2](https://www.invesco.com/au/en/insights/media/2026/sovereign-investors-prioritise-resilience-diversification-amid-heightened-global-uncertainty.html)
- **Horizons:**  about 40% of SWFs admit their real investment horizon is shorter than the one they state. [invesco](https://www.invesco.com/igsams/en/home/insights/how-sovereign-investors-are-rethinking-long-term-strategies-in-a-less-supportive-world.html)
- **ETFs:**  39% now use ETFs. [invesco](https://www.invesco.com/au/en/insights/media/2026/sovereign-investors-prioritise-resilience-diversification-amid-heightened-global-uncertainty.html)
- **AI risk:**  52% name market concentration as the main portfolio risk of AI-related investing. [invesco](https://www.invesco.com/au/en/insights/media/2026/sovereign-investors-prioritise-resilience-diversification-amid-heightened-global-uncertainty.html)

Global SWF finds that funds using a total portfolio approach (TPA) outperformed by about 75bps, [amazonaws](https://argaamplus.s3.amazonaws.com/3ffe681b-beab-49ba-9c30-bfe8ca8bd0bb.pdf) which helps explain why GIC and others are moving toward that framework.

**Central banks are a different category.**  FX reserve managers such as SAFE’s core reserves and most central banks prioritize liquidity and capital preservation in high-grade sovereign bonds and, increasingly, gold. The Invesco study finds central banks adding gold amid dollar concerns. [invesco](https://www.invesco.com/content/dam/invesco/apac/en/pdf/insights/2026/june/invesco-global-sovereign-asset-management-study-2026.pdf) When reserves exceed what liquidity needs require, governments move the surplus into separate return-seeking vehicles: CIC, GIC, KIC, the HKMA’s Long-Term Growth Portfolio. That is the conceptual line between reserve management and SWF investing. The HKMA Exchange Fund straddles it. It returned 8.0% (HK$331.0bn) in 2025 on HK$4.15tn of assets, [universalassetowners](https://www.universalassetowners.com/registry/institution/hkma-exchange-fund/)[info](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202601/28/P2026012800478.htm) but its main purpose is backing the currency board.

## 4. Governance and Transparency

**Frameworks.**  The Santiago Principles (24 Generally Accepted Principles and Practices, 2008) and the IFSWF that oversees them remain the baseline. ADIA began publishing its annual Review only after signing them in 2009. [agbi](https://www.agbi.com/banking-finance/2026/09/adia-at-50-still-evolving-after-all-these-years/) Global SWF’s GSR Scoreboard has become the main comparative yardstick: 25 indicators across governance, sustainability and resilience, scoring 200 funds. [blogspot](https://lcbackerblog.blogspot.com/2026/06/global-swf-2026-gsr-scoreboard-best.html)[amazonaws](https://global-swf.s3.amazonaws.com/file-uploads/SzSEQhyu4OgLMS85F4Gr87sO4iJj74GgY0uYLybA.pdf) The SWFI’s Linaburg-Maduell Transparency Index is an older 10-point measure.

**Transparency comparison.**

| Tier | Funds | What they disclose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full transparency (GSR 100%) | NBIM, Temasek, Future Fund, NZ Super, NSIA | AUM, holdings or segment detail, returns vs. benchmark, costs |
| Partial | GIC, ADIA, CIC, KIC, PIF, Mubadala, TWF (GSR 84%) [tvf](https://www.tvf.com.tr/en/contact/disclosures/2026/twf-among-the-state-owned-investment-funds-with-the-greatest-improvement-in-gsr-score-1783688129302) | GIC: 20-year returns and allocation, no AUM or one-year return; ADIA: ranges and long-run returns, no AUM; PIF: AUM, pools, TSR |

Of the 14 Gulf funds in Global SWF’s top 50, only Mubadala publishes its private-markets share. [Substack](https://marianogrosmanipo.substack.com/p/the-50-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds)

**Mandates and withdrawal rules.**  These vary from strict to discretionary:

- **Norway:**  the fiscal rule caps structural spending at about the fund’s expected real return.
- **Australia:**  the Future Fund cannot be drawn down before 2032–33. [asiaasset](https://www.asiaasset.com/sovereign-wealth-funds/australias-future-fund-posts-14-8-annual-return-hits-record-a289-7-billion/)
- **Kuwait:**  the Future Generations Fund receives a legislated share of revenue.
- **Gulf funds in wartime:**  Global SWF warns that KIA and QIA may be called on to transfer money to budgets hit by the Hormuz disruption. [semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/05/2026/gulf-swfs-on-track-for-major-investments-despite-war) Stabilization roles can override savings mandates in a crisis.

**Norway’s ethics crisis.**  This is the most important governance development of the period.

- **The trigger:**  in November 2025, after US scrutiny of the fund’s divestment from Caterpillar over Gaza and West Bank links, and with the Council on Ethics reviewing Amazon, Microsoft and Alphabet, parliament suspended the Council’s recommendations. [irishtimes +2](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/11/05/norway-suspends-21tn-oil-funds-ethics-rules-to-avoid-selling-big-tech-stakes/)
- **Temporary guidelines:**  Norges Bank may not decide new exclusions or observations, only revoke old ones. It revoked two exclusions in the first half of 2026. [nbim](https://www.nbim.no/en/responsible-investment/exclusion-of-companies/)
- **The review:**  the Gjedrem Committee reported in mid-October 2026. It recommended keeping the Council on Ethics but limiting it to analysis, without the power to recommend exclusions. It also recommended reconsidering the nuclear-weapons ban that excludes 14 companies, including Lockheed Martin, Boeing, Airbus, BAE Systems and Northrop Grumman. [CNBC +2](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/16/norway-sovereign-wealth-fund-defense-investment.html)
- **Reaction:**  the proposals drew sharp opposition from parties on the left. The Progress Party supports letting the fund decide for itself. A parliamentary decision is expected in 2027. [kucoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/norway-s-2-3t-wealth-fund-may-reinvest-in-defense-firms)[newsinenglish](https://www.newsinenglish.no/2026/10/15/oil-fund-ethics-review-stirs-up-a-storm/)

The lesson is clear: the world’s model ethical investor retreated once ethics collided with its largest holdings and with US diplomatic pressure.

**Criticisms and risks.**

- **Political direction and opacity:**  the US approach is the starkest example. Executive Order 14196 (February 2025) ordered a plan for a US SWF. No formal fund exists, and Commerce Secretary Lutnick has said none is being created. [cato](https://www.cato.org/blog/shakedowns-sovereign-wealth-fund)[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Plan_for_Establishing_a_United_States_Sovereign_Wealth_Fund_(Executive_Order_14196)) Instead the administration has invested roughly $26.7bn across 30 equity or quasi-equity deals, according to Fortune. The largest is a 9.9% Intel stake (433.3 million shares at $20.47, tied to $8.9bn in CHIPS and other funding), which Fortune values at about $42bn. None of this appears in budget documents. Officials disagree on structure: Semafor reported Bessent favoring “Trump Accounts” and Lutnick a sovereign fund. On 1 October 2026 Trump told TIME he “might” take equity stakes in AI labs. [compliancehub](https://compliancehub.wiki/nationalization-but-maybe-10-government-stake-openai-anthropic-would-mean/)
- **Domestic-investment pressure and quasi-fiscal risk:**  Danantara’s Patriot Bonds carry a 2% coupon; a circulated list, never confirmed by Danantara, identified 46 conglomerates buying a combined Rp51.75tn (Tempo), and a second Rp12.62tn tranche at 2% followed in November 2025 (Asia Asset Management). Law 4/2026 (Article 50A) grants buyers protection from criminal, tax and civil action without checking where the funds came from. Civil-society groups have written to the FATF, Mirae Asset flagged governance concerns, and Fitch cut Indonesia’s outlook to negative, partly citing Danantara.
- **Concentration:**  state-owned investors hold $2.2tn of Magnificent 7 stocks. [amazonaws](https://argaamplus.s3.amazonaws.com/3ffe681b-beab-49ba-9c30-bfe8ca8bd0bb.pdf) Index-trackers like NBIM have no choice, and active Gulf funds are piling into the same AI names.
- **Scandal legacy:**  Global SWF chose Malaysia as its 2025 “Region of the Year” for finally “turning the 1MDB page,” [amazonaws](https://argaamplus.s3.amazonaws.com/3ffe681b-beab-49ba-9c30-bfe8ca8bd0bb.pdf) a reminder of how badly state funds can fail.

## 7. Key Takeaways and What to Watch

1. **Sovereign capital is a leveraged bet on US tech.**  It comes passively through index weights (NBIM) and actively through AI deals (MGX, QIA, Temasek, GIC). With 47% of 2025 deal flow and $2.2tn in Magnificent 7 stocks, a sustained AI-valuation correction would hit sovereign balance sheets at the same time. Watch whether “selectivity” talk (QIA, ADIA, Invesco’s concentration warnings) turns into actual reallocation toward infrastructure and private credit.
2. **The Gulf is now the marginal price-setter in global private markets.**  It has kept that role through a war. Its funds are using volatility to co-invest at scale. [enterpriseam](https://enterpriseam.com/ksa/2026/07/02/gulf-swfs-deployed-a-record-usd-53-9-bn-in-1h-2026-showing-no-signs-of-slowing-despite-the-war/) Watch for budget draws from KIA and QIA if Hormuz revenues stay impaired, and for further domestic consolidation (QIA’s Doha Investment, PIF’s ecosystems).
3. **The “strategic fund” model is spreading faster than its governance.**  New vehicles often come from deficit countries (Indonesia, the US, the UK, Canada, Spain). [beehiiv](https://globalswf.beehiiv.com/p/global-swf-2026-gsr-scoreboard) Global SWF notes that governance and transparency scores did not improve in 2026, even as sustainability and resilience did. [blogspot](https://lcbackerblog.blogspot.com/2026/06/global-swf-2026-gsr-scoreboard-best.html) Danantara’s Patriot Bonds and the off-budget US stakes are the clearest warning signs.
4. **Norway’s retreat matters beyond Norway.**  If parliament adopts the Gjedrem proposals in 2027, the benchmark for ethical sovereign investing will have bent under geopolitical pressure, and others will feel freer to follow.
5. **Private markets are being reshaped, not abandoned.**  Private equity and real estate lagged in 2025. Funds are rotating within private markets toward infrastructure, private credit and AI-linked real assets, and are moving to total-portfolio frameworks (GIC) that blur public and private buckets.
6. **For anyone raising capital or setting policy:**  sovereign money is easiest to attract for US/allied AI and digital infrastructure, energy-security assets, and large club deals with trusted GPs. It is hardest to attract for emerging markets (excluding the Gulf), China, and traditional real estate.

## Caveats

- **Estimated sizes:**  AUM for ADIA, KIA, QIA, GIC and SAFE IC are third-party estimates (mostly Global SWF), and other trackers differ by $100–300bn. [altss](https://altss.com/rankings/largest-sovereign-wealth-funds)[universalassetowners](https://www.universalassetowners.com/the-worlds-largest-sovereign-wealth-funds-2026/) Some secondary sites cite conflicting ADIA figures (for example, $870bn AUM and 6.8%/7.9% returns). [themiddleeastinsider](https://themiddleeastinsider.com/2026/04/20/adia-portfolio-2026-abu-dhabi-investment-authority/) These do not match ADIA’s own Review (6.6%/7.2%) and were disregarded.
- **Mismatched dates:**  figures run from end-2024 (CIC) to 30 June 2026 (NBIM, Future Fund). Currency conversions are approximate.
- **Danantara:**  its claimed $900bn and Global SWF’s about $230bn measure different things: an SOE “ecosystem” versus investable assets.
- **PIF return figure:**  sources disagree on whether 5.8% is PIF’s 2025 return or its annualized return since 2017. PIF’s own release describes it as annualized since 2017. [spa](https://spa.gov.sa/en/N2655240)
- **EA ownership:**  the 93.4%/5.5%/1.1% split comes from a reported Brazilian filing, not the US closing release. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leveraged_buyout_of_Electronic_Arts)[vision2030](https://vision2030.ai/analysis/electronic-arts-acquisition-closed-pif-ownership-financing/)
- **Forecasts:**  Global SWF projections ($80tn by 2030, $8.8tn Gulf AUM by 2030, $136bn 2026 Gulf deployment) and PIF’s SAR 10tn target are forecasts, not outcomes.
