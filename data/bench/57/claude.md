# AI at the Major Consulting Firms (2023–October 2026): Investments, Platforms, Partnerships, Results and Strategy

The big consulting firms have stopped making AI promises and are now reporting AI results. Accenture, IBM, BCG and Capgemini disclose the most measurable AI revenue or bookings. Deloitte has made the largest committed AI investment among the Big Four (US$3 billion through 2030).\[1\] Across the industry, AI is cutting junior hiring, pushing firms toward outcome-based pricing, and making alliances with model and infrastructure providers (Microsoft, NVIDIA, Anthropic, OpenAI) as important to strategy as the firms' own intellectual property.

## TL;DR
- **Who has the biggest AI business:** IBM, Accenture and BCG have the largest disclosed AI businesses.
  - IBM reported a cumulative generative AI book of business of more than $12.5B by Q4 2025, about four-fifths of it Consulting.
  - Accenture reported $5.9B in generative AI bookings and $2.7B in revenue in FY2025. It then stopped breaking these out and now reports headcount and client counts instead (nearly 110,000 AI and data professionals and more than 400 new advanced-AI clients in FY2026).
  - Bloomberg reported that AI work produced 25% of BCG's $14.4B revenue in 2025. BCG's own April 23, 2026 press release says "AI- and tech-focused services now represent over 40% of BCG's total revenue, driven by 25% year-over-year growth in AI services."
  - The Big Four mostly report growth rates rather than dollar amounts. For example, EY says its AI services sales grew 49% in FY2026.
- **What the investment headlines mean:** they are multi-year, self-reported pledges that mix talent, platforms, alliances and acquisitions.
  - Accenture: $3B (2023).
  - PwC: $1B in the US (2023), now "nearly US$1.5B" across its network.
  - EY: $1.4B (EY.ai, 2023).
  - KPMG: $2B with Microsoft (2023).
  - Deloitte: $3B through FY2030.
  - The hard money is in acquisitions: Accenture spent $4.9B on 17 deals in FY2026, IBM paid $11B for Confluent, and Capgemini paid $3.3B for WNS.
- **Where it's heading:** every firm is converging on "agentic" platforms built on partner technology, and differentiation now rests on three things:
  - proprietary data and IP (McKinsey's Lilli, Deloitte's Omnia, EY Canvas, KPMG Clara);
  - depth of alliances (Bain with OpenAI; KPMG and PwC with Anthropic; Deloitte, EY and Capgemini with NVIDIA);
  - willingness to sell outcomes rather than hours.
  
  The main risks are quality failures in AI-generated deliverables (Deloitte Australia, EY), shrinking junior pipelines, and pricing deflation.

## Details by Firm

### 1. Deloitte

**Investment**
- Deloitte's FY2026 revenue release (year ended May 31, 2026) describes "a US$3 billion investment through 2030 to modernize delivery and operations across all our businesses and bring new products and offerings to market."\[2\]
- The commitment was first reported in 2025 as generative AI development spending through FY2030.\[3\] It is the largest disclosed Big Four AI pledge.\[1\]
- FY2026 revenue was $74.5B, up 3.8% in local currency and 5.7% in US dollars.\[2\]
- Learning and development spending was "nearly US$700 million," with more than 23 million training hours.\[2\]

**Platforms**
- **Zora AI** launched in March 2025. It is built on NVIDIA AI, including Llama Nemotron reasoning models and the AI-Q Blueprint. Deloitte describes it as a "digital workforce" of agents for finance, human capital, supply chain, procurement, sales and marketing, and customer service.\[4\]\[5\]
- **Omnia** is Deloitte's audit platform. It gained a "unified agentic intelligence network" in FY2026.\[2\]

**Partners**
- NVIDIA is the anchor partner for Zora.
- Deloitte announced in October 2025 that it would deploy Anthropic's Claude to more than 470,000 people, its largest enterprise AI deployment.\[6\]\[7\]
- Its alliance roster also includes Google, AWS, Oracle, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow and Workday.\[2\]

**Case studies**
- Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) uses Zora AI for Finance for financial statement analysis, scenario modeling and competitive analysis. Deloitte's HPE case page says the Zora-based CFO Insights tool (called "Alfred" inside HPE) "is cutting financial reporting cycle time at HPE by approximately 40% and processing costs by 25% (at a minimum)." Fortune reported the same figures on February 12, 2026.
- Inside Deloitte, the stated target for its own finance function is a 25% cost reduction and a 40% productivity increase.\[5\] That is a target, not a result.

**Controversy**
- In October 2025, Deloitte Australia agreed to partially refund the A$440,000 (about US$290,000) it was paid for a 237-page welfare-compliance review for the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations.\[8\]
- The report contained fabricated citations and a made-up quote from a court judgment. The revised version disclosed that Azure OpenAI GPT-4o had been used.\[9\]
- Deloitte said the recommendations were unchanged. Senator Barbara Pocock called for a full refund.\[10\]\[11\]
- This is the clearest public example of the reputational risk of AI-assisted deliverables.\[6\]

### 2. PwC

**Investment**
- In April 2023, PwC US committed $1B over three years "to expand and scale its artificial intelligence (AI) offerings," with Microsoft and OpenAI as collaborators.\[12\]
- In its FY2025 results (October 28, 2025), PwC reported "US$3.1 billion invested across the network, including 12 acquisitions and strategic investments and nearly US$1.5 billion ongoing investment expanding and scaling our AI capabilities." Separately, it continues a US$1B Next Generation Audit platform investment.\[13\]
- FY2025 gross revenue was $56.9B (+2.7% in local currency).\[14\] Headcount fell by 5,600 to about 364,000.\[15\]

**Platforms and partners**
- **ChatPwC:** a Microsoft customer story cites use by 200,000 employees.\[16\]
- **ChatGPT Enterprise:** in May 2024, PwC became OpenAI's first reseller of ChatGPT Enterprise and its largest user (about 100,000 seats in the US and UK).\[17\]\[18\]
- **agent OS:** launched March 27, 2025, with "over 250 AI agents" in use internally at the time. A PwC spokesperson later told Business Insider that 25,000 agents had been deployed across client operations. PwC's own materials say "thousands."\[19\]\[20\]\[21\]
- **Assurance for AI:** launched June 3, 2025, described as "a first-to-market set of services… to provide independent assurance… over artificial intelligence (AI) systems."\[14\]\[22\]
- **Anthropic:** on May 14, 2026, PwC expanded its alliance with Anthropic. The terms include enterprise-wide Claude access globally, Claude Code and Cowork rollouts, a Claude-based Office of the CFO business group, and a program to train and certify 30,000 professionals.\[23\]\[24\]\[25\]

**Talent and headcount**
- PwC says it has upskilled more than 315,000 people in AI.\[26\]\[27\]
- In August 2025, Business Insider obtained an internal PwC US slide showing tax and assurance associate hires falling from 3,242 in FY2025 to 2,197 in FY2028, a 32% drop. The slide cited "the impact of AI" among the reasons, and PwC confirmed it was "decreasing our campus hiring goals."
- PwC UK took on 1,300 graduate and school-leaver entrants versus 1,500 the year before. UK chief Marco Amitrano said "AI is reshaping roles," while naming the economic slowdown as the "single biggest factor."\[28\]
- PwC UK's FY2026 results (September 2026) showed group revenue falling to £6.16B from £6.35B. The Times reported staff numbers cut by more than 4,000.\[29\]\[30\]

**Note:** PwC's FY2026 global results had not been published as of this report's date.\[31\]

### 3. EY

**Investment**
- In September 2023, EY launched EY.ai following a US$1.4B investment.\[32\]
- FY2026 (year to June 30, 2026) revenue was $56.97B, up 4.7% in local currency.\[33\]
- EY says sales from AI services grew 49%, but it does not disclose their absolute value.\[31\]
- Training spending was $448M, with 5.2M hours of formal AI learning and more than 40,000 AI badges earned (104,000 more in progress). Headcount was 415,182.\[34\]

**Platforms**
- **EY.ai Agentic Platform:** launched March 2025, built on NVIDIA AI Enterprise and AI-Q, starting with tax, risk and finance.\[32\]\[35\]
- **EY.ai enterprise private:** an on-premises version with Dell and NVIDIA, launched mid-2025.\[35\]\[36\]
- **Physical AI platform and EY.ai Lab:** opened December 2025 in Alpharetta, Georgia.\[35\]\[37\]
- **EY.ai Agentic for Sales:** launched March 2026 with Snowflake and Canva.\[38\]
- **NVIDIA NemoClaw for LangChain:** EY validated it in July 2026.\[39\]
- **Agentic SOC services:** built on CrowdStrike, with NVIDIA acceleration.\[40\]
- **Agentic audit:** in April 2026, EY rolled out enterprise-scale agentic AI in Assurance through EY Canvas, covering 160,000 audit engagements.\[38\] EY says 130,000 assurance professionals are AI-enabled.\[33\]

**Partners:** Microsoft is the deepest integration in EY's technology platform.\[38\] NVIDIA is the main partner for agentic and physical AI.

**Controversy:** City AM reported that EY withdrew a study on loyalty programmes in 2026 after it was found to contain AI hallucinations and fake footnotes.\[41\]

### 4. KPMG

**Investment**
- In July 2023, KPMG committed $2B over five years to its Microsoft alliance and cited an expected $12B revenue opportunity.\[7\] The $12B is a projection, not a result.

**Platforms**
- **KPMG Workbench:** launched June 2025 on Microsoft Azure AI Foundry, with 50 AI agents live and "nearly a thousand" in development at launch. It orchestrates agents across:\[42\]\[43\]
  - **KPMG Clara:** audit
  - **Digital Gateway:** tax
  - **KPMG Velocity:** advisory
- **Trusted AI framework:** governs all of it.

**Partners**
- On June 9, 2026, KPMG and Microsoft expanded their relationship. KPMG is adopting Microsoft Agent 365 for agent governance and extending Microsoft 365 Copilot to its full workforce of more than 276,000 people.\[44\]\[45\]
- In May 2026, an Anthropic alliance gave Claude to all 276,000+ staff and embedded it in Digital Gateway. Anthropic also named KPMG a preferred partner for private equity, and the two launched KPMG Blaze, which uses Claude Code for IT modernization.\[46\]
- In February 2026, KPMG US brought in the PrivateBlok agentic AI team to grow its AI product development function.\[47\]

**Talent:** KPMG's global AI workforce lead told Business Insider, "We want juniors to become managers of agents."\[21\]

### 5. Accenture

**Investment**
- In June 2023, Accenture committed $3B to Data & AI and set a goal of doubling its AI and data workforce from 40,000 to 80,000.\[48\]\[49\]
- In FY2026, Accenture:
  - deployed $4.9B across 17 acquisitions, with about $3B more for Cyber OT deals including Dragos that closed in September 2026;\[49\]\[50\]
  - invested $1.0B in learning and development;\[49\]
  - invested $1.0B in R&D.\[49\]
- About $5B more in acquisitions is planned for FY2027. Recent deals include Ookla, NeuraFlash (Salesforce/AI) and COMWARE.\[50\]\[51\]

**Results**
- FY2025: generative and agentic AI revenue roughly tripled to $2.7B, and bookings nearly doubled to $5.9B.\[51\] Accenture notes that these figures cover only advanced AI and exclude data, classical AI and AI used in delivery.\[52\]
- Q1 FY2026: $2.2B in advanced AI bookings.\[53\] Accenture then stopped reporting AI bookings separately, saying AI is now embedded in almost all of its work.\[51\]
- FY2026 (year ended August 31, 2026):
  - revenue was $74.2B and bookings $84.5B;\[54\]\[55\]
  - about 110,000 AI and data professionals, beating the 80,000 target;\[49\]
  - more than 400 clients started their first advanced AI work;\[50\]
  - bookings with its 8 emerging AI and data partners more than tripled;\[56\]
  - fixed-price work, including outcome-based work, is more than 65% of bookings.\[49\]

**Restructuring**
- In September 2025, Accenture announced an $865M six-month restructuring. CEO Julie Sweet said: "We are exiting on a compressed timeline people where reskilling… is not a viable path for the skills we need."\[57\]\[58\]
- Headcount fell from 791,000 to 779,000 in one quarter.\[59\] It had recovered to about 814,000 by October 2026.\[50\]
- Accenture has also trained more than 550,000 employees in generative AI fundamentals.\[60\]

**Watch item:** On the Q4 FY2026 call, Sweet acknowledged "lower pricing in many areas of our business."\[50\] This is an early sign that AI-driven productivity is being passed on to clients.

### 6. McKinsey & Company (QuantumBlack)

**Platforms**
- **Lilli:** launched 2023. Reported monthly use is above 75% of staff. Reported counts are about 150 centrally built agents and about 17,000 built by employees.\[61\]
- **QuantumBlack:** about 1,700 people.\[62\]
- **Agents:** in early 2026, Global Managing Partner Bob Sternfels described McKinsey's workforce on Harvard Business Review's IdeaCast podcast as "40,000 humans and 20,000 agents," later raising the agent figure to 25,000. He set a goal of parity between agents and humans.
- **Graduate recruiting:** reportedly now includes an AI interview stage.\[63\]

**Reported AI share and use**
- Senior partner and QuantumBlack co-lead Alex Singla told Business Insider in January 2026 that QuantumBlack, "with its 1,700-person team, drives all of McKinsey's AI initiatives, which now account for 40% of the firm's work." This figure is not audited and comes from executive remarks, not a revenue disclosure.
- Fortune reports that Lilli agents now build slides and check tone ("Tone of Voice"). Confidential client data can be entered only into Lilli, not into public tools.\[64\]

**Ecosystem:** Anthropic, Cohere and Mistral, among others.\[64\]

**Caveat:** Counting agents as "workforce" is a rhetorical choice. An agent is not the same as a full-time employee, so treat these numbers as signalling rather than a measure of capacity.

### 7. Boston Consulting Group (BCG X)

**Results**
- 2025 revenue was $14.4B (+7%), down from 10% growth in 2024.\[65\]
- Bloomberg reported on April 23, 2026 that AI work produced 25% of revenue, about $3.6B. BCG's own press release the same day says "AI- and tech-focused services now represent over 40% of BCG's total revenue, driven by 25% year-over-year growth in AI services." The 25% figure is therefore reported both as a revenue share (Bloomberg) and as a growth rate (BCG).

**People and partners**
- Headcount is 33,500. BCG X has more than 3,000 specialists.\[66\]\[67\]
- Nearly 4,000 employees are actively building AI workflows.\[68\]
- Partners include Anthropic, AWS, Google, IBM, Microsoft, OpenAI, Salesforce and SAP.\[66\]
- Named AI clients include L'Oréal, BMW, IBM, New York Life, Reckitt and Foxconn.\[66\]\[69\]

**Positioning:** BCG's "10-20-70" rule (10% algorithms, 20% technology and data, 70% people and process) frames AI as a change-management business.\[70\] That framing is where strategy firms have an edge over integrators.

### 8. Bain & Company

**OpenAI alliance**
- Bain was OpenAI's earliest major services partner (global alliance in 2023, expanded October 2024).\[71\]
- In May 2026, Bain took a stake in the OpenAI Deployment Company, which launched with 19 partners. Bain's private-equity clients get priority access.\[72\]
- Bain was named an OpenAI Elite Partner in July 2026.\[73\]

**Disclosure:** Bain has not disclosed an AI revenue share.\[74\]

**Research:** Bain's seventh Global Technology Report (September 29, 2026) argues that the AI industry needs $6 trillion in annual revenue by 2031 to justify data-center capital spending, while existing AI products may generate only $1.2–1.8 trillion. Lead author David Crawford wrote that "AI infrastructure is being built well ahead of the demand curve." This is a useful counterweight to hype from a firm that sells AI services.

**Strategic risk:** Bain is heavily concentrated on a single model provider.\[74\]

### 9. IBM (IBM Consulting, watsonx, Granite)

**Investment**
- HashiCorp (closed 2025) and DataStax.\[75\]
- Confluent, $11B at $31 per share, announced December 8, 2025 and closed March 17, 2026. Its purpose is to provide real-time data for agentic AI.\[76\]\[77\]

**Results**
- IBM's generative AI book of business passed $12.5B by Q4 2025. IBM defines it as cumulative software transactional revenue plus SaaS annual contract value plus Consulting signings. About four-fifths comes from Consulting, and IBM says it "should be viewed independently of revenue."\[78\]\[79\]
- In Q2 2026, IBM stopped citing the cumulative total. Instead it said generative AI was "about 50% of our signings in the quarter and now makes up over 30% of our backlog."\[80\]\[81\]
- Consulting revenue nonetheless stayed flat at about $5.3B per quarter, up 1% in constant currency. Consulting gross margin rose 1.4 points to 28.9% on productivity actions.\[82\]\[83\]\[84\]

**Platforms**
- **IBM Consulting Advantage:** IBM says it "equips nearly 150,000 IBM consultants" with AI assistants and agents.\[85\]
- **Productivity claims:** IBM's 2025 Investor Day claimed "up to 50% productivity gains on projects" and 75,000 consultants certified in generative AI.\[86\]
- **Software stack:** watsonx, watsonx Orchestrate, Granite models and Red Hat.\[87\]

**Interpretation:** IBM shows the bookings-versus-revenue gap most clearly. AI is taking over the mix of work without yet lifting consulting growth, which is consistent with AI cannibalizing traditional services such as legacy modernization.

### 10. Capgemini (including Capgemini Invent and WNS)

**Investment**
- Capgemini announced the WNS acquisition on July 7, 2025 ($76.50 per share, $3.3B) and closed it on October 17, 2025. It was funded by a €4.0B bond.\[88\]
- WNS had FY2025 revenue of $1.27B at an 18.7% margin.\[89\]
- Expected synergies by 2027 are €100–140M in revenue and €50–70M in cost.\[90\]
- Capgemini also acquired Cloud4C.\[91\] It plans about €700M in restructuring charges over 2026–27 to realign skills toward AI.\[92\]\[93\]

**Results**
- 2025 revenue was €22.47B (+3.4% at constant currency).\[93\]
- Generative and agentic AI bookings rose from about 5% of group bookings early in 2025 to more than 10% in Q4 2025, and above 11% in Q1 2026.\[93\]\[94\]\[95\]
- H1 2026 revenue was €12.08B (+11.3% at constant currency, mostly from acquisitions). Full-year guidance was raised to 8.5–9%.\[96\]\[97\]
- Capgemini reports a first agentic "mega deal" above €600M on a non-FTE model.\[95\]
- An agentic procurement transformation for one client produced €27M in reported savings.\[95\]
- Capgemini is redesigning shared services for a major North American automaker on an AI-enabled global business services model.\[96\]

**Positioning:** CEO Aiman Ezzat has said "Business Process Services will be the showcase for Agentic AI."\[90\] Capgemini's bet is that agents will reshape business process outsourcing first.

## Industry Context and Trends

**Market size**
- Gartner's September 2026 forecast puts worldwide AI spending at $2.7 trillion in 2026 (+49.5%).\[98\]
- Within that, Gartner forecasts AI services (consulting, implementation, managed services) at $576.5B in 2026, rising to about $746B in 2027. John-David Lovelock, Distinguished VP Analyst at Gartner, describes a "$1.2 trillion opportunity in AI services by 2030."
- Gartner also places generative AI in the "Trough of Disillusionment" in 2026.\[99\] That fits with PwC's 29th Global CEO Survey (January 19, 2026; 4,454 CEOs in 95 countries), in which "only one-in-eight (12%) CEOs say AI has delivered both cost and revenue benefits" and 56% reported neither.

**How the economics are changing**
1. **Fewer juniors, different juniors.**
   - PwC US plans to cut tax and assurance associate hires by 32% by FY2028, according to a slide obtained by Business Insider. Accenture "exits" staff it cannot reskill.\[60\]
   - McKinsey and KPMG talk of juniors as "managers of agents."
   - PwC's own 2026 AI Jobs Barometer finds that AI-exposed entry-level roles are seven times more likely to require senior skills.\[100\]
   - The traditional leverage pyramid is narrowing at the base.
2. **Shift from hours to outcomes and platforms.**
   - Fixed-price and outcome-based work is more than 65% of Accenture's bookings.\[49\]
   - Capgemini signed its first agentic mega-deal on a non-FTE model.\[95\]
   - Accenture is buying companies with "non-FTE commercial models."\[50\]
3. **Price deflation.** Accenture reports lower pricing, and IBM's AI share of signings is rising while revenue stays flat. Both point to AI productivity gains going to clients.
4. **Model-provider alliances as strategy.** Anthropic signed enterprise-wide deals with Deloitte, PwC and KPMG within about eight months.\[101\] OpenAI anchors Bain and earlier PwC work. NVIDIA anchors the agent platforms of Deloitte, EY and Capgemini.
5. **AI assurance and governance as a new service line.** Examples include PwC's Assurance for AI, KPMG's Trusted AI framework with Agent 365, and EY's private/on-premises AI for regulated sectors.
6. **Sovereign AI.** Deloitte forecasts nearly $100B of global investment in sovereign AI compute in 2026.\[102\]\[103\] This drives demand for private and on-premises deployments such as EY.ai enterprise private.

## Risks and Criticisms
- **Quality and liability.** Deloitte Australia's partial refund and EY's withdrawn study show that AI hallucinations in paid deliverables are already happening, with regulatory and political consequences.\[10\]\[41\]
- **Metric opacity.**
  - The partnerships don't disclose AI revenue.
  - IBM's "book of business" is cumulative and is not revenue.
  - Accenture and IBM both stopped reporting their headline AI totals in 2026, which removes the cleanest check on progress.
  - Agent counts (McKinsey's 25,000, PwC's 25,000) are not standardized.
- **Workforce effects.** Accenture cut about 11,000 people in one quarter.\[104\] Capgemini has €700M of restructuring planned. PwC UK headcount fell by more than 4,000.\[29\] Graduate pipelines are shrinking, which may weaken future partner development.
- **Vendor concentration.** Bain depends on OpenAI and KPMG on Microsoft, which exposes them to partners' pricing and roadmap choices.\[74\] Model providers are also building their own deployment arms, such as OpenAI's Deployment Company, which may compete with consultancies.\[71\]
- **Hype and cannibalization.** Bain's own $6 trillion revenue-gap analysis and Gartner's "trough" placement suggest that AI demand may be cyclical.\[99\]\[105\] AI coding tools also threaten lucrative legacy-modernization work.

## Analysis and Recommendations
- **The winners are hybrid.** The firms with the clearest momentum (Accenture, IBM, BCG, Capgemini) combine advisory work with execution and run-the-business capacity, through managed services, BPS or software. Strategy-only and audit-led firms are mostly selling AI as an embedded upgrade to existing service lines.
- **Ranking AI positioning in October 2026, on the evidence:**
  1. Accenture: scale, disclosure and ecosystem.
  2. IBM: owns its stack, but consulting growth is stalled.
  3. BCG: highest disclosed AI share of revenue among strategy firms.
  4. Deloitte: largest Big Four pledge and a product strategy.
  5. Capgemini: a credible agentic-BPS bet that depends on integrating WNS.

  EY, KPMG and PwC are advancing quickly in agentic audit and tax but give the least financial transparency. McKinsey and Bain have strong internal AI but disclose little.
- **For buyers of consulting services:**
  - insist on outcome-based or productivity-sharing pricing, because the firms' own disclosures show large internal productivity gains;
  - require disclosure of AI use in deliverables, with human verification of citations;
  - check which model and hyperscaler lock-in comes bundled with each firm's platform.
- **For investors and competitors:** watch revenue per employee, the share of fixed-price and outcome-based work, and pricing commentary. Self-reported AI bookings are a less reliable guide.

## Caveats
- Most figures for private partnerships (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG, McKinsey, BCG, Bain) are self-reported and unaudited. Several McKinsey and Bain figures come from executive remarks reported secondhand.
- Investment pledges mix operating spend, talent, alliances and acquisitions, so they cannot be compared directly across firms.
