# Buffett, Munger and Duan Yongping: One Philosophy in Three Accents

Buffett, Munger and Duan Yongping (段永平) hold one shared core idea: a stock is part of a business, and you should buy only a business you understand, at a price below what its future cash flows are worth, and then hold it patiently. What separates them is emphasis. Buffett supplied the framework and the temperament, Munger supplied the quality-first mindset and a multidisciplinary way of avoiding error, and Duan added an operator's ethic ("本分", the Stop Doing List) learned by building companies before he invested in them. Duan is best read as a Buffett–Munger practitioner with an entrepreneur's lens, not as a separate school.

## TL;DR
- **Same foundation:** all three treat stocks as ownership of businesses, stay inside a "circle of competence," concentrate in a handful of high-quality companies, hold them for years, and avoid leverage, shorting and anything they don't understand. Duan's 2018 Stanford remark that "not doing wrong things" matters more than "doing right things" is close to Munger's line, from a letter to Wesco shareholders reprinted in Janet Lowe's *Damn Right!*, that much of their long-term advantage came from "trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent."
- **Different emphases:** Buffett began as a Graham-style bargain hunter and was pushed by Munger toward "wonderful businesses at fair prices." Munger added mental models, inversion and the psychology of misjudgment. Duan reads companies mainly through business model and corporate culture, and calls value "the discounted future cash flow," which he treats as a way of thinking, not a formula.
- **What to take from it:** if you want to learn from them, treat Buffett's letters as the base text, Munger's talks as a checklist against mistakes, and Duan's Xueqiu Q&As as a guide to judging business models and culture. Also copy the discipline Duan shares with them: no shorting, no borrowing, few decisions.

## Introduction

Warren Buffett (b. 1930) and Charlie Munger (1924–2023) ran Berkshire Hathaway together for decades. Buffett stepped down as CEO on January 1, 2026, handing over to Greg Abel, and stepped down as chairman in September 2026 to become chairman emeritus.\[1\]\[2\] Duan Yongping (b. 1961) founded Subor (小霸王) and BBK (步步高), retired to the US around age 40 in 2001, and became one of the best-known value investors in the Chinese-speaking world.\[3\]\[4\]\[5\] He writes under the Xueqiu handle "大道无形我有型."\[6\] His philosophy grew directly out of Buffett's: in 2006, at a charity auction, he paid $620,100 for lunch with Buffett.\[5\]\[7\]\[8\]

## Warren Buffett

**Background and evolution.** Buffett studied under Benjamin Graham at Columbia in 1950 and later worked at Graham-Newman.\[9\] From Graham he took three ideas he never dropped:
- a stock is a share of a business;\[10\]
- "Mr. Market" is there to serve you, not to guide you;\[11\]
- the margin of safety.\[10\]

His early style was "cigar butt" investing: buying mediocre companies so cheaply that one last "puff" of profit was nearly guaranteed.\[12\] In his 1989 letter he wrote that this approach was foolish unless you are a liquidator, because "time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre."\[13\] He then gave his best-known formulation: it is far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price. He added, "Charlie understood this early; I was a slow learner."\[14\] In the 2014 letter he credited Munger with Berkshire's "blueprint": forget buying fair businesses at wonderful prices, and buy wonderful businesses at fair prices instead.\[15\]\[16\]\[17\]

**Core principles.**
- **Circle of competence:** the size of the circle matters little, but "knowing its boundaries… is vital" (1996 letter).\[18\] Anything he can't evaluate goes into the "too hard" pile.\[19\]
- **Economic moats:** a great business has an enduring "moat" protecting its returns on capital from competitors attacking the "castle."\[20\]
- **Margin of safety and price vs. value:** he keeps Graham's discipline but applies it to the quality of the business, not just to the balance sheet.\[11\]
- **Long holding periods and temperament:** he leaves compounding alone and treats temperament, not IQ, as the real edge.\[18\]
- **Contrarianism:** in his October 2008 New York Times op-ed "Buy American. I Am." he stated his rule: be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful.\[21\]\[22\]

**Investments that show the philosophy.**
- **See's Candies (1972, $25 million):** a turning point away from Graham-style bargains.\[16\] At the 2019 Berkshire annual meeting Buffett said, "We put $25 million into it, and it's given us over $2 billion of pretax income."
- **Coca-Cola:** a cost basis of $1.299 billion, made up of $1.024 billion in 1988–89 and $0.275 billion added in 1994. After two stock splits Berkshire holds 400 million shares and has never sold one (Motley Fool), and the Motley Fool valued the stake at $34.7 billion on October 3, 2026.
- **Apple:** about $38 billion invested between 2016 and 2023. The position was worth more than $170 billion by early 2024, before Berkshire sold about three-quarters of it in 2024–25 to reduce concentration.\[23\]

## Charlie Munger

**Background.** Munger was born in Omaha in 1924 and worked as a boy in Buffett's grandfather's grocery store. He left the University of Michigan to serve as an Army meteorologist in World War II, then graduated from Harvard Law.\[24\] In 1962 he co-founded the firm now called Munger, Tolles & Olson, and ran the Wheeler, Munger investment partnership from 1962 to 1975. He met Buffett in 1959, was vice chairman of Berkshire from 1978 until his death on November 28, 2023, chaired the Daily Journal Corporation, and sat on Costco's board.\[25\]\[26\]\[27\]\[28\]\[29\]\[30\] His legal training shows in his habit of arguing a case from every side and his intolerance of self-deception.

**Core principles.**
- **Latticework of mental models:** in his 1994 USC talk on "elementary worldly wisdom" he argued that you must "hang experience on a latticework of models."\[31\] He meant models from psychology, economics, mathematics, physics and biology.\[32\] If you have only one model, everything looks like a nail.\[31\]
- **Inversion:** "Invert, always invert," borrowed from the mathematician Carl Jacobi. Ask how you would fail, then don't do that.\[33\]\[34\] In his joke, he only wants to know where he will die so he never goes there.\[35\]
- **Avoiding stupidity over seeking brilliance:** in a letter to Wesco shareholders, reprinted in Janet Lowe's *Damn Right!*, he wrote that it is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like them gained by "trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent," adding the folk saying that "it's the strong swimmers who drown."
- **Psychology of human misjudgment:** in talks at Harvard in 1994–95, later expanded in *Poor Charlie's Almanack*, he catalogued about 25 tendencies that cause misjudgment, starting with incentives.\[36\]\[37\]
- **"Sit-on-your-ass investing" and concentration:** if you buy something only because it is cheap, you soon have to decide when to sell it. If you buy a few great companies, you can just sit.\[38\] He saw three companies as enough diversification, with lower fees and taxes and less "nonsense."\[25\]\[37\]
- **Quality first:** the "blueprint" Buffett credits to him is wonderful businesses at fair prices.\[15\]\[39\]

**Investments and lessons.** He pushed Berkshire toward See's and other franchise businesses.\[39\] He backed Costco as a director and long-time admirer.\[40\] Late in life, through the Daily Journal, he bought Alibaba with borrowed money, then called it one of his worst mistakes and cut the position.\[41\] Even the inventor of inversion could step outside his own rules.

## Duan Yongping (段永平)

**Background.** Duan was born in Nanchang in 1961 and studied radio electronics at Zhejiang University, followed by a master's at Renmin University. He turned the struggling Subor into the leading "learning computer" maker. He left in 1995 to found BBK Electronics.\[5\] In 2001 he stepped back and split BBK into independent businesses, and that ecosystem produced OPPO and vivo (OnePlus and realme came later).\[3\]\[42\] His protégés, including OPPO's Tony Chen, vivo's Shen Wei, BBK's Jin Zhijiang and Pinduoduo's Colin Huang, call him their mentor.\[43\] Huang once called himself Duan's "fourth disciple."\[44\]\[45\] Ding Lei introduced Huang to Duan in 2002, and Duan advised him to join Google rather than Microsoft.\[46\]\[47\] In 2006 Duan, bidding as "fastisslow," won Buffett's charity lunch for $620,100.\[5\] It was a record at the time, nearly double the previous year's price, and he was the first Chinese winner.\[7\]\[8\]\[43\] He brought along Huang, then 26 and working at Google, and later said he simply wanted to thank Buffett for what he had learned from him.\[43\]\[48\]

**Core principles.**
- **本分 + 平常心 (benfen plus an ordinary, calm mind):** asked why BBK's companies succeeded, Duan rejects the usual explanations (advertising, employee shares, dealer networks) and says the secret is simply "本分+平常心."\[49\] He defines 平常心 as being rational and getting back to the essence of things.\[50\] He defines 本分 as "doing the right things, and doing things right" (做对的事情，把事情做对), or more plainly as doing what you are supposed to do and being who you are.\[49\]
- **The Stop Doing List (不为清单):** at Stanford in September 2018 he said that doing the right thing "must be put into practice by not doing wrong things." Once you find you are doing something wrong, stop at once, whatever the cost, because that will be the smallest cost.\[49\]\[51\]\[52\] The company's version was built "one item at a time" from painful lessons.\[53\] It includes: no sales department and no haggling, no selling on credit, no paying suppliers late, no attacking competitors, and no dishonest dealings.\[49\]\[52\]
- **不懂不做 (don't do what you don't understand):** he says his investing Stop Doing List is simply "don't touch what you don't understand," and that understanding comes down to two things, the **business model** and the **corporate culture**.\[54\] He once asked Buffett what investors should never do and got three answers: no shorting, no borrowing, and above all nothing you don't understand.\[49\]\[55\]\[56\]
- **买股票就是买公司 (buying a stock is buying the company):** buying a company means buying "its discounted future cash flows, full stop."\[57\] The "future" means the company's whole life. The discount rate is your opportunity cost. He calls DCF "a way of thinking," something to estimate roughly (毛估估), not plug into a calculator. He has said he generally aims to buy at 50% or more below his estimate of value.\[54\]\[58\]\[59\]
- **Few decisions and holding with flexibility:** about 20 investment decisions in a lifetime is plenty.\[52\] Long-term holding is the intention when you buy, not a vow never to sell. In a 2025 interview he said that if he had understood Nvidia five years earlier while holding only Moutai, he would have had no reason not to switch.\[60\]\[61\]

**Notable investments.**
- **NetEase (2001–02):** after the dot-com crash, with delisting threats and a lawsuit hanging over the company, he found it held more cash per share than its share price (shares at about $0.80, cash above $2 per share). He checked the legal risk with lawyers, saw promise in its online games, and put in most of the money he could deploy, about $2 million, for a stake of 5–7%.\[47\]\[62\] The shares reached $70 by October 2003, a return of roughly 50–100x depending on the account.\[3\]\[63\]\[64\]\[65\] This was half Graham cigar butt, half bet on the business: a margin of safety backed by an operator's judgment of the business.\[65\]
- **Apple (from 2011):** by his own account he began buying on January 19, 2011, once he concluded that Apple "without Jobs is still strong."\[66\] He has called Jobs a "clock-builder," said Cook is a more rational CEO, and described Apple's culture as a "classic" of good corporate culture, adding that OPPO and Apple share "a lot of the same genes."\[67\]\[68\] Apple is still his largest position.\[4\] In H&H International's Q2 2026 13F, Apple was about 41% of the $19.1 billion reported portfolio, with Berkshire Hathaway about 24% and PDD about 10%.\[69\]\[70\]
- **Kweichow Moutai (2012–14):** he started buying near ¥180 during the plasticizer scare and kept buying down to about ¥120. His reasoning was that over a ten-year view the difference between those prices barely matters,\[71\] and that a business collecting customer prepayments is rare.\[72\] He has said his RMB account is "basically all Moutai."\[73\]
- **Pinduoduo:** he was one of its angel investors alongside Ding Lei, Sun Tongyu and Wang Wei, and has said he invested because he knows and trusts Huang personally, not because he does early-stage deals.\[3\]\[45\]\[74\] He added roughly 27% to his PDD stake in Q2 2026.\[75\] Pinduoduo's F-1 shareholder letter named "本分" as its core value, carrying his ethic into the next generation.\[49\]\[76\]

**Mistakes he admits.** In a September 14, 2010 Xueqiu post he said that shorting Baidu cost him "roughly $150–200 million" across all his accounts, and that "this mistake came after Buffett told me not to short." He later called it a loss of 平常心 that confirmed his no-shorting rule. He was also hurt by an airline bankruptcy after Buffett had warned him about airlines.

## How They Relate

**Duan as Buffett's student.** Duan says that the most important thing he learned from Buffett was not doing what he doesn't understand.\[51\] His core vocabulary maps directly onto Berkshire's:
- 能力圈 is the circle of competence;
- 护城河 is the moat;
- 买股票就是买公司 restates Graham and Buffett's ownership view;
- his DCF framing follows Buffett's definition of intrinsic value.

**What Duan adds or stresses differently.**
1. **An operator's view of culture.** Buffett judges management, while Duan treats culture as part of the moat itself and doubts a company can have a wide moat without a strong culture.\[77\] His culture test is "what is right and what is not right, and how to do the right thing right," which comes straight from running BBK.\[68\]
2. **Benfen as an ethic for the company, not just the portfolio.** Buffett and Munger stress integrity in managers. Duan made 本分 a written core value that runs from BBK to OPPO, vivo and Pinduoduo.\[49\]\[78\]
3. **The Stop Doing List as a way of running a company.** It is Munger's inversion made operational: a growing list of rules about what not to do, learned through losses.\[49\]\[53\]
4. **More flexibility with tools and sector rotation.** Duan openly uses options, sells puts, and rotates between holdings on opportunity cost.\[79\]\[80\] That is more active than Buffett's "forever" holdings, though Berkshire also trimmed Apple. Duan also accepts tech-hardware businesses much earlier than Buffett did.

**Where Buffett and Munger differ.** Munger was the one who pushed for quality; Buffett, by his own account, was the "slow learner" who had to unlearn Graham.\[81\] Munger's toolkit was broader and drew on psychology, biology and physics through the latticework and the misjudgment talks. Buffett's output is plainer: annual letters about accounting, insurance float and capital allocation. Munger was more willing to concentrate heavily, including abroad (Alibaba through the Daily Journal, and BYD).\[41\] Buffett has also recommended index funds to most investors.\[81\]

### Comparison Table

| Dimension | Warren Buffett | Charlie Munger | Duan Yongping |

| Formative background | Graham's student; bargain hunter turned business owner | Lawyer, real-estate investor, polymath | Founder and operator (Subor, BBK) turned investor |
| Core definition of value | Intrinsic value = discounted future cash flows | Same, judged through business quality | "买股票就是买公司": discounted future cash flow as a way of thinking |
| Signature idea | Circle of competence, moat, margin of safety, Mr. Market | Latticework of mental models, inversion, misjudgment | 本分 + 平常心, Stop Doing List, 不懂不做 |
| Quality vs. price | Moved from cigar butts to "wonderful company at a fair price" | Quality first from early on | Business model and culture first; aims for ~50% discount on value |
| Concentration | High (Coca-Cola, Apple, AmEx) | Very high ("three companies is plenty") | Very high (Apple ~41% of 13F) |
| View of risk | Permanent loss; avoid leverage | Avoid stupidity; invert | No shorting, no borrowing, nothing not understood |
| Holding period | "Forever" ideally | "Sit-on-your-ass" | Long-term intent, but switches on opportunity cost |
| Famous holdings | See's, Coca-Cola, GEICO, Apple | Costco, BYD (via Li Lu), Daily Journal holdings | NetEase, Apple, Moutai, Pinduoduo, Berkshire |
| Teaching medium | Annual letters, AGMs | Speeches, *Poor Charlie's Almanack* | Xueqiu Q&A, blog, Stanford/Zhejiang talks |

**Common threads.** All three:
- are long-term owners of businesses;
- insist on real understanding before acting;
- concentrate in few ideas;
- wait patiently for fat pitches, often during panics (Buffett in 2008, Duan with NetEase and Moutai);
- value temperament over IQ;
- put avoiding big mistakes first.

## Conclusion

The three philosophies are one system with different weights. Buffett is the architect: owner mindset, moats, margin of safety, patience. Munger is the editor who raised the quality bar and built tools for catching errors in judgment. Duan is the practitioner who turned both into an operating ethic, 本分 and a Stop Doing List, and who reads companies through business model and culture because he has built them himself. For an investor, the practical lesson is that the shared parts (understand it, own it, concentrate, don't lever, don't short, wait) do most of the work. The individual accents are refinements, not alternatives.

## Caveats
- Many of Duan's figures, such as NetEase share counts and prices, Moutai entry prices and Apple purchase dates, come from secondary Chinese compilations of his Xueqiu posts, and they vary. NetEase cost estimates range from about $0.16 to $1.\[3\]\[63\] Some sources date his first Apple purchase to November 2011, but he himself says January 19, 2011.\[6\]\[66\]
- Some popular claims, such as that Duan "taught Buffett to buy Apple" or that he earned annualized 36% returns, are unverified, and you should not rely on them.\[64\]\[82\]
- 13F data covers only US long equity positions.\[83\]
