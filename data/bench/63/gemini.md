# Mitigating Plasma Etching Damage in Thin-Film Lithium Niobate for Nonlinear Photonics

## Physical and Chemical Mechanisms of Plasma Etch Damage in Lithium Niobate

The transition from bulk diffused waveguides to thin-film lithium niobate on insulator (LNOI or TFLN) has increased optical mode confinement by orders of magnitude, elevating normalized second-harmonic generation (SHG) conversion efficiencies to 2,600–4,600% W−1cm−22,600\text{--}4,600\%\text{ W}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-2} in single-pass waveguides and 250,000%/W250,000\%/\text{W} in periodically poled microring resonators [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). Realizing these metrics requires anisotropic dry etching via inductively coupled plasma reactive-ion etching (ICP-RIE) or ion beam etching (IBE) to transfer sub-micrometer waveguide geometries [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242). Because lithium niobate (LiNbO3\text{LiNbO}_3, LN) is chemically inert and structurally complex—characterized by a trigonal crystal system (3m3m point group, lattice parameters a=b=5.148 A˚a = b = 5.148\text{ \AA}, c=13.863 A˚c = 13.863\text{ \AA}) consisting of oxygen octahedra housing alternating niobium (Nb\text{Nb}) and lithium (Li\text{Li}) cations [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242)—plasma patterning inevitably inflicts severe surface and subsurface material degradation [8](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/advanced-photonics/volume-8/issue-5/056005/Encoding-functional-nanocircuits-into-ferroelectric-photonics/10.1117/1.AP.8.5.056005.pdf).

In nonlinear photonic applications, etch-induced damage degrades device performance far more severely than in linear passive circuits, manifesting across three distinct physical regimes that span from the outer waveguide boundary into the crystalline core [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242). At the outermost boundary, plasma chemistry and physical sputtering generate non-volatile redeposition crusts [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242). Conventional fluorine-based RIE plasmas (SF6\text{SF}_6, CF4\text{CF}_4, CHF3\text{CHF}_3) volatilize niobium into fluorinated species but react with lithium to form non-volatile lithium fluoride (LiF\text{LiF}) [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242). Redeposited LiF\text{LiF} and sputtered metal-oxide complexes coat the waveguide sidewalls, creating micromasking, severe line-edge roughness, and two-level system (TLS) interface defects [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242). Even in pure physical argon (Ar+\text{Ar}^+) milling or chlorine- and bromine-based (Cl2/BCl3/Ar\text{Cl}_2/\text{BCl}_3/\text{Ar}, HBr/BCl3/Ar\text{HBr}/\text{BCl}_3/\text{Ar}) plasmas, sputtered LN and eroded mask species redeposit onto sloped sidewalls unless chamber pressure and ion energy are strictly balanced [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/).

Directly beneath this surface crust, high-energy ion bombardment induces subsurface lattice amorphization [8](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/advanced-photonics/volume-8/issue-5/056005/Encoding-functional-nanocircuits-into-ferroelectric-photonics/10.1117/1.AP.8.5.056005.pdf). Energetic ions initiate two-step knock-on atomic displacement cascades (Ion→Nb\text{Ion} \rightarrow \text{Nb} and Ion→O\text{Ion} \rightarrow \text{O}), generating localized thermal spikes and plastic flow that collapse the single-crystal trigonal phase into an amorphous surface skin [8](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/advanced-photonics/volume-8/issue-5/056005/Encoding-functional-nanocircuits-into-ferroelectric-photonics/10.1117/1.AP.8.5.056005.pdf). Depending on the RF bias and ICP power, this amorphous damaged layer ranges from 6 nm6\text{ nm} under moderate surface-activated bombardment to ∼20 nm\sim 20\text{ nm} in standard Ar+\text{Ar}^+ ion-milled thin films, and up to 260 nm260\text{ nm} under unoptimized high-power RIE conditions [9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12479742/). Because amorphous LN lacks inversion-symmetry breaking, this dead layer exhibits zero second-order nonlinear susceptibility (χ(2)=0\chi^{(2)} = 0) and introduces substantial sub-bandgap optical absorption [16](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/111806/1/Du_Asymmetric_Proton_Exchange.pdf).

Extending still deeper into the crystalline transition zone beneath the amorphous skin, preferential sputtering and radiation damage strip light cations and anions, driving Li2O\text{Li}_2\text{O} segregation and generating charged lithium vacancies (VLiV_{\text{Li}}) alongside near-surface oxygen vacancies (VOV_{\text{O}}) [8](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/advanced-photonics/volume-8/issue-5/056005/Encoding-functional-nanocircuits-into-ferroelectric-photonics/10.1117/1.AP.8.5.056005.pdf). Core-level X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of plasma-etched ferroelectric metasurfaces confirms the emergence of sub-oxidation peaks in the O 1s\text{O } 1s spectrum alongside reduced Nb4+\text{Nb}^{4+} states [8](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/advanced-photonics/volume-8/issue-5/056005/Encoding-functional-nanocircuits-into-ferroelectric-photonics/10.1117/1.AP.8.5.056005.pdf). In reduced or irradiated congruent LN, charge compensation traps electrons at niobium sites to form NbLi4+\text{Nb}_{\text{Li}}^{4+} small polarons, which produce a near-infrared absorption band centered at 760 nm760\text{ nm}, and NbNb4+–NbLi4+\text{Nb}_{\text{Nb}}^{4+}\text{--}\text{Nb}_{\text{Li}}^{4+} bipolarons, which produce a visible absorption band at 500 nm500\text{ nm} characterized by a thermal dissociation energy of 0.27 eV0.27\text{ eV} [17](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4352/11/7/764).

For nonlinear frequency conversion, these three damage mechanisms interact destructively. Rayleigh scattering from sidewall roughness scales as λ−4\lambda^{-4}, meaning that surface irregularities that are tolerable at 1550 nm1550\text{ nm} become prohibitive at visible or ultraviolet second-harmonic wavelengths such as 775 nm775\text{ nm} or 375 nm375\text{ nm} [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). Concurrently, polarons and oxygen vacancies act as deep donor and acceptor traps that trigger the photorefractive (PR) effect and green-induced infrared absorption (GRIIRA) [21](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14777v2). Under intense intracavity pump fields, photo-excited carriers migrate and establish space-charge fields that modulate the refractive index via the electro-optic effect, causing resonance frequency blue-shifts with characteristic time constants of ∼100 s\sim 100\text{ s}, thermal lensing, and DC bias hysteresis [18](https://pubs.acs.org/nalefd/article/doi/10.1021/acs.nanolett.6c03406/5436048/Hysteresis-and-Trap-Emission-in-dc-Biased). Furthermore, charged point defects (VLiV_{\text{Li}}, VOV_{\text{O}}) and implanted Ar+\text{Ar}^+ ions establish localized built-in electric fields and alter surface conductivity [8](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/advanced-photonics/volume-8/issue-5/056005/Encoding-functional-nanocircuits-into-ferroelectric-photonics/10.1117/1.AP.8.5.056005.pdf). In periodically poled lithium niobate (PPLN) waveguides, these conductive defect pathways cause premature dielectric breakdown before the coercive field for domain inversion is reached, or pin domain walls to degrade the 50%50\% quasi-phase-matching (QPM) duty cycle [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1).

## Post-Etch Wet Chemical Remediation: Redeposition Stripping and Selective Damage Etching

The immediate post-etch requirement is the physical removal of the hardened resist, metallic or dielectric hard mask remnants, the sputtered sidewall redeposition crust, and the outer amorphous LN skin [26](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/adv/article/14/6/065317/3297431/Optimization-of-waveguide-fabrication-processes-in). Because high-energy Ar+\text{Ar}^+ bombardment cross-links organic photoresists and sinters redeposited metal oxides, standard organic solvents such as acetone fail to strip the post-etch residue [31](https://orbit.dtu.dk/files/458749468/Thesis_vahta_DTU_Electro.pdf).

### Alkaline Peroxide (SC-1 / RCA-1) and Piranha Protocols

A dual-stage wet oxidative clean utilizing Standard Clean 1 (SC-1 / RCA-1, consisting of NH4OH:H2O2:H2O\text{NH}_4\text{OH}:\text{H}_2\text{O}_2:\text{H}_2\text{O}) paired with Piranha solution (H2SO4:H2O2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4:\text{H}_2\text{O}_2) or hot potassium hydroxide (KOH\text{KOH}) serves as the foundational stripping protocol [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). Immersing the etched TFLN chip in heated SC-1 at 70 ∘C70\text{ }^\circ\text{C} for 60 minutes60\text{ minutes} effectively oxidizes and undercuts ion-hardened photoresist and dissolves the loosely bound, non-stoichiometric redeposited LN crust from the waveguide sidewalls [30](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/app/article/11/8/086110/3400638). In workflows utilizing hydrogen silsesquioxane (HSQ) or negative-tone electron-beam resists such as ma-N 2400, a sequence of hot KOH\text{KOH} followed by heated SC-1 and Piranha completely eliminates sidewall redeposition without roughening the underlying crystalline LN [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1).

### Selective Hydrofluoric Acid Stripping of the Subsurface Amorphous Layer

While SC-1 removes surface redeposition, it does not rapidly etch the denser, mechanically amorphized subsurface LN layer generated by knock-on ion implantation [9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12479742/). To strip this damaged crystal skin, fabrication engineers exploit the vast differential chemical reactivity between amorphous and single-crystal LiNbO3\text{LiNbO}_3 in hydrofluoric acid (HF\text{HF}) [9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12479742/).

Pristine, single-crystal LN is remarkably resistant to HF\text{HF}, whereas amorphous or ion-damaged LN etches at rates orders of magnitude faster [9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12479742/). Specifically, fully amorphous LN films dissolve at ∼5 μm/min\sim 5\text{ }\mu\text{m/min} in 25%25\% aqueous HF\text{HF} and ∼7 μm/min\sim 7\text{ }\mu\text{m/min} in 4%4\% HF\text{HF} at room temperature [38](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2002075444A1/en?authuser=1). Quantitative X-ray reflectometry (XRR) and selective wet-etch profiling on Ar+\text{Ar}^+ ion-milled LNOI films demonstrate that a 3-minute3\text{-minute} immersion in 49%49\% HF\text{HF} cleanly strips the ∼20 nm\sim 20\text{ nm} ion-damaged amorphous surface layer and self-terminates once the undamaged single-crystal core is exposed [9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12479742/). Alternatively, dilute HF\text{HF} or HF:HNO3\text{HF}:\text{HNO}_3 mixtures can be timed to remove subsurface damage (SSD) and expose a pristine crystalline boundary [36](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387478/).

### Critical Integration Trade-offs for Nonlinear Waveguides (BOX Undercut and PPLN Corrugation)

Applying HF\text{HF}-based wet treatments to TFLN nonlinear devices requires navigating two severe structural hazards involving buried oxide undercutting and domain-selective etching [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). In LNOI wafers, the underlying SiO2\text{SiO}_2 buffer layer etches rapidly in HF\text{HF}, dissolving at 5–6 nm/min5\text{--}6\text{ nm/min} even in dilute 1%1\% HF\text{HF} and much faster in concentrated 49%49\% HF\text{HF} or buffered oxide etchant [9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12479742/). If the plasma etch punches completely through the LN film to create a fully etched ridge waveguide exposing the SiO2\text{SiO}_2 floor, an aggressive HF\text{HF} dip will severely undercut the waveguide base [41](https://www.oejournal.org/article/doi/10.29026/oea.2025.240139). Consequently, selective HF\text{HF} damage removal is best restricted to partially etched rib waveguides where a continuous LN slab (100–300 nm100\text{--}300\text{ nm} thick) shields the buried SiO2\text{SiO}_2 layer, or limited to brief, highly dilute HF\text{HF} exposures [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242).

A second and equally critical chemical hazard arises in periodically poled waveguides [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). In ferroelectric LN and lithium tantalate (LiTaO3\text{LiTaO}_3), opposite polar faces exhibit strong differential etch rates in HF\text{HF}, HF:HNO3\text{HF}:\text{HNO}_3, and even heated SC-1 [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). In HF\text{HF} and buffered HF\text{HF}, protonation reactions preferentially attack the −Z-Z face (and differentially etch +Y+Y versus −Y-Y faces) while leaving the +Z+Z face virtually untouched [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). If a TFLN substrate is periodically poled *before*  waveguide etching ("pole-before-etch"), subjecting the etched waveguide to an HF\text{HF} or extended SC-1 bath etches periodic trenches into the inverted domains along the waveguide sidewalls [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). This differential attack imprints a parasitic surface relief grating at the exact QPM period, causing severe optical scattering and radiation loss. Therefore, when fabricating PPLN waveguides, engineers must either adopt a **pole-after-etch**  workflow [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1)—permitting aggressive post-etch wet cleaning and thermal annealing prior to domain inversion—or restrict post-etch wet chemistry on pre-poled chips to controlled SC-1 and Piranha cleans followed by dry thermal healing [33](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08221v1).

## Thermal Recrystallization and Oxygen Vacancy Healing

Wet chemical stripping removes the outer amorphous skin and redeposition, but cannot heal deep-seated point defects (VOV_{\text{O}}, VLiV_{\text{Li}}), Nb4+\text{Nb}^{4+} polarons, or sub-threshold lattice strain extending hundreds of nanometers into the waveguide core from both plasma etching and the initial Smart-Cut helium ion implantation (He+\text{He}^+ dose ∼4×1016 ions/cm2\sim 4 \times 10^{16}\text{ ions/cm}^2) [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). High-temperature thermal annealing in an oxidizing atmosphere is the primary mechanism to restore bulk-like crystal stoichiometry and optical transparency [20](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02758v1).

### The 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} Dry Oxygen Annealing Protocol

The industry-standard thermal recovery process consists of annealing the wet-cleaned TFLN chip in a tube furnace under a flowing **dry oxygen (O2\text{O}_2) atmosphere at 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} for 2 to 5 hours2\text{ to }5\text{ hours}** , or alternatively at 300 ∘C300\text{ }^\circ\text{C} for 8 hours8\text{ hours} in ambient or oxygen when lower thermal budgets are mandated [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). This specific temperature window is governed by a balance between solid-phase defect healing and hetero-epitaxial thermal expansion limits [16](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/111806/1/Du_Asymmetric_Proton_Exchange.pdf). Above 400–500 ∘C400\text{--}500\text{ }^\circ\text{C}, molecular oxygen dissociates and diffuses into the oxygen-deficient near-surface sublattice, filling VOV_{\text{O}} point defects [48](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12529841B2/ja?authuser=1). Simultaneously, thermal activation dissociates NbNb4+–NbLi4+\text{Nb}_{\text{Nb}}^{4+}\text{--}\text{Nb}_{\text{Li}}^{4+} bipolarons (Ed=0.27 eVE_d = 0.27\text{ eV}) and oxidizes Nb4+\text{Nb}^{4+} small polarons back to stoichiometric Nb5+\text{Nb}^{5+}, eliminating residual absorption across the 500 to 2900 nm500\text{ to }2900\text{ nm} spectral window [19](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/231068278_Thermal_Dissociation_of_Bipolarons_in_Reduced_Undoped_LiNbO3). Furthermore, solid-phase epitaxial regrowth recrystallizes the strained lattice boundary created by Ar+\text{Ar}^+ ion bombardment and electron-beam lithography (EBL) irradiation [20](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02758v1).

At the same time, while bulk LN crystals are routinely annealed at 900–1100 ∘C900\text{--}1100\text{ }^\circ\text{C} to diffuse titanium or crystallize amorphous films [38](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2002075444A1/en?authuser=1), LNOI wafers cannot tolerate extreme temperatures due to the severe coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) mismatch between single-crystal LN (7.5–14.4×10−6 K−17.5\text{--}14.4 \times 10^{-6}\text{ K}^{-1}, depending on crystal cut) and the silicon handle substrate (2.6×10−6 K−12.6 \times 10^{-6}\text{ K}^{-1}) [16](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/111806/1/Du_Asymmetric_Proton_Exchange.pdf). Exceeding 550–600 ∘C550\text{--}600\text{ }^\circ\text{C} on silicon-backed LNOI risks shear-induced film cracking, interface delamination, and volatile Li2O\text{Li}_2\text{O} out-diffusion, making 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} the optimal upper operating point [16](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/111806/1/Du_Asymmetric_Proton_Exchange.pdf).

### Quantitative Recovery of Optical Loss and Photorefractive Stability

Kerr-calibrated photothermal pump-probe spectroscopy demonstrates the transformative effect of the 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} O2\text{O}_2 anneal on nonlinear TFLN microresonators [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). In as-fabricated, unannealed ion-sliced TFLN waveguides, total propagation loss is typically ∼3 dB/m\sim 3\text{ dB/m}, of which ∼1.5 dB/m\sim 1.5\text{ dB/m} stems directly from material photothermal absorption (κabs\kappa_{\text{abs}}) [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). Executing the post-etch 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} O2\text{O}_2 anneal drives the material-limited absorption loss down to **∼0.2 dB/m\sim 0.2\text{ dB/m}**  at telecom wavelengths—approaching the intrinsic bulk LN absorption limit and supporting a material-limited quality factor of Qmat≈1.6×108 to 1.8×108Q_{\text{mat}} \approx 1.6 \times 10^8\text{ to }1.8 \times 10^8 [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). In practical monolithic racetracks and microrings combining multipass EBL, ICP-RIE, wet cleaning, and 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} O2\text{O}_2 annealing, intrinsic quality factors reach Qi=2.93×107Q_i = 2.93 \times 10^7, corresponding to a total propagation loss of 1.3 dB/m1.3\text{ dB/m} (0.013 dB/cm0.013\text{ dB/cm}) [27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16161).

Crucially for nonlinear optics, O2\text{O}_2 annealing suppresses photorefractive damage [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). When unannealed TFLN microrings are optically pumped at ∼1550 nm\sim 1550\text{ nm}, trap-assisted space-charge buildup induces a strong photorefractive resonance blue-shift with a relaxation time constant of ∼100 s\sim 100\text{ s} [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). In resonators subjected to post-etch O2\text{O}_2 annealing, this photorefractive detuning drift is completely eliminated over one-minute probe windows, and average intrinsic QQ-factors increase by over threefold, rising from 1.5×1061.5 \times 10^6 in unannealed rings to 5.0×1065.0 \times 10^6 mean and 1.2×1071.2 \times 10^7 peak in annealed rings [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides).

### Cladding Preservation and Poling Coercive Field Adjustments

Two secondary process interactions must be managed when implementing thermal annealing into a full nonlinear photonic integrated circuit flow [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). First, depositing a top SiO2\text{SiO}_2 cladding via standard plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) *after*  the 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} O2\text{O}_2 anneal can re-damage the healed LN surface via plasma bombardment and hydrogen incorporation, erasing the absorption reduction [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). To preserve the healed lattice, engineers must either employ a **low-temperature oxide (LTO)**  cladding protocol [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides) or perform a secondary low-temperature anneal, such as 350 ∘C350\text{ }^\circ\text{C} in argon or oxygen, to outgas trapped hydrogen without re-inducing oxygen vacancies [54](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acsphotonics.6b00350).

Second, because charged point defects (VLiV_{\text{Li}}, VOV_{\text{O}}) act as nucleation sites that lower the barrier for domain inversion [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242), healing these defects via thermal annealing increases the external electric field required for periodic poling [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). In thin-film ferroelectric waveguides utilizing a pole-after-etch or oxide-interlayer architecture, unannealed films pole at ∼31 kV/mm\sim 31\text{ kV/mm} (460 V460\text{ V} across 10–15 μm10\text{--}15\text{ }\mu\text{m} gaps), whereas chips annealed at 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} for 5 hours5\text{ hours} in O2\text{O}_2 with an 80 nm80\text{ nm} PECVD SiO2\text{SiO}_2 interlayer require 43 kV/mm43\text{ kV/mm}, and chips annealed at 300 ∘C300\text{ }^\circ\text{C} for 8 hours8\text{ hours} in ambient with 50–100 nm50\text{--}100\text{ nm} SiO2\text{SiO}_2 require 53 to 70 kV/mm53\text{ to }70\text{ kV/mm} [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). Poling pulse voltages and ramp-down durations (typically a 90 s90\text{ s} linear ramp-down) must therefore be recalibrated whenever an annealing step precedes poling [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1).

## Surface Passivation via Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD)

Even after wet chemical stripping and 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} O2\text{O}_2 recrystallization, the terminated crystal plane at the etched waveguide sidewall contains uncompensated dangling bonds and surface states that act as carrier traps under high-intensity visible or UV light [18](https://pubs.acs.org/nalefd/article/doi/10.1021/acs.nanolett.6c03406/5436048/Hysteresis-and-Trap-Emission-in-dc-Biased). Depositing an ultrathin, conformal dielectric passivation shell via **Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD)** —specifically aluminum oxide (Al2O3\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3) or titanium dioxide (TiO2\text{TiO}_2)—mitigates these residual interface defects through both chemical bond termination and geometric smoothing [55](https://opg.optica.org/ome/abstract.cfm?URI=ome-16-9-3123).

During ALD growth, self-limiting surface reactions consume sub-stoichiometric native surface oxides and terminate dangling oxygen and metal bonds at the etched LN boundary [55](https://opg.optica.org/ome/abstract.cfm?URI=ome-16-9-3123). In wide-bandgap nonlinear platforms—where LiNbO3\text{LiNbO}_3 exhibits a bandgap of ∼4.0 to 4.71 eV\sim 4.0\text{ to }4.71\text{ eV} and a UV absorption edge near 350 nm350\text{ nm} [7](http://web.science.mq.edu.au/~benjamin/personal/thesis%20complete%20draft.pdf)—ALD Al2O3\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 passivation suppresses surface trap emission, reduces DC bias hysteresis, and stabilizes microresonator propagation loss against multi-day environmental degradation and photorefractive drift [18](https://pubs.acs.org/nalefd/article/doi/10.1021/acs.nanolett.6c03406/5436048/Hysteresis-and-Trap-Emission-in-dc-Biased). Simultaneously, a conformal post-etch ALD film (10–50 nm10\text{--}50\text{ nm} thick) blankets microscopic striations on the etched waveguide sidewalls, reducing the effective refractive index contrast of high-spatial-frequency surface roughness and lowering Rayleigh scattering loss [55](https://opg.optica.org/ome/abstract.cfm?URI=ome-16-9-3123). This dual mechanism is particularly vital for visible-wavelength integrated photonics (400–780 nm400\text{--}780\text{ nm}), where Al2O3\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 exhibits negligible intrinsic absorption with propagation losses below 0.7 dB/cm0.7\text{ dB/cm} at 405 nm405\text{ nm} [35](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11696560/). To prevent the ALD film itself from introducing optical absorption via localized oxygen vacancy states, the ALD oxidation half-cycle must be fully saturated, as insufficient oxidant pulsing creates oxygen-deficient amorphous oxide layers that exhibit ion-transition absorption peaks [61](https://www.oejournal.org/oea/article/doi/10.29026/oea.2026.250217).

## In-Situ Plasma Optimization and Atomic Layer Etching (ALE)

Post-etch remediation is most effective when the depth of the initial plasma damage layer is minimized during patterning, and when mechanical sputtering is followed by digital, low-energy dry finishing [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/).

### Redeposition-Free Physical Ar+\text{Ar}^+ ICP-RIE Regimes

In physical Ar+\text{Ar}^+ ICP-RIE, sidewall redeposition and surface damage are governed by the ratio of ion flux, which is controlled by ICP power and gas flow, to ion kinetic energy and mean free path, which are controlled by DC bias and chamber pressure [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/). Systematic mapping of the Ar+\text{Ar}^+ ICP-RIE parameter space reveals that operating in an **ultralow-pressure, controlled-bias regime**  completely suppresses sidewall redeposition during the etch itself [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/). Lowering chamber pressure from 11 mTorr11\text{ mTorr} down to **1 to 5 mTorr1\text{ to }5\text{ mTorr}**  with an Ar\text{Ar} flow of 10–20 sccm10\text{--}20\text{ sccm} minimizes ion-neutral collisions in the plasma sheath, preserving highly directional ion trajectories and allowing sputtered LN species to escape the trench rather than sticking to the sidewalls [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/). Surface roughness (SqS_q) improves monotonically as chamber pressure drops, measuring 0.15 nm0.15\text{ nm} at 7 mTorr7\text{ mTorr}, 0.12 nm0.12\text{ nm} at 5 mTorr5\text{ mTorr}, and **0.10 nm0.10\text{ nm} at 3 mTorr3\text{ mTorr}**  [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/).

At a 1 mTorr1\text{ mTorr} chamber pressure, selecting a DC bias of **600 V600\text{ V}**  with a moderate ICP power of **300 W300\text{ W}**  yields a linear, redeposition-free etch rate of 24 nm/min24\text{ nm/min}, scaling linearly at 0.09 nm/min per Watt0.09\text{ nm/min per Watt} of ICP power and 0.10 nm/min per Volt0.10\text{ nm/min per Volt} of DC bias [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/). Conversely, at 5 mTorr5\text{ mTorr} and 20 sccm20\text{ sccm} Ar\text{Ar}, a milder RF bias power of 100–150 W100\text{--}150\text{ W} (112 W112\text{ W} in ultra-high-QQ microring recipes) paired with 500 W500\text{ W} ICP power achieves a controlled 21.87 nm/min21.87\text{ nm/min} etch rate—scaling at 0.072 nm/min per W0.072\text{ nm/min per W} of bias and 0.059 nm/min per W0.059\text{ nm/min per W} of ICP power—that minimizes deep knock-on lattice amorphization [42](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=optica-4-12-1536). Throughout these processes, active water-cooling of the lower electrode chuck (15–20 ∘C15\text{--}20\text{ }^\circ\text{C}) is mandatory to prevent thermal runaway, mask erosion, and pyroelectric charge accumulation driven by LN's pyroelectric coefficient (∼−4×10−9 C⋅cm−2⋅∘C−1\sim -4 \times 10^{-9}\text{ C}\cdot\text{cm}^{-2}\cdot{}^\circ\text{C}^{-1} to −95 μC⋅m−2⋅K−1-95\text{ }\mu\text{C}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}) [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/). Additionally, annealing developed HSQ electron-beam masks prior to etching doubles the LN/HSQ\text{LN/HSQ} etch selectivity from 0.550.55 to ∼1.0\sim 1.0 without degrading resonator QQ-factors [66](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357491498_Lithium_niobate_microring_with_ultra-high_Q_factor_above_108).

### Digital Damage Removal via Bromine-Based Atomic Layer Etching (ALE)

When high-rate continuous RIE, such as Cl2/BCl3/Ar\text{Cl}_2/\text{BCl}_3/\text{Ar} at 900 W900\text{ W} ICP and 280 W280\text{ W} RIE bias (which etches Z-cut LN at 147 nm/min147\text{ nm/min}) [13](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/113857/bitstreams/373612/data.pdf), is used for bulk trench definition, it leaves behind a rough, halogen-contaminated, and ion-damaged surface [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1). **Atomic Layer Etching (ALE)**  provides a self-limiting, low-damage digital finishing technique that strips this damaged atomic skin layer-by-layer without introducing new subsurface defects [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1).

Recent breakthroughs in directional and isotropic ALE of LiNbO3\text{LiNbO}_3 utilize a cyclic **HBr/BCl3/Ar\text{HBr}/\text{BCl}_3/\text{Ar} plasma**  sequence composed of two self-limiting half-cycles separated by inert gas purges [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1). During the surface modification half-cycle, exposure to an HBr/BCl3/Ar\text{HBr}/\text{BCl}_3/\text{Ar} plasma chemically converts only the top ∼1 nm\sim 1\text{ nm} of the LiNbO3\text{LiNbO}_3 lattice into weakly bound, volatile brominated and chlorinated metal-halide complexes, followed by a chamber purge [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1). In the subsequent low-energy desorption half-cycle, a low-power Ar+\text{Ar}^+ plasma selectively sputters off the chemically weakened surface monolayer at ion energies well below the displacement damage threshold of the underlying pristine LiNbO3\text{LiNbO}_3 crystal, followed by a final purge to complete the cycle [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1).

Operating this directional Br-based ALE process at a substrate temperature of **200 ∘C200\text{ }^\circ\text{C}**  yields an etch rate (etch per cycle, EPC) of **1.12 to 1.25 nm/cycle1.12\text{ to }1.25\text{ nm/cycle}**  with a sidewall angle of 68∘68^\circ and complete aspect-ratio-independent etching (ARIE) down to 150 nm150\text{ nm} photonic crystal or grating gaps, whereas conventional physical Ar+\text{Ar}^+ RIE suffers from severe aspect-ratio loading in gaps narrower than 400 nm400\text{ nm} [5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/). At 200 ∘C200\text{ }^\circ\text{C}, thermal desorption reduces residual surface bromine contamination to just 0.35±0.06 at.%0.35 \pm 0.06\text{ at.}\% as measured by XPS [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1). Most importantly, applying **50 cycles of Br-based ALE**  as a post-etch surface treatment on rough plasma-etched LN reduces the AFM root-mean-square roughness (RqR_q) from **2.07±1.16 nm2.07 \pm 1.16\text{ nm} down to 0.34±0.07 nm0.34 \pm 0.07\text{ nm}** —an **84%84\% roughness reduction**  that restores the etched surface to the smoothness of unetched commercial TFLN wafers (Rq=0.30±0.04 nmR_q = 0.30 \pm 0.04\text{ nm}) while maintaining the top waveguide width without lateral critical-dimension loss [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1).

## Waveguide Modal Engineering and Etch-Bypassing Architectures

Because nonlinear parametric processes—including Kerr comb generation, optical parametric oscillation, and microwave-to-optical quantum transduction—are exponentially sensitive to scattering and surface-state absorption, fabrication remedies should be paired with optical mode engineering that minimizes field overlap with the plasma-etched interfaces [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242).

### Shallow-Etched Rib Geometries and Wide-Core Multimode Waveguides

Rather than etching completely through the TFLN device layer, retaining a partial crystalline slab—such as etching a 300–350 nm300\text{--}350\text{ nm} rib into a 600 nm600\text{ nm} X-cut LN film, or a 100 nm100\text{ nm} rib into a 200 nm200\text{ nm} visible-band film—confines the optical mode core within the undamaged bulk crystal and pulls the evanescent field away from the plasma-exposed sidewalls [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242). This slab simultaneously enhances RF and microwave electric field confinement for electro-optic modulation and facilitates uniform heat dissipation [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242).

Furthermore, expanding the waveguide top width from the single-mode regime (Wtop≈800 nmW_{\text{top}} \approx 800\text{ nm}) to a wide-core multimode geometry (Wtop=2.4 to 4.5 μmW_{\text{top}} = 2.4\text{ to }4.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}) drastically reduces the electromagnetic energy density at the etched sidewall boundaries [27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16161). In monolithic TFLN microrings, widening the waveguide from 800 nm800\text{ nm} to 2.4 μm2.4\text{ }\mu\text{m} improves the intrinsic QQ-factor from ∼1.5×106\sim 1.5 \times 10^6 to over 1.0×1071.0 \times 10^7 [42](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=optica-4-12-1536). To prevent higher-order spatial mode excitation (TE1\text{TE}_1) in wide-core waveguides (Wtop=3.0 μmW_{\text{top}} = 3.0\text{ }\mu\text{m}), replacing constant-radius circular bends with **modified adiabatic Euler bends**  (Rmin=80 μmR_{\text{min}} = 80\text{ }\mu\text{m}, Rmax=4 mmR_{\text{max}} = 4\text{ mm}, effective radius 156.86 μm156.86\text{ }\mu\text{m}) suppresses intermodal crosstalk to −10 dB-10\text{ dB} while cutting propagation loss by more than half—from 28.04 dB/m28.04\text{ dB/m} in single-mode rings down to 8.19 dB/m8.19\text{ dB/m} (Qi=4.05×106Q_i = 4.05 \times 10^6) in a single-etch process, and down to **0.39 dB/m0.39\text{ dB/m}**  in wide-core photonic-crystal Fabry-Pérot and racetrack resonators [27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16161).

### Etch-Free Patterning: Chemo-Mechanical Polishing (PLACE) and Rib-Loaded Platforms

For nonlinear applications where even remediated plasma sidewall damage is unacceptable, plasma etching of the LN layer can be bypassed entirely via chemo-mechanical or rib-loaded waveguide architectures [6](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apm/article/12/2/020601/3262490/Fabrication-and-photonic-applications-of-Si). In **Photolithography-Assisted Chemo-Mechanical Etching (PLACE)** , a chromium (Cr\text{Cr}) hard mask (200–600 nm200\text{--}600\text{ nm} thick) is patterned on LNOI via femtosecond laser ablation or deep-UV stepper lithography, and the exposed LN is sculpted via **chemo-mechanical polishing (CMP)**  rather than plasma bombardment, followed by mask stripping and a secondary smoothing CMP step [6](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apm/article/12/2/020601/3262490/Fabrication-and-photonic-applications-of-Si). Because CMP relies on gentle tribo-chemical slurry erosion rather than high-energy ions, it introduces zero subsurface knock-on amorphization or oxygen vacancies and yields ultra-smooth sidewalls (Rq<0.3 nmR_q < 0.3\text{ nm}, top surface roughness 0.1–0.2 nm0.1\text{--}0.2\text{ nm}) [20](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02758v1). Isolated PLACE microrings achieve intrinsic quality factors of Qi=1.08×108 to 1.23×108Q_i = 1.08 \times 10^8\text{ to }1.23 \times 10^8 and propagation losses as low as **0.34 dB/m0.34\text{ dB/m} (0.0034 dB/cm0.0034\text{ dB/cm})**  [53](https://www.researching.cn/ArticlePdf/m00005/2026/24/9/091301.pdf). The primary trade-off is the isotropic nature of CMP, which produces shallow sidewall angles that increase minimum bend radii and limit narrow-gap evanescent coupling compared to ICP-RIE [53](https://www.researching.cn/ArticlePdf/m00005/2026/24/9/091301.pdf).

Alternatively, **heterogeneous rib-loaded waveguides**  avoid plasma exposure of the ferroelectric layer by depositing a high-index-contrast dielectric or semiconductor strip—such as silicon nitride (Si3N4\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4), amorphous silicon, or tantalum pentoxide (Ta2O5\text{Ta}_2\text{O}_5)—onto an unetched LNOI wafer and plasma-etching *only*  the top loading layer [6](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apm/article/12/2/020601/3262490/Fabrication-and-photonic-applications-of-Si). This confines the optical mode within the pristine underlying LN slab without exposing the LiNbO3\text{LiNbO}_3 crystal to ion bombardment [6](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apm/article/12/2/020601/3262490/Fabrication-and-photonic-applications-of-Si). While rib loading eliminates LN sidewall scattering and etch-induced poling breakdown, it dilutes the optical confinement factor (ΓLN\Gamma_{\text{LN}}) inside the nonlinear crystal, reducing the effective χ(2)\chi^{(2)} overlap compared to monolithic etched TFLN ridges [4](https://opg.optica.org/aop/abstract.cfm?uri=aop-13-2-242).

For quasi-phase-matched devices, engineers should adopt a **pole-after-etch**  workflow [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). Performing periodic poling after plasma etching, wet chemical stripping, and thermal annealing prevents HF\text{HF} and SC-1 baths from differentially etching inverted ferroelectric domains into rough sidewall corrugations, while allowing the 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} O2\text{O}_2 anneal to heal etch-induced conductive defects that otherwise cause premature electrical breakdown during high-voltage poling pulses [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17980v1). Across all etched waveguide geometries, pairing wet SC-1 and Piranha stripping with 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} dry O2\text{O}_2 annealing serves as the non-negotiable baseline: wet alkaline or acid cleaning alone leaves behind subsurface oxygen vacancies and Nb4+\text{Nb}^{4+} polarons that drive GRIIRA and photorefractive resonance drift, whereas thermal annealing without prior wet stripping sinters the amorphous sidewall redeposition crust permanently onto the waveguide [19](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/231068278_Thermal_Dissociation_of_Bipolarons_in_Reduced_Undoped_LiNbO3). Executing heated SC-1 and Piranha cleaning immediately followed by 520 ∘C520\text{ }^\circ\text{C} dry O2\text{O}_2 annealing restores the material absorption limit to 0.2 dB/m0.2\text{ dB/m} [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides). Finally, for short-wavelength SHG, sum-frequency generation, and high-power Kerr or electro-optic microcombs, capping the oxygen-annealed waveguide with a conformal ALD Al2O3\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 passivation layer prior to depositing a low-temperature SiO2\text{SiO}_2 cladding ensures that subsequent plasma radicals and hydrogen do not re-introduce interface absorption states [22](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362969900_Reduced_material_loss_in_thin-film_lithium_niobate_waveguides).
